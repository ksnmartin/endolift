# Reformulations and relaxations for sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y) (global optimization, polynomial optimization, submodularity)

Notation used throughout: n = number of sites (y in {0,1}^n), S = number of scenarios, D = number of distinct candidate distributions (when the distribution is a lookup over opening patterns), J_s = number of Benders (dual extreme point) cuts for scenario s, d = degree of the multilinear polynomial p_s, r = rank (largest monomial size) of the monomial hypergraph. "Exact" below means exact at binary y; "relaxation" means a valid bound; "approximation" means a worst-case ratio.

A structural point that recurs in several sections (inference, stated here once): at a binary point y, p_s(y) is a *fractional* number in [0,1] even though y is binary. So McCormick/Glover applied to the pair (p_s, theta_s) with both treated as continuous is NOT exact at binary y. Exactness at binary points requires first expanding p_s(y) into monomials z_M = prod_{i in M} y_i (which ARE 0/1 at binary y) and then linearizing each product z_M * theta_s as a binary-times-continuous product. This is exactly what Medal and Affar do ("sequentially decomposing the nonlinear terms into multiple bilinear functions ... then apply the McCormick envelopes") — [Medal & Affar 2024, arXiv 2409.08403](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08403).

## Q1. Products of binary with bounded continuous variables: Glover (1975), McCormick (1976), exactness, weakness of the LP relaxation, and perspective-type reformulations

### Takeaway
For a single product w = eta*z with z binary and eta in [eta_lo, eta_hi], the four-inequality Glover/McCormick set is an exact reformulation at binary z and is the convex hull over the box; the only information coupling w to eta when z is fractional is the bound width (eta_hi - eta_lo), which is why LP relaxations are weak when the continuous factor is a Benders epigraph variable with only a loose a-priori upper bound. The perspective idea removes the need for that bound when the continuous factor is itself a convex piecewise-linear function of y: the perspective of max_j (a_j^T y + b_j) with respect to z is max_j (a_j^T v + b_j z) with v = z*y, i.e. the problem moves entirely into the lifted monomial space.

### Cited Findings
- Glover's 1975 Management Science paper "Improved Linear Integer Programming Formulations of Nonlinear Integer Problems" replaces cross-product terms of 0-1 variables with new variables plus auxiliary constraints; the Glover technique "linearizes a binary quadratic integer problem of n variables by introducing n new continuous variables and 4n auxiliary linear constraints" — [Revisiting some classical linearizations, arXiv 2404.09437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.09437); [Compact linearization, arXiv 1808.08806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.08806)
- Exact binary-times-continuous set (Basciftci, Ahmed & Shen eq. (13)): for z in {0,1} and eta in [eta_lo, eta_hi], M' = {(w, eta, z): eta - (1-z) eta_hi <= w <= eta - eta_lo (1-z), eta_lo z <= w <= eta_hi z}; "Because variable z is binary valued, we have an exact reformulation in (13)". One auxiliary variable and 4 constraints per product. — [Basciftci, Ahmed, Shen, arXiv 1912.05577 (EJOR 292(2):548-561, 2021)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.05577v2.pdf)
- Exact trilinear set with two binaries (Basciftci et al. eq. (14), Proposition 2): for 0 <= eta_lo <= eta <= eta_hi and z1, z2 in {0,1}, the 8-inequality set {w <= eta_hi z1, w <= eta_hi z2, w <= eta - eta_lo(1-z1), w <= eta - eta_lo(1-z2), w >= eta_lo(-1+z1+z2), w >= eta + eta_hi(-2+z1+z2), ...} equals conv{(w, eta, z1, z2): w = eta z1 z2}. — [Basciftci, Ahmed, Shen, arXiv 1912.05577](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.05577v2.pdf)
- Glover-Woolsey form for x_i x_j (x_i + x_j - y_ij <= 1, y_ij <= x_i, y_ij <= x_j, y_ij >= 0) "appears as a special case of the convex envelopes for general nonlinear programming problems as proposed by McCormick (1976)"; its inequalities "do not couple related linearization variables, and depending on the concrete problem to be solved, this may result in rather weak linear programming relaxations" — [Compact linearization, arXiv 1808.08806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.08806)
- "While these envelopes are exact for binary solutions, the linear programming relaxation can be quite weak, as we found in our computational experimentation" (products of binary first-stage variables with scenario recourse costs) — [Medal & Affar 2024, arXiv 2409.08403](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08403)
- For a single bilinear term over a box, McCormick is convex-hull defining (Al-Khayyal & Falk 1983, Math. OR 8(2):273-286), but for a general bilinear function (a sum of terms) it often is not; the ratio c between the McCormick gap and the true envelope gap "cannot be bounded by a constant independent of n", with c <= 600 sqrt(n) — [Boland, Dey, Kalinowski, Molinaro, Rigterink, Math Prog 2017, DOI 10.1007/s10107-016-1031-5](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10107-016-1031-5); [Luedtke, Namazifar, Linderoth TR1678](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/techreports/2010/TR1678.pdf)
- Luedtke-Namazifar-Linderoth: for a multilinear function with a single product term, McCormick and the envelopes coincide when variable bounds are symmetric around zero (a different condition from the single-bilinear-term-over-rectangle result) — [TR1678](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/techreports/2010/TR1678.pdf)
- Perspective reformulation (Günlük & Linderoth, Math Prog 124:183-205, 2010): indicator variables that "either fix continuous variables at a value when switched off or confine them to a convex set when switched on"; the paper gives the explicit convex hull for a single separable quadratic constraint with variable bounds and extends it to the union of a point and a bounded convex set; the hull is conic-quadratic representable; the perspective reformulation yields the closed convex hull of the on-off set in the original variable space — [Günlük & Linderoth preprint](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2009/09/2393.pdf); [Perspective reformulation and applications (2012 chapter)](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/perspective-reformulation-and-applications/1827222)
- Solver-presolve observation (AIMMS): a constraint a x + b x y + c y <= d with x binary can be transformed into a linear constraint under some conditions, and y^2 <= z can be replaced by its perspective counterpart for a tighter relaxation — [AIMMS documentation](https://documentation.aimms.com/user-guide/aimms-ide/Aimms/Options/Solvers General/AIMMS Presolver/NonlinPres_-_Reformulate_Constraints.html)
- Gu, Dey, Richard: "lifted bilinear cover inequalities" are second-order-cone-representable convex inequalities valid for a separable bilinear constraint set with variable bounds; adding them to McCormick's relaxation at the root "significantly improves" gap closed in a global solver — [Gu, Dey, Richard, arXiv 2208.00345 (IJOC)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00345)

### Inferences
- Why the LP relaxation is weak for sum_k y_k * theta: with y_k fractional, the Glover set only forces w in [theta - U(1 - y_k), theta - L(1 - y_k)] and [L y_k, U y_k]; the relaxation error at y_k = 1/2 is of order (U - L)/2 per product. When theta is an epigraph variable bounded below only by Benders cuts, U must be a global a-priori bound on recourse cost, so the error is as large as the recourse-cost range. Any tightening must come from either (i) a better U (dual bounds; Medal & Affar's "tight upper bound on the dual multipliers" assumption serves this role), or (ii) avoiding U altogether via lifting.
- Perspective reformulation for piecewise-linear convex recourse (my derivation from the Günlük-Linderoth hull statement): let f(y) = max_j (a_j^T y + b_j) (the Benders cut envelope of Q_s). The on-off set {(z, y, w): z in {0,1}; z = 0 => (y, w) = 0; z = 1 => w >= f(y), y in B} has convex hull given by the perspective z f(y/z), which for piecewise-linear f is linear: w >= a_j^T v + b_j z for all j, with v = z y in z*B. So y_k * Q_s(y) is modelled exactly, and with no bound on Q_s, by introducing v^{k} = y_k y (n products of binaries, i.e. Boolean-quadric-polytope variables) and writing w_{ks} >= a_j^T v^{k} + b_j y_k for each Benders cut j. Cost: n^2 product variables v^{k}_i (shared across scenarios and cuts) plus one epigraph variable per (k, s) and one constraint per (k, s, j). Tightness is then governed by the relaxation used for v^{k}_i = y_k y_i (standard linearization = Boolean quadric polytope relaxation), not by any bound on Q_s. This is a relaxation in general and exact at binary y.
- Extended to a general multilinear p_s(y) >= 0: because p_s >= 0, p_s(y) Q_s(y) = max_j p_s(y)(a_j^T y + b_j), and p_s(y)(a_j^T y + b_j) is a multilinear polynomial of degree deg(p_s) + 1 in binary y. So "multiplying each Benders cut by p_s(y)" (an RLT step, see Q2) is an exact reformulation at binary y in the lifted monomial space, with no bound on Q_s needed.

## Q2. RLT / Sherali-Adams / Lovász-Schrijver / Lasserre and the multilinear polytope (Del Pia & Khajavirad)

### Takeaway
Writing p_s(y) * (Benders cut) linearly requires RLT level deg(p_s) + 1 just to generate the monomials; the n-th level of any of the three hierarchies is exact but has O(n^n)-type size. The multilinear-polytope results give much sharper statements: the standard (McCormick-style) linearization of the monomial set is exact iff the monomial hypergraph is Berge-acyclic; gamma-acyclic hypergraphs have an exact, strongly-polynomially-separable flower relaxation; beta-acyclic hypergraphs have an exact extended formulation of size (r-1)|V| + |E| variables and (3r-4)|V| + 4|E| inequalities; general (alpha-acyclic) hypergraphs need 2^r min{|V|, |F|} variables, which for an independent-site product distribution over all n sites is 2^n — i.e. scenario enumeration.

### Cited Findings
- RLT: "for the family of mixed 0-1 linear (and polynomial) programs in n 0-1 variables, the RLT generates an n-level hierarchy, with the n-th level providing an explicit algebraic characterization of the convex hull of feasible solutions" — [Sherali & Adams RLT survey](https://www.academia.edu/48587889/Reformulation_Linearization_Methods_for_Global_Optimization)
- Sherali (1997, Acta Math. Vietnamica 22(1)) characterizes the convex envelope of multilinear functions over the unit hypercube via an RLT-derived convex-hull representation and extends it to special discrete sets including GUB-constrained binaries; explicit formulas when all coefficients are +1 or all -1 — [Sherali 1997](https://beta.math.ac.vn/uploads/files/9701245.pdf)
- Laurent (Math. OR 28:470-496, 2003) puts Sherali-Adams, Lovász-Schrijver and Lasserre in a common framework and shows Lasserre gives the tightest relaxations; all three terminate with an exact description after at most n steps; Lasserre refines Sherali-Adams which refines Lovász-Schrijver (secondary) — [Laurent 2003 listing](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/401356); [Exact subgraph hierarchy paper, arXiv 2003.13605](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.13605); Laurent's SA-rank-n example and the Kurpisz-Leppänen-Mastrolilli Lasserre-rank bounds Omega(sqrt n) to n - Omega(n^{1/3}) — [arXiv 1605.03019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.03019)
- Multilinear set / polytope definitions (Del Pia & Khajavirad survey, Jan 2025): S(G) = {z in {0,1}^{V u E}: z_e = prod_{v in e} z_v}; MP(G) = conv S(G); the standard linearization MP^LP(G) is {z_v <= 1, z_e >= 0, z_e >= sum_{v in e} z_v - |e| + 1, z_e <= z_v for v in e} and has at most |V| + (r+2)|E| inequalities, r = rank — [Del Pia & Khajavirad, arXiv 2501.04805](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04805v1)
- Theorem (Berge-acyclic): MP(G) = MP^LP(G) if and only if G is Berge-acyclic; graph case is Padberg's theorem BQP(G) = MP^LP(G) iff G acyclic — [arXiv 2501.04805](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04805v1)
- Theorem (gamma-acyclic): MP(G) equals the flower relaxation iff G is gamma-acyclic; flower inequalities have at most r/2 nonzeros; MP(G) may have exponentially many facets but flower inequalities are separable in strongly polynomial time (SIAM J. Optim. 2018, DOI 10.1137/16M1095998); Khajavirad's slides state gamma-acyclic hypergraphs admit a polynomial-size extended formulation — [arXiv 2501.04805](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04805v1); [Khajavirad ICERM slides](https://app.icerm.brown.edu/assets/429/4663/4663_3627_Khajavirad_013020231430_Slides.pdf)
- Running intersection relaxation (Math. OR 46(3):1008-1037, 2021): running-intersection inequalities have at most 2(r-1) nonzeros; the relaxation coincides with MP(G) for kite-free beta-acyclic hypergraphs (a class between gamma- and beta-acyclic) — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormoor/v46y2021i3p1008-1037.html); [arXiv 2501.04805](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04805v1)
- Theorem (beta-acyclic): polynomial-size extended formulation with at most (r-1)|V| + |E| variables and (3r-4)|V| + 4|E| inequalities, all coefficients in {0, +-1}; facets in the original space can have Theta(|E|) nonzeros even for r = 8; explicit original-space description open — [arXiv 2501.04805](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04805v1)
- Theorem (alpha-acyclic / general): MP(G) has an extended formulation with at most 2^r min{|V|, |F|} variables and inequalities (F = maximal edges), coefficients in {0, +-1}; equivalently 2^w |V| where w is the treewidth of the intersection graph; polynomial only for bounded rank — [arXiv 2501.04805](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04805v1)
- Complexity: binary polynomial optimization over alpha-acyclic hypergraphs is strongly NP-hard and NP-hard to approximate within kappa > 16/17; under NP not subset of BPP, BPO over treewidth-k families needs super-polynomial time in k; separating flower inequalities is NP-hard in general but O(|E|^2) for fixed rank — [arXiv 2501.04805](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04805v1)
- Complete edge relaxation (2025 tech report): it is an extension of the multilinear polytope iff the hypergraph is alpha-acyclic; the standard linearization describes MP exactly when the hypergraph is Berge-acyclic — [Lehigh ISE 25T_011](https://engineering.lehigh.edu/sites/engineering.lehigh.edu/files/_DEPARTMENTS/ise/pdf/tech-papers/25/25T_011.pdf)
- Khajavirad (2022/23): "any recursive McCormick relaxation is implied by the extended flower relaxation", which for fixed degree is solvable in strongly polynomial time; choosing the recursive sequence is "a difficult combinatorial optimization problem" — [Khajavirad, arXiv 2209.13034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13034)
- For the multilinear set with r > 2, polyhedral studies are "rather scarce"; an explicit (x,y)-space description of the convex hull of multilinear graphs is not known in general though exponential-size extended formulations exist — [arXiv 2012.06394](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2012.06394); [arXiv 2211.13545](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.13545)

### Inferences
- Level needed: a product p_s(y) * (a_j^T y + b_j), with p_s of degree d, has monomials of degree d+1. The level-t RLT introduces linearization variables for all products of up to t bound factors, so level d+1 is required merely to represent the objective after Benders cuts are multiplied through; the level-(d+1) relaxation has O(n^{d+1}) variables and O(n^{d+1} * m) constraints (m = number of original constraints and cuts). Exactness only at level n (all three hierarchies). Lasserre at the same level is tighter than SA but is an SDP of matrix size O(n^{ceil((d+1)/2)}).
- Hypergraph of the lifted objective: the edge set is H = union over s of {supports of monomials of p_s} union {M union {i}: M a monomial of p_s, i in supp(a_j) for some cut j}. Three regimes follow from the cited theorems: (i) p_s affine in y (Hellemo's "affine distortion" / "convex combination" models): monomials are pairs {i, k}, H is a graph; the standard linearization (McCormick on y_i y_k) is exact iff that graph is acyclic (Padberg), and in general one is in the Boolean quadric polytope and should add its triangle inequalities; (ii) p_s a product of affine univariate factors over a site set A_s (independent site effects): every subset of A_s is a monomial, the maximal edge is A_s itself, and the hypergraph is alpha-acyclic with a single maximal edge, so the exact extended formulation has 2^{|A_s|} variables — which is scenario enumeration over A_s; (iii) if the monomial family is Berge-/gamma-/beta-acyclic (e.g. p_s depends on disjoint site groups, or nested chains of sites), the exact hulls are polynomial-size, with sizes as given above.
- The standard linearization of the monomial set is exactly the "recursive McCormick" that solvers and Medal & Affar use; by Khajavirad's result it is dominated by the extended flower relaxation, which is therefore a strictly-no-worse drop-in for bounded-degree p_s at strongly-polynomial separation cost.

## Q3. Multilinear extension of set functions (Calinescu, Chekuri, Pál, Vondrák 2011): is sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y) a multilinear extension, and does continuous greedy apply?

### Takeaway
sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y) is the multilinear extension of a set function only when it is itself a multilinear polynomial in y, which happens when the recourse value Q_s is constant in y (the "activation" model where opening a site switches on an independent random variable and closed sites contribute a null state). In that model Asadpour-Nazerzadeh prove the non-adaptive objective F(S) = E[f(Theta_S)] is monotone submodular whenever f is monotone submodular in the realized vector, so continuous greedy gives (1 - 1/e - eps) under a matroid constraint. When Q_s depends on y through a convex piecewise-linear LP value function, the sum is not multilinear and the CCPV machinery does not apply directly.

### Cited Findings
- Continuous greedy (Calinescu, Chekuri, Pál, Vondrák 2011): first algorithm with a (1-1/e)-optimal solution in expectation for monotone submodular maximization under a general matroid; the multilinear extension of a monotone submodular set function is smooth, monotone and submodular; the method combines multilinear extension, continuous greedy and pipage rounding — [Conditional Gradient Methods survey, arXiv 2211.14103](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14103); [arXiv 1709.02910](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.02910)
- Cost: exact evaluation of the multilinear extension requires 2^n function values; sampling gives an eps-accurate estimate with O((log n)/eps^2) evaluations; the original analysis needs Omega(n^8) evaluations; Badanidiyuru & Vondrák (2014) reduced the cost of a (1-1/e-eps)-solution to O(n^2/eps^4) — [arXiv 2211.14103](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14103); [arXiv 1709.02910](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.02910)
- Beyond 1-1/e is NP-hard (Nemhauser, Wolsey, Fisher 1978); Feldman et al. generalized continuous greedy to a 1/e-type guarantee for any down-closed solvable polytope — [arXiv 1608.03611](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.03611)
- Asadpour & Nazerzadeh (Management Science 2016, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2015.2254; arXiv 0908.2788): ground set of n independent random variables X_i; f: R_+^n -> R_+ monotone submodular (componentwise max/min lattice); selection reveals the chosen element's realized value, "Unselected elements keep their original distribution"; non-adaptive objective F(S) = E[f(Theta_S)]; "Lemma 3 shows F as a set function is monotone and submodular", and F(y) for fractional y is exactly the multilinear extension (each X_i included independently with probability y_i); Lemma 4: any y in the matroid base polytope can be rounded to an independent set S with F(S) >= F(y) in polynomial time — [Asadpour & Nazerzadeh, ar5iv 0908.2788](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0908.2788)
- Theorems: adaptivity gap e/(e-1), tight (stochastic max k-coverage); a polynomial-time non-adaptive policy achieves (1 - 1/e - eps) of the optimal adaptive value via stochastic continuous greedy (Thm 2); non-adaptive myopic achieves (1/2)(1-1/e); adaptive myopic achieves 1/(kappa+1) for an intersection of kappa matroids and 1-1/e for a uniform matroid; without independence the adaptivity gap can be >= n/2; without monotonicity it is unbounded — [ar5iv 0908.2788](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0908.2788)

### Inferences
- Multilinear extension identification: a multilinear polynomial in y is the (unique) multilinear extension of its restriction to {0,1}^n. If Q_s is constant in y (the scenario already encodes which sites are open, as in Laumanns et al.'s "cost in each scenario is a constant"), then G(y) = sum_s p_s(y) Q_s is a multilinear polynomial and hence the multilinear extension of g(A) = E_{D(A)}[Q]. Continuous greedy applies to G directly, with an exact (no sampling) gradient: dG/dy_i = sum_s (dp_s/dy_i) Q_s, computable with S recourse evaluations (each an LP, or a closed form). The (1-1/e) guarantee requires g monotone submodular; by Asadpour-Nazerzadeh Lemma 3 this holds under the activation model when the realized-vector objective is monotone submodular (e.g. coverage/profit-type recourse), and fails or is unknown otherwise.
- When Q_s(y) is a convex piecewise-linear LP value function of y, G(y) = sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y) is piecewise-polynomial, not multilinear, so it is not the multilinear extension of any set function (the multilinear extension of its binary restriction is a different continuous function). One can recover the CCPV setting only by absorbing y into the scenario (activation model) or by using the lifted monomial representation of Q2, at the cost of losing the "product distribution" interpretation of fractional y.
- Submodular vs supermodular: with minimization of expected cost, the relevant property for guarantees is supermodularity of cost (equivalently submodularity of savings); the approximation literature above is for maximization of monotone submodular utilities, so a cost-minimization model with decision-dependent probabilities gets no ratio from these results unless re-expressed as profit maximization.

### Gaps
- The CCPV SIAM J. Comput. 2011 paper and Vondrák's thesis were not fetched; the statements come from secondary surveys.
- I found no source proving submodularity (or supermodularity) of g(A) = E_{D(A)}[Q(A, xi)] when D(A) is a product distribution whose per-site parameters shift with openings of *other* sites (cross-site effects); only the activation model is covered by Asadpour-Nazerzadeh.

## Q4. Adaptive submodularity (Golovin & Krause 2011) and stochastic submodular optimization: do the guarantees survive when decisions change the distribution rather than reveal information?

### Takeaway
All of these frameworks assume a fixed prior over item states; decisions only reveal (or activate) states. The guarantees transfer to decision-dependent probabilities only in the special "activation" case, where the law of each site's outcome is fixed and opening merely makes that outcome count. No source was found that extends adaptive-submodular or stochastic-probing guarantees to settings where opening one site changes the outcome distribution at another site.

### Cited Findings
- Golovin & Krause (JAIR 42:427-486, 2011) introduce adaptive submodularity as a generalization of submodular set functions to adaptive policies; the adaptive greedy policy achieves a 1 - 1/e ratio for adaptive monotone, adaptive submodular objectives; the property reduces to classical submodularity when outcome distributions are deterministic — [Golovin & Krause, arXiv 1003.3967](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.3967); [JAIR version](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/10731/25633)
- Esfandiari, Karbasi, Mirrokni (COLT 2021) prove greedy achieves a logarithmic guarantee for adaptive-submodular minimum-cost coverage (conjectured by Golovin & Krause) and give a semi-adaptive policy with polylogarithmic adaptive rounds — [PMLR v134](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v134/esfandiari21a.html)
- Asadpour & Nazerzadeh: "Decisions therefore do not change the distribution of any item. They only reveal realizations." Independence of items is needed: without it the adaptivity gap is at least n/2 — [ar5iv 0908.2788](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0908.2788)
- Stochastic probing on matroids (Adamczyk, Sviridenko, Ward): each element is active with independent probability p_e, probing reveals the state, an active probed element must be taken; with k_in inner and k_out outer matroid constraints the algorithm is a (1-1/e)/(k_in + k_out + 1)-approximation for monotone submodular objectives and 1/(k_in + k_out) for linear — [Adamczyk, Sviridenko, Ward, arXiv 1310.4415](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4415)

### Inferences
- The activation model is exactly where "decision-dependent probability" and "information revelation" coincide: P(xi | y) = prod_i [y_i * Law_i + (1 - y_i) * delta_null] is a product measure whose per-site factors do not depend on other sites' decisions; equivalently, draw all site outcomes from a fixed prior and let y select which coordinates matter. For the facility-location objective this means: demand/outcome at a site is random with a law that does not depend on which *other* sites are open. Under that assumption, Golovin-Krause (adaptive, sequential opening with observation) and Asadpour-Nazerzadeh / stochastic probing (non-adaptive, fixed matroid constraint) apply.
- Cross-site endogeneity (demand at j shifts because k opened; cannibalization; induced demand) violates the fixed-prior assumption of every framework above. Nothing found supports a ratio in that case.

### Gaps
- No paper found on adaptive submodularity or stochastic probing with decision-altered (rather than decision-revealed) distributions.
- Gotovos et al. (non-monotone adaptive submodular) was not retrieved beyond a 2021 arXiv mention (arXiv 2104.04853) and is not detailed here.

## Q5. Decision-dependent / endogenous uncertainty in approximation algorithms (Ravi-Sinha, Swamy-Shmoys, etc.)

### Takeaway
Constant-factor approximations for stochastic facility location exist only for exogenous distributions (black-box or polynomial-scenario). I found no constant-factor approximation that tolerates decision-dependent scenario probabilities; the only convergence-type result found (Pantuso) is an exact/convergent scheme, not a ratio.

### Cited Findings
- Swamy & Shmoys: SAA/black-box sampling gives a (1+eps) factor for a broad class of 2-stage and k-stage stochastic linear programs with exogenous distributions — [Dagstuhl proceedings](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/DagSemProc.05031.5); [Tutte seminar abstract](https://uwaterloo.ca/combinatorics-and-optimization/events/tutte-seminar-david-shmoys-apr-4-2008)
- Byrka & Srinivasan: 2.2975-approximation for 2-stage facility location in the polynomial-scenario model — [arXiv 1712.06996](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.06996v1.pdf)
- Pantuso (seminar): for programs where the distribution depends on the decision vector through an arbitrary distribution map, piecewise-constant approximations on partitions of the feasible set converge to the optimum as the partition is refined; stated as convergence, not a fixed ratio — [TUM AdONE seminar abstract](https://www.gs.tum.de/en/adone/news/article/adone-seminar-prof-giovanni-pantuso-university-of-copenhagen/)
- Hardness pointer: maximizing a multilinear polynomial over binaries (BPO) over alpha-acyclic hypergraphs is strongly NP-hard and inapproximable beyond 16/17 — [Del Pia & Khajavirad, arXiv 2501.04805](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04805v1)

### Inferences
- The activation model (Q3/Q4) is the only identified route to a constant factor: there the objective is a multilinear polynomial of degree n with the structure of a stochastic monotone submodular function, giving (1 - 1/e - eps) under a matroid (e.g. a cardinality/budget constraint on openings). Outside it, the general problem contains arbitrary degree-n multilinear maximization and cannot have a PTAS-like guarantee without structure.

### Gaps
- Ravi & Sinha and the Swamy-Shmoys JACM 2012 paper were not fetched; no result on decision-dependent probabilities exists in the sources retrieved.
- No literature found on "endogenous" approximation algorithms with provable ratios; this appears to be an open area as of the searched sources.

## Q6. Difference-of-convex (DC) programming and the convex-concave procedure

### Takeaway
DC decompositions give a local method (DCA/CCP: linearize the concave part, solve a sequence of convex MIPs, converge to stationary points) but no relaxation tighter than McCormick for a single bilinear term, because McCormick is already the convex envelope of that term over a box. Potential tightness gains from DC must come from treating the whole sum sum_s p_s Q_s jointly, where McCormick's envelope gap can be a factor up to ~sqrt(n) off the true envelope.

### Cited Findings
- Okuno & Ikebe (arXiv 1702.00553): DCA for mixed-integer DC programs via a continuous relaxation "with no integrality gap" plus smoothing; each DCA subproblem is a convex mixed-integer program; generated sequences converge to stationary points under mild assumptions — [arXiv 1702.00553](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.00553)
- Gabriel et al. (2025): bilinear complementarity constraints reformulated as DC functions and solved by sequential linear approximation of the concave term using standard DCA — [Optimization Online, Oct 2025](https://optimization-online.org/2025/10/a-heuristic-for-complementarity-problems-using-difference-of-convex-functions/)
- Pham Dinh & Le Thi DCA for mixed 0-1 DC programs (portfolio with nonconvex transaction costs) combined with branch and bound — [theses.fr 2013](https://www.theses.fr/2013ISAM0005/document)
- alphaBB (Adjiman et al. 1998) replaces bilinear/trilinear terms "with customized tight convex lower bounding functions" rather than the generic quadratic (DC-type) alpha perturbation — [Adjiman et al. 1998 (course copy)](https://www.ou.edu/class/che-design/che5480-11/A global optimization method, aBBTheory(Adjiman et al)-98.pdf); Skjäl, Westerlund, Misener, Floudas generalized alphaBB to off-diagonal Hessian (bilinear) entries — [JOTA 2012 listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/joptap/v154y2012i2d10.1007_s10957-012-0033-6.html)
- McCormick is convex-hull defining for a single bilinear term but the gap ratio for a bilinear *function* grows up to 600 sqrt(n) — [Boland et al. 2017](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10107-016-1031-5)

### Inferences
- Local method: with Q_s convex PL and p_s multilinear, the DC split p_s Q_s = ((p_s + Q_s)^2 - (p_s - Q_s)^2)/4 is valid wherever both are finite; CCP linearizes -(p_s - Q_s)^2 at the current iterate, so each step minimizes a convex function of (p, Q) over the master, which is a convex MINLP in y unless p_s is also linearized. No global bound is produced.
- Bound quality: since McCormick is the convex envelope of a single bilinear term over its box, no convex relaxation in the same (p_s, theta_s) space can be tighter for an isolated term; a DC-based convex underestimator (secant of the concave part) is therefore at best equal and generally weaker. The alphaBB authors' own choice of McCormick for bilinear terms is consistent with this.
- Binary-specific DC trick (uncited, standard): for binary y one may add alpha(y_i^2 - y_i) = 0 to shift convexity, which is the basis of the Hammer-Rubin / "diagonal shift" convexification of quadratic binary programs; this is relevant if p_s is quadratic (affine distortion model) but is only as strong as the corresponding SDP (see Q7).

### Gaps
- Horst & Tuy and Tuy's DC chapter were not fetched. No source was found that compares a DC-based convex relaxation with McCormick on binary-times-piecewise-linear products.
- No source was found that benchmarks CCP/DCA on stochastic programs with endogenous probabilities.

## Q7. Rank-one / low-rank / SDP relaxations of the bilinear form sum_s p_s Q_s

### Takeaway
After lifting p_s and Q_s to variables, the objective is a separable bilinear form sum_s p_s q_s, and for separable bilinear programs the Shor SDP relaxation provably adds nothing over McCormick (Gu, Dey, Richard). Any SDP gain must come from coupling through y (p = p(y), q bounded by cuts in y), for which I found no dedicated result.

### Cited Findings
- Gu, Dey, Richard (arXiv 2208.00345; IJOC): "first prove that the semi-definite programming relaxation provides no benefit over the McCormick relaxation" for separable bilinear optimization problems; they then propose SOC-representable lifted bilinear cover inequalities that strengthen McCormick — [arXiv 2208.00345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00345); [IJOC listing](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/ijoc.2022.0230)
- The standard Shor relaxation replaces X = x x^T with X - x x^T PSD; Anstreicher's "Shor plus RLT" adds McCormick envelopes; for general QCQPs Shor plus partial first-order RLT is equivalent to the doubly nonnegative relaxation and dominates six other SDP relaxations — [arXiv 2005.02667](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.02667); [Math Prog listing via unpaywall](https://unpaywall.org/10.1007%2Fs10107-011-0462-2)
- SDP relaxations are inexact for some hydro-thermal bilinear problems — [arXiv 1711.03823](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.03823)

### Inferences
- The objective sum_s p_s Q_s is a bilinear form <p, q> between two S-vectors; the matrix p q^T has rank one by construction, so a rank-one or nuclear-norm constraint carries no information beyond the lifting itself. The Gu-Dey-Richard result then says the Shor relaxation of the lifted products w_s = p_s q_s is no tighter than McCormick. Coupling through y is the only lever: with p_s(y) multilinear of degree d and q_s >= a_j^T y + b_j, a joint SDP on moments of (y, q) of order ceil((d+1)/2) is the Lasserre relaxation of Q2, with matrix size O(n^{ceil((d+1)/2)} + S); I found no paper evaluating this for endogenous-probability stochastic programs.
- Burer-Monteiro low-rank factorizations address scalability of such SDPs, not tightness.

### Gaps
- No bilinear-matrix-inequality or Burer-Monteiro source relevant to this product form was found; the inferences above are the only content.

## Q8. MIP-community exact reformulations for products of binaries and recourse costs (endogenous probabilities)

### Takeaway
Five exact approaches exist: (1) Medal-Affar's recursive-McCormick deterministic equivalent with a successive-refinement (Jensen-bound) scheme inside branch-and-cut; (2) Laumanns-Prestwich-Kawas "distribution shaping": a chain of n probability-measure vectors linked by Bayes-rule linear inequalities (n * 2^n continuous variables, exact MILP when scenario costs are constants, mixed-integer bilinear when recourse depends on y); (3) Haus-Michini-Laumanns BDD factorization (|V(B)| + n variables and 4|V(B)| + 1 constraints per BDD, polynomial when BDD width is bounded); (4) Basciftci-Ahmed-Shen exact McCormick/trilinear-hull MILP for piecewise-linear moment functions with dual-boundedness valid inequalities; (5) Pantuso / Pantuso-Hewitt decision-dependent L-shaped method with distribution-specific optimality cuts over an enumerated family of up to 2^|Z| distributions.

### Cited Findings
- Medal & Affar (arXiv 2409.08403, 2024): model min c^T x + sum_xi P[xi | x] Theta(xi); independence assumption P[xi | x] = prod_i f_i(xi_i; x_i); with integer allocations x_i in {0..L} encoded by binaries x_{il}, P[xi | x] = prod_i sum_l f_i(xi_i; l) x_{il} (eq. 4); deterministic equivalent (5) with scenario copies y^s, objective sum_s (prod_i sum_l f_i(xi_i^s; l) x_{il}) q^T y^s; "the McCormick envelopes used to linearize products of variables are exact for binary solutions"; benchmark: sequential decomposition into bilinear terms, "For every bilinear term, we introduce an auxiliary variable, and ... four constraints"; the model "is only able to solve small problems" — [arXiv 2409.08403](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08403)
- Medal & Affar structural results: Theta convex in xi gives the Jensen lower bound Theta(mean xi(x)) <= E[Theta(xi) | x] (6); a penalized reformulation Theta~(xi) = min q^T y + xi^T M y (M = diagonal upper bounds on duals beta of capacity constraints) agrees with Theta on the support and is concave in xi, giving an upper bound E[Theta~ | x] <= Theta~(mean) ; partition trees refine both bounds (Theorems 1-2, Corollary 3); the successive refinement algorithm (SRA) adds rows dynamically in branch-and-cut; "works best for problems in which a tight upper bound can be found for the dual multipliers beta_i" (max-flow, capacitated coverage: beta <= 1); SRA "significantly outperforms" the McCormick benchmark — [arXiv 2409.08403](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08403)
- Laumanns, Prestwich, Kawas (2014, SPEPS): with independent Bernoulli site events and binary decisions x that boost p_i to q_i, measures for neighbouring decisions satisfy P_{x+e_i}{xi} = P_x{xi} * q_i/p_i if xi_i = 1 and * (1-q_i)/(1-p_i) if xi_i = 0 (Bayes' rule); auxiliary measure vectors pi_k = P_{x^k} (x^k = x with components > k zeroed) satisfy linear inequalities pi_k^xi <= (q_k/p_k) pi_{k-1}^xi + 1 - x_k (xi_k = 1), pi_k^xi <= ((1-q_k)/(1-p_k)) pi_{k-1}^xi + 1 - x_k (xi_k = 0), pi_k^xi <= pi_{k-1}^xi + x_k, sum_xi pi_k^xi = 1; the resulting problem min sum_xi pi_n^xi f(xi) "is a mixed-integer linear program ... given that the cost in each scenario is a constant"; "if f(xi) for instance was a recourse function of a stochastic linear program, then we would obtain a mixed-integer bi-linear program instead"; "The above MIP has only n binary variables, but 2^n * n auxiliary continuous variables"; the approach is "exact" versus prior "linearizations [29] or convex approximations [11]"; Istanbul highway instance solved to optimality within seconds — [Laumanns, Prestwich, Kawas (edoc HU Berlin)](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/bitstreams/5cd4f601-25ad-46cc-a822-2d281505e874/download)

- The three "probability-flow" formulations (Laumanns chain, BDD, Pantuso's delta_d) all share the property that the probability of a scenario becomes a *continuous auxiliary variable* that is linear-constrained in y. The product with a y-dependent recourse cost is then a product of two continuous variables, which is why Laumanns et al. obtain a mixed-integer bilinear program and Pantuso needs a big-M bound U; only when the recourse cost is a constant per scenario (activation model) or is indexed by the enumerated distribution does the model stay linear.

## Q9. Which techniques can be combined with Benders decomposition on the Q_s part (assessment)

### Takeaway
Three combinations are workable, in increasing order of tightness and cost: (A) Pantuso-Hewitt distribution-specific cuts when the set of reachable distributions is enumerable (big-M on Q, exact, finitely convergent); (B) "RLT-multiplied Benders cuts" w_s >= p_s(y) * (a_j^T y + b_j) linearized in the multilinear-monomial space with a multilinear-polytope relaxation (exact at binary y, no bound on Q_s, tightness controlled by hypergraph acyclicity, size O(#monomials * S * J)); (C) Medal-Affar successive refinement (Jensen bounds on scenario partitions) as a bound-tightening layer inside branch-and-cut, which needs dual bounds on the recourse LP. SDP, DC and submodular methods do not combine naturally with Benders: SDP adds nothing on the separable bilinear part, DC is a local method, and continuous greedy needs a value oracle rather than cuts.

### Cited Findings
- Pantuso's decomposition: master O(|Z|), distribution-specific optimality cuts with big-M U, finite convergence (Prop. 3), ~1 cut per distribution, solved 2^15 distributions in ~2 h — [arXiv 2509.09816](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09816v1); cuts for linear and integer recourse — [arXiv 2506.12753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12753)
- Medal & Affar: "The core algorithmic framework that we present in this paper is designed to enhance other method[s] for solving (1) rather than completely solve (1) by itself"; requires a tight upper bound on dual multipliers; Jensen lower bound from convexity of Theta in xi — [arXiv 2409.08403](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08403)
- Laumanns chain gives an exact MILP when scenario costs are constant and a mixed-integer bilinear program when recourse depends on decisions — [Laumanns et al.](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/bitstreams/5cd4f601-25ad-46cc-a822-2d281505e874/download)
- Standard linearization is exact iff Berge-acyclic; beta-acyclic extended formulation size (r-1)|V| + |E| variables; general 2^r min{|V|,|F|} — [arXiv 2501.04805](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04805v1)
- SDP gives no benefit over McCormick for separable bilinear programs — [arXiv 2208.00345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00345)
- Cut-and-project (extended-space) cuts are stronger than cuts generated in the Benders master — [Bodur et al. 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/orijoc/v29y2017i1p77-91.html)

### Inferences
- (A) Enumerated-distribution Benders (Pantuso-Hewitt) fits when p_s(y) depends on y only through a small number of zone/pattern indicators (|Z| <= ~15-17 per the reported scale). Cost per iteration: one LP per scenario of the enforced distribution. Weakness: the big-M U on Q enters the conditional cut and the master LP bound is as weak as the McCormick/big-M bound in Q1.
- (B) RLT-multiplied Benders cuts: for each scenario s and each dual extreme point (a_j, b_j) of the recourse LP, add w_s >= sum_M c_{sM} [ sum_i a_{ji} z_{M u {i}} + b_j z_M ] where p_s(y) = sum_M c_{sM} z_M. Because p_s >= 0, these cuts are valid and, at binary y, w_s = p_s(y) Q_s(y) is attained, so the master is exact at integer points without any bound on Q_s. The master is a MILP in (y, z, w) with |monomials| + S variables and S * J constraints; the LP bound equals the bound of the chosen multilinear-polytope relaxation of the monomial set (standard linearization, flower, running-intersection, or the acyclic extended formulations of Q2). For affine p_s the monomials are pairs and the relaxation is the Boolean quadric polytope relaxation; for product-form p_s over site sets A_s it is 2^{|A_s|} variables per s, which is only tractable if the sets A_s are small (local site effects). Cuts can be separated in the extended space and projected, in line with Bodur et al.'s finding that extended-space cuts dominate master-space cuts.
- (C) Successive refinement as a bound layer: SRA's Jensen lower bound E[Theta | x] >= Theta(mean xi(x)) and the concave-penalty upper bound are expressible as rows added in branch-and-cut and are valid for any master including (A) or (B); they require Theta convex in the random parameter (true for RHS/capacity uncertainty in an LP) and dual bounds for the upper-bound side.
- Not naturally Benders-compatible: SDP (no gain on the separable part; a joint moment SDP in y would replace, not complement, Benders); DC/CCP (local, no bound); continuous greedy (needs a value/gradient oracle for g(A) = E_{D(A)}[Q], which is S recourse LPs per evaluation, and a submodularity certificate that holds only in the activation model).

### Gaps
- No paper was found that implements combination (B) (Benders cuts multiplied by multilinear probability factors and placed in a multilinear-polytope relaxation); it is a synthesis of the cited pieces, not a published method.
- No computational comparison between Pantuso-Hewitt cuts and recursive-McCormick deterministic equivalents on the same instances was found, other than Pantuso's own extensive-form baseline.
