# MDP / RL / control reformulation devices for sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y) with binary y

**Standing notation used throughout these notes.** Target problem: min_{y in {0,1}^n} f^T y + F(y), with
F(y) = sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y), Q_s(y) = min { q^T x : W x >= h_s - T y, x >= 0 } (an LP value, convex piecewise-linear in y), p(y) in the simplex Delta_S. "Affine p" means p_s(y) = p_s^0 + a_s^T y; "multilinear p" means p_s is a polynomial that is linear in each y_j separately (e.g. product-form independent-site models). Performative-prediction notation: theta <-> y, D(theta) <-> p(y), loss l(z; theta) <-> Q_s(y) with z = scenario index s, PR(theta) <-> F(y).

Source-access note: full texts were obtained and checked for Perdomo et al. 2020, Miller et al. 2021, Drusvyatskiy & Xiao, Izzo et al., Zahavy et al., Agarwal et al., Basciftci et al., Haus et al. (BDD), Bazotte et al., Pantuso & Hewitt, Morton et al., Mandal et al. 2023, Mandal 2026. Hellemo et al. 2018 and Wiesemann, Kuhn & Rustem 2013 could only be checked through abstracts / secondary restatements (see Gaps).

## KQ1. Occupation-measure LP of MDPs (Manne; Puterman; Altman): does the change of variables x(s,a) = d(s) pi(a|s) absorb p_s(y) Q_s(y)?

### Takeaway
The MDP LP absorbs the policy/state-distribution bilinearity by (i) indexing variables by the *action* (so the action-dependent kernel enters only through constant coefficients) and (ii) allowing randomized policies, whose occupation measures fill a polytope exactly. The literal transfer to the two-stage problem exists and is exact, but it indexes variables by the whole configuration y, so it is an LP with 2^n columns (enumeration). The practically useful descendant of the same change of variables is the product lift z = p_s(y) x_s (or y_j x_s) with McCormick/big-M linearization, which is exact for binary y and affine/multilinear p and yields a MILP, not a convex program; its LP relaxation is a valid bound only.

### Cited Findings
- Manne (1960), "Linear programming and sequential decisions," Management Science 6(3):259-267, formulates a sequential probabilistic model "in terms of (a) an initial decision rule and (b) a Markov process" and optimizes it by LP; the Cowles DP 62 version says the decision variables are "joint probabilities of the state taking a value i and the decision taking a value j" — i.e. the occupation measure in its original form. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v6y1960i3p259-267.html); [Cowles DP 62](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cwl/cwldpp/62.html)
- Exact form of the occupancy polytope (Zahavy et al. 2021, Definition 1, citing Puterman [56]): discounted case K_gamma = { d >= 0 : sum_a d(s,a) = (1-gamma) d_0(s) + gamma sum_{s',a'} P(s | s',a') d(s',a') for all s }; average case K_avg = { d >= 0, sum_{s,a} d = 1, sum_a d(s,a) = sum_{s',a'} P(s|s',a') d(s',a') }. "We note that being a polytope implies that K is a convex and compact set." — [Zahavy et al. 2021, arXiv 2106.00661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00661)
- The RL objective "can be written in terms of the occupancy measure as J_pi = sum_{s,a} r(s,a) d_pi(s,a)" (Proposition 1), and the policy is recovered by pi(s,a) = d_pi(s,a) / sum_a d_pi(s,a) when the denominator is positive, else 1/|A|. — [Zahavy et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00661)
- Definition of the occupancy measure: d^gamma_pi(s,a) = (1-gamma) E sum_t gamma^t Pr_pi(s_t = s) pi(s,a) — the product of state distribution and policy is the single lifted variable. — [Zahavy et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00661)
- Altman (1999) is credited with showing that the constrained MDP "becomes a linear program when reformulated over occupation measures," that optimal CMDP policies "can be randomized," and that the set of occupancy measures "is a polytope." — [arXiv 2603.08956 (survey)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.08956); [arXiv 2306.07001](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.07001)
- Lemma 1 (Zahavy et al.): "There exists an MDP M and convex function f for which there is no stationary reward r such that argmax_{d in K} d.r = argmin_{d in K} f(d)" — "for some choices of f no deterministic policy is optimal." (Randomization is intrinsic to the polytope, not a convenience.) — [Zahavy et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00661)
- The occupation-measure trick breaks when the kernel depends on the *policy* rather than on the action: in performative RL "the feasible region of the optimization problem also changes with time" because P_t = P_{d_t} varies, "the set of optimal solutions need not be convex," and convergence is only to a *stable* point and only after adding a quadratic regularizer -(lambda/2)||d||^2 with lambda above a threshold of order S^{3/2}(2 eps_r + 5 S eps_p)/(1-gamma)^4 (Theorem 1). — [Mandal, Triantafyllou, Radanovic, ICML 2023, arXiv 2207.00046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00046)
- Morton, Dowson & Pagnoncelli (Math. Prog. 2026) on action-dependent transition matrices Phi(y) in policy graphs: "the products between y (or y_d) and V_j(x', omega_j) induce non-convexities ... optimizing over y yields the minimum of a set of convex functions, i.e., it yields a non-convex, but piecewise convex cost-to-go function"; and "it is always possible to express a decision-dependent model as a decision-independent model with an expanded state space." — [arXiv 2509.22981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22981); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-026-02368-8)
- The product-lift that *is* used in the OR literature: Basciftci, Ahmed & Shen (EJOR 2021) take moment functions mu_j(y) = min{ mu_bar_j (1 + sum_{j'} lambda_{jj'} y_{j'}), mu^UB }, dualize the inner worst-case problem, and linearize bilinear terms "w = eta z when eta in [eta_lo, eta_hi] and z in {0,1}" with the McCormick set M = { eta - (1-z) eta_hi <= w <= eta - eta_lo (1-z), eta_lo z <= w <= eta_hi z } — "Because variable z is binary valued, we have an exact reformulation" — and trilinear terms w = eta z_1 z_2 with Proposition 2: for 0 <= eta_lo <= eta_hi the McCormick set equals conv{ (w, eta, z1, z2) : w = eta z1 z2, z in {0,1}^2 }. Products of location binaries are handled by Y_lm := y_l y_m. Result: Theorem 2, "Problem (3) is equivalent to the following mixed-integer linear program (16)." — [arXiv 1912.05577](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05577); [EJOR 292(2):548-561](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v292y2021i2p548-561.html)
- Haus, Michini & Laumanns (BDD scenario aggregation): with independent events whose probabilities are p_i(x) = p_i if x_i = 0 and p_i + Delta_i if x_i = 1, Delta_i in [-p_i, 1-p_i], the scenario probability mass is encoded as a "flow of probability" on a BDD and the decision dependence is imposed by four linear inequalities per BDD node, e.g. p(u) <= (p_i + Delta_i) p(v) + (1 - p_i - Delta_i) p(w) + (1 - x_i) and p(u) <= p_i p(v) + (1-p_i) p(w) + x_i (and the two >= counterparts); the formulation has |V(B)| + |E| variables and 4|V(B)| + 1 constraints. But the BDD can be exponential: the union-of-products special case "is NP-hard, even when the sets M(f) are explicitly given — thus unless P = NP the BDD construction will be exponential in these cases." — [arXiv 1701.04055](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.04055)
- Bazotte, Carvalho & Vidal (2024/25): rewrite the endogenous random vector as xi_x = t(x, theta) with theta exogenous (Lemma 3.1: same optimal solutions); for discrete selection "the binary x selects the distribution, so xi = sum_d theta_{ud} x_u^d is linear in x"; Theorem 4.1: if each p-hat_r^u(x) is "either convex or concave over [0,1]^{n_1}" the inversion constraints have a convex continuous relaxation; otherwise "Bilinear terms are linearized with McCormick envelopes, which yields MILPs for discrete cases." — [arXiv 2402.15486](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15486)
- Pantuso & Hewitt (Math. Prog. 2025): problem min c^T x + sum_d 1_d(x) E_{P_d}[Q(x, xi)] where the sets X_d partition X and "the membership of x to a subset X_d determines a distribution P_d"; the paper calls it "a type of disjunctive program," replaces indicators with binaries delta_d, and derives distribution-specific optimality cuts of the form mu >= (dual-based expected cost at x) - U (1 - 1_{d^v}(x)); Theorem 8: "the L-Shaped algorithm with distribution-specific cuts converges to an optimal solution in a finite number of iterations." Assumption A1: each P_d discrete with finitely many scenarios. — [arXiv 2506.12753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12753)
- Medal & Affar (2024): with product-form decision-dependent probabilities, writing each scenario probability as "a product of the first-stage decision variables" yields "an intractable model with a large number of variable products with high-degree," motivating a successive-refinement branch-and-cut. — [arXiv 2409.08403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08403)

### Inferences
- **Literal transfer (exact, exponential).** The facility problem *is* a one-step MDP with action-dependent transitions: state 0, action y in {0,1}^n, kernel P(s | 0, y) = p_s(y), terminal cost Q_s(y). Its Manne LP has variables d(y) >= 0, sum_y d(y) = 1, objective sum_y d(y) F(y): linear, extreme points deterministic, but 2^n columns each needing |S| LP solves — the MDP "linearity" is action enumeration. The sequential version (state = set opened so far, action = open one more site) has <= 2^n(n+1) occupation variables and the same terminal enumeration. Replacing the pre-solved Q_s(y) by occupation-weighted recourse variables z_{y,s} = d(y) p_s(y) x_{y,s} with W z_{y,s} >= d(y) p_s(y)(h_s - T y) keeps everything linear (p_s(y) and y are constants per column) — an exact LP of size O(2^n (n + |S| dim x)). This is the Dantzig-Wolfe master over configurations; its pricing problem is the original problem, so nothing is gained. This matches Morton et al.'s remark that decision-dependence can always be removed by expanding the state.
- **Why the MDP gets a polynomial LP and we do not.** In the MDP the action is chosen *per state* and the reward is additive over (s,a); the set of occupation measures of randomized per-state policies is a polytope whose description is polynomial because the dynamics factor through single (s,a) pairs. The facility analogue would need (a) p(y) to factor across sites and (b) Q_s(y) to be additive across sites; (b) fails because the recourse LP couples sites through shared demand/capacity rows. Randomizing y with a product (mean-field) distribution does not help: E_{y~Bern(q)}[sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y)] is multilinear in q but not linear, because Q_s is not additive. See KQ5 (rectangularity).
- **Exact "scenario-occupation" product lift for binary y (the useful descendant).** Since p_s(y) >= 0, F(y) = min_{x_1..x_S} sum_s p_s(y) q^T x_s s.t. W x_s >= h_s - T y. With affine p, p_s(y) q^T x_s = p_s^0 q^T x_s + sum_j a_{sj} q^T (y_j x_s). Introduce w_{sj} = y_j x_s with big-M/McCormick (exact for binary y_j given bounds on x_s). Result: an exact MILP with |S| * n * dim(x) extra continuous variables and 4 |S| n dim(x) constraints; multilinear p adds products y_j y_k -> Y_jk (Basciftci's Proposition 2 form). Equivalently, in "occupation" form: rho_s := p_s(y), z_s := p_s(y) x_s, u_s := p_s(y) y, objective sum_s q^T z_s, "flow" constraints W z_s >= rho_s h_s - T u_s, and the realizability constraint (rho, u) in { (p(y), p(y) (x) y) : y in {0,1}^n }. The MDP's flow polytope is replaced by this finite realizability set; its convex hull is not given by the McCormick inequalities, so **the LP relaxation is a valid lower bound but not exact** — linearity is bought with integrality, not with a polytope. For relaxed y in [0,1]^n the lifted constraints z_s = p_s(y) x_s are genuinely bilinear and nonconvex.
- **Distribution-index lift (Pantuso-Hewitt) is the right "state" when p depends on y through a low-dimensional statistic.** If p(y) = p(g(y)) with g taking |K| values (e.g. number of sites opened, or which of a few key sites are open), introduce delta_k = 1[g(y) = k] (linear links to y), then F(y) = sum_k delta_k sum_s p_s(k) Q_s(y) and delta_k x_s products are linearized exactly -> MILP with |K||S| recourse copies, or solved without copies by distribution-specific L-shaped cuts (Theorem 8). With generic affine p every y induces a different distribution (|K| = 2^n) and this collapses back to enumeration.
- The BDD device shows the same pattern: linearity in the probability "flow" is obtained because each node's probability is a *known* affine function of one binary; exactness relies on the independent-events (product-form) structure and the size is governed by the BDD, which is exponential in general.

## KQ2. Policy gradient theorem, performance difference lemma, gradient domination: is there a first-order method for the relaxed problem with a global guarantee?

### Takeaway
The policy-gradient / performative-gradient form of grad F exists and is cheap to compute exactly for a finite scenario set (grad F = sum_s grad p_s(y) Q_s(y) - sum_s p_s(y) T^T pi_s, pi_s the recourse duals). But gradient domination in Agarwal et al. rests on the performance-difference lemma, which has no analogue for F; F is nonconvex on [0,1]^n in general, only (2 eps beta)-weakly convex, so projected gradient converges only to stationary points, and the relaxed optimum is not integral anyway.

### Cited Findings
- Performance difference lemma (Agarwal et al., Lemma 3.2, after Kakade & Langford 2002): V^{pi'}(s_0) - V^pi(s_0) = (1/(1-gamma)) E_{s ~ d^{pi'}_{s_0}} E_{a ~ pi'(.|s)} A^pi(s,a). — [arXiv 1908.00261](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00261)
- Gradient domination (Lemma 4.1, direct parameterization): V*(rho) - V^pi(rho) <= || d^{pi*}_rho / d^pi_mu ||_inf max_{pi-bar} (pi-bar - pi)^T grad_pi V^pi(mu) <= (1/(1-gamma)) || d^{pi*}_rho / mu ||_inf max_{pi-bar} (pi-bar - pi)^T grad_pi V^pi(mu); the proof is three lines from the performance difference lemma. — [arXiv 1908.00261](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00261)
- Lemma 3.1: "There is an MDP M such that the optimization problem V^{pi_theta}(s) is not concave for both the direct and softmax parameterizations" — gradient domination is what rescues non-concave policy optimization. — [arXiv 1908.00261](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00261)
- Theorem 4.1: projected gradient ascent with eta = (1-gamma)^3 / (2 gamma |A|) satisfies min_{t<T} V*(rho) - V^(t)(rho) <= eps whenever T > 64 gamma |S||A| || d^{pi*}_rho / mu ||_inf^2 / ((1-gamma)^6 eps^2). — [arXiv 1908.00261](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00261)
- Policy gradient for the direct parameterization: dV^pi(mu)/d pi(a|s) = (1/(1-gamma)) d^pi_mu(s) Q^pi(s,a) (eq. 7). — [arXiv 1908.00261](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00261)
- The performative-risk gradient (Miller et al., Sec. 4): grad_theta PR(theta) = E_{z~D(theta)} grad_theta l(z;theta) + E_{z~D(theta)} l(z;theta) grad_theta log p_theta(z); "earlier approaches ... such as repeated retraining fail on this problem ... they essentially ignore the second term." — [arXiv 2102.08570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)
- PerfGD (Izzo, Ying & Zou 2021): assumes D(theta) "has a continuously differentiable density p(z; f(theta)), where the functional form of p(z;w) is known and the quantity f(theta) is easily estimatable"; splits the gradient into grad_1 L = E_{D(theta)}[grad l] and grad_2 L (the distribution-shift term), estimates df/dtheta by finite differences df/dtheta ~ Delta f (Delta theta)^+, requiring H >= p previous steps; Theorem 2 gives min_t |grad L_t|^2 = max{ O(l_max sqrt(M G^2 / T + M G^3 delta / g)), O(g + eps*)^2 }, i.e. "PerfGD converges to an approximate critical point"; "if L is alpha-strongly convex, then ... |theta_t - theta_OPT| <= alpha^{-1} |grad L_t|." — [arXiv 2102.07698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07698)
- Mandal (2026) shows the policy-gradient toolkit transfers to performative RL only for *local* (stationarity) stability: a weighted per-state Hedge dynamic drives the local stability gap to zero at O(1/sqrt T) "for an arbitrary, possibly discontinuous, environment map," but "every mixture has global stability gap bounded away from zero" in a constructed instance; the local notion "is equivalent to stationarity." — [arXiv 2609.06467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06467)
- Morton et al. (2026) solve the decision-dependent-transition policy-graph models by SDDP variants whose cuts are built from convex relaxations (Theorems 1, 2 give lower-bounding convex functions V_lower^K <= V^K <= V), and note "we obtain a suboptimal policy because of the problem's non-convexity, but we can assess an optimality gap using bounds." — [arXiv 2509.22981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22981)

### Inferences
- **Exact gradient (policy-gradient form) for the relaxed facility problem.** For y in [0,1]^n with p differentiable and Q_s given by an LP with dual pi_s(y): grad F(y) = sum_s grad p_s(y) Q_s(y) + sum_s p_s(y) grad Q_s(y) = E_{s~p(y)}[ Q_s(y) grad log p_s(y) + grad Q_s(y) ], with grad Q_s(y) = -T^T pi_s(y) (a subgradient at LP degeneracy). This is the REINFORCE/score-function form of the policy gradient theorem with scenario s playing the role of the trajectory. For a finite scenario set with known p it is computable exactly with |S| LP solves; there is no estimation problem, so PerfGD reduces to exact projected gradient on F. With affine p, grad log p_s(y) = a_s / p_s(y).
- **Why gradient domination does not transfer.** The PDL expresses the gap between two policies as an expectation of *one* policy's advantage under the *other* policy's occupancy — a consequence of additive rewards and Markov structure. The facility analogue, F(y') - F(y) = sum_s (p_s(y') - p_s(y)) Q_s(y') + sum_s p_s(y) (Q_s(y') - Q_s(y)), bounds the second term from below by first-order terms (convexity of Q_s) but the first term has no sign. With affine p: F(y') - F(y) - grad F(y)^T (y'-y) >= sum_s (a_s^T (y'-y)) (Q_s(y') - Q_s(y)), which can be negative, so stationary points need not be near-optimal. The deficiency is exactly Miller et al.'s "2 eps beta" term (KQ4).
- **What survives: weak convexity.** For p affine and Q_s L_s-Lipschitz on the box, each term (p_s^0 + a_s^T y) Q_s(y) has Hessian (for smooth Q) p_s(y) grad^2 Q_s + a_s grad Q_s^T + grad Q_s a_s^T, whose minimum eigenvalue is >= -2 ||a_s|| L_s (eigenvalues of a v^T + v a^T are a.v +/- ||a|| ||v||), provided p_s(y) >= 0 on the box. So F is rho-weakly convex with rho <= sum_s 2 ||a_s|| L_s (nonsmooth Q_s by approximation). Consequences: (i) proximal/projected subgradient methods on [0,1]^n converge to stationary points at standard weakly-convex rates, no global guarantee; (ii) because y_j^2 = y_j on binaries, G(y) := F(y) + (rho/2)(||y||^2 - 1^T y) is convex on the box and equals F on {0,1}^n, so the binary problem is equivalent to minimizing a convex (nonsmooth) function over binaries — a convex MINLP whose continuous relaxation gives a lower bound. This is the classic Hammer-Rubin-type convexification; it changes the relaxation, not the complexity.
- The relaxed problem's optimum is generally fractional; even a convex relaxation would require rounding. First-order methods on the relaxation therefore give heuristics plus bounds (if the relaxation is convex), mirroring the Morton et al. SDDP strategy (convex relaxation for cuts, nonconvex model for evaluation).

- No paper was found that applies policy-gradient / gradient-domination analysis to a two-stage stochastic program with decision-dependent probabilities; the inferences above are derived, not cited.
- The weak-convexity modulus and the Hammer-Rubin convexification are standard but I did not fetch a primary source for them; treat as my derivation.

## KQ3. Dual / mirror-descent formulations (REPS, dual policy iteration, convex MDPs): does convexity in the occupation measure transfer to a "scenario occupation measure"?

### Takeaway
Convex-MDP methods need an objective that is a convex function of a variable ranging over an exactly-realizable polytope. For the facility problem the natural scenario measure rho = p(y) does not determine F (Q_s depends on y, not rho), and any lift that does determine F ranges over a finite nonconvex set whose convex hull is exponential. Reparameterizing in the induced distribution (Chen et al. 2023) is the same idea and carries the same requirement; it does not produce convexity here.

### Cited Findings
- Convex MDP: min_{d_pi in K} f(d_pi) with K the occupancy polytope; "both the standard RL problem (Eq. (1)) and the convex MDP problem (Eq. (2)) are convex optimization problems in variable d_pi." — [Zahavy et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00661)
- Fenchel reformulation (eq. 4): f_OPT = min_{d in K} f(d) = min_{d in K} max_{lambda in Lambda} (lambda . d - f*(lambda)) = max_lambda min_d (lambda . d - f*(lambda)), Lambda the closure of the subgradient space; "As both sets are convex, this is a convex-concave saddle-point problem"; for fixed lambda the inner problem "is a standard RL problem ... equivalent to maximizing a reward r = -lambda." Theorem 1: with regret bounds eps_K, delta_K for the two players, the averaged output satisfies f(d-bar) - f_OPT <= eps_K + delta_K, e.g. O(1/sqrt K). — [Zahavy et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00661)
- Non-convex f: "the game max_lambda min_d (lambda . d - f*(lambda)) is concave-convex for any function f ... From weak duality the value of the Lagrangian on the output ... is a lower bound on the optimal solution f_OPT ... L(d-bar, lambda-bar) <= f_OPT <= f(d-bar)." — [Zahavy et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00661)
- Performative RL dual: the Lagrangian with multipliers h(s) on the flow constraints gives a dual that "is strongly convex with modulus A(1-gamma)^2/lambda," and strong duality "relies on the quadratic regularizer"; the shifting feasible set (P depends on d) is why supervised-learning performative arguments do not apply directly. — [Mandal et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00046)
- Reparameterization in the induced distribution (Chen, Tang, Ho & Liu 2023/24): PR(theta) = PR^dagger(phi(theta)) with theta*(phi) the minimum-risk model inducing distribution parameter phi; Assumption 1: PR^dagger is convex and L^dagger-Lipschitz in phi; Example 5: y ~ Unif[0, phi(theta)], loss -(theta x - y)^2, PR(theta) = theta^2 x^2 - theta x phi(theta) + phi(theta)^2/3, with phi(theta) = theta^2 "convex in the distribution parameter" but not in theta; regret O~((d_Theta + d_Phi) N_KL^{1/6} N^{5/6}). — [arXiv 2305.01094](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01094)
- Survey of performative prediction (Kehrenberg et al., Feb 2026) classifies settings by knowledge of the distribution map and solution methods for stability vs. optimality. — [arXiv 2602.10176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10176)

### Inferences
- **No convex "scenario occupation measure."** Candidate rho = p(y) in Delta_S: F(y) = sum_s rho_s Q_s(y) is not a function of rho alone unless p is injective; with affine p and relaxed y, y = A^+(rho - p^0) on the image and F(rho) = sum_s rho_s Q_s(A^+(rho - p^0)) is still a product of a linear and a convex function of rho — nonconvex in general. With binary y the image of p is a finite set, so "convexity in rho" is vacuous. The joint lift (rho, u = rho (x) y, z = rho x) makes the objective linear (KQ1) but the realizable set is finite and nonconvex; its convex hull is the configuration master polytope (exponential). Chen et al.'s Assumption 1 (convexity of PR^dagger in the distribution parameter) is exactly the missing ingredient and is not implied by anything in the facility structure.
- **What the Fenchel game does give.** Zahavy's non-convex remark transfers: for any lift with a linear objective over a nonconvex realizable set R, max_lambda min_{r in conv R} gives weak-duality bounds; but min over conv R of a linear function equals min over R, so the lower bound is the LP over conv R — again the exponential master. With the McCormick relaxation in place of conv R one recovers the MILP's LP bound.
- **REPS / mirror descent analogue.** REPS's primal is a linear objective over state-action distributions with a KL trust region; the facility analogue is mirror descent over a distribution on {0,1}^n (exponential) or over a product/mean-field distribution q in [0,1]^n, whose objective E_{y~q} F(y) is the multilinear extension — nonconvex, so mirror descent yields stationary points only. (Mandal 2026 is the RL-side evidence that mixtures over policies buy local, not global, stability when transitions are performative.)

- No source treats a dual/saddle reformulation of a decision-dependent two-stage program; inferences are mine.
- Did not fetch REPS (Peters, Muelling & Altun 2010) or Dual Policy Iteration (Sun et al. 2018) primary texts; their primal forms are stated from background knowledge and used only qualitatively.

## KQ4. Performative prediction / decision-dependent stochastic optimization: exact convexity conditions and whether affine or multilinear p_s(y) satisfies them

### Takeaway
Affine scenario probabilities satisfy Miller et al.'s mixture dominance with equality (their Bernoulli(a^T theta + b) remark generalizes directly), but convexity of PR additionally needs the loss to be gamma-strongly convex with gamma > 2 eps beta; Q_s is piecewise-linear (gamma = 0), so Theorem 3.1 yields only (2 eps beta)-weak convexity, consistent with simple counterexamples. Perdomo-type results (RRM/RGD convergence, stable-optimal gap 2 L_z eps / gamma) also degenerate at gamma = 0. Multilinear p on the relaxed box does not satisfy mixture dominance in general.

### Cited Findings
- Definitions (Perdomo et al. 2020): PR(theta) = E_{Z~D(theta)} l(Z;theta); Definition 2.1 theta_PO = argmin_theta E_{Z~D(theta)} l(Z;theta); Definition 2.3 theta_PS = argmin_theta E_{Z~D(theta_PS)} l(Z;theta), with DPR(theta, theta') = E_{Z~D(theta)} l(Z;theta'); "Performatively stable models are fixed points of risk minimization." Definition 3.1: D is eps-sensitive if W_1(D(theta), D(theta')) <= eps ||theta - theta'||_2. — [arXiv 2002.06673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06673)
- Theorem 3.5 (RRM): loss beta-jointly smooth and gamma-strongly convex, D eps-sensitive; (a) the RRM map is eps(beta/gamma)-Lipschitz; (b) if eps < gamma/beta, RRM converges linearly to a unique stable point, ||theta_t - theta_PS|| <= delta for t >= (1 - eps beta/gamma)^{-1} log(||theta_0 - theta_PS||/delta); Proposition 3.6(c) gives a counterexample where RRM fails to converge when eps >= gamma/beta. Theorem 3.8 (RGD): contraction if eps < gamma/((beta+gamma)(1 + 1.5 eta beta)) and eta <= 2/(beta+gamma). Theorem 4.3: if l is L_z-Lipschitz in z and gamma-strongly convex and D is eps-sensitive, ||theta_PO - theta_PS||_2 <= 2 L_z eps / gamma. Proposition 4.2: "The performative risk PR(theta) can be concave in theta" even when the loss is beta-jointly smooth, gamma-strongly convex and eps < gamma/beta. — [arXiv 2002.06673 (HTML v4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2002.06673v4)
- Perdomo Example 2.2 (biased coin): X in {+-1}, Y|X ~ Bernoulli(1/2 + mu X + eps theta X), squared loss, linear predictor theta x + 1/2: theta_PO = mu/(1 - 2 eps) while theta_PS = mu/(1 - eps); "they identify if and only if eps = 0." (A discrete, affine-in-theta probability model where stable and optimal points differ.) — [arXiv 2002.06673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06673)
- Miller, Perdomo & Zrnic (ICML 2021) assumptions: (A1) eps-sensitivity in W_1; (A2) beta-smooth in z: ||grad_theta l(z;theta) - grad_theta l(z';theta)|| <= beta ||z - z'||; (A3a) gamma-strong convexity in theta under every D(theta_0); (A3b) gamma_z-strong convexity in z; (A4) mixture dominance: for all theta, theta', theta_0 and alpha in (0,1), E_{z ~ D(alpha theta + (1-alpha) theta')} l(z; theta_0) <= E_{z ~ alpha D(theta) + (1-alpha) D(theta')} l(z; theta_0). "mixture dominance (A4) essentially posits convexity of DPR in the first argument. If mixture dominance (A4) holds, then the performative risk is lambda-convex for lambda = gamma - 2 eps beta." The threshold gamma/(2 beta) is sharp: Proposition 2.1's example (point mass at eps theta, loss -beta theta^T z + (gamma/2)||theta||^2) is a location family satisfying mixture dominance, yet "for eps > gamma/(2 beta) the performative risk is strictly concave" and "the unique stable point theta_PS maximizes the performative risk." — [arXiv 2102.08570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)
- When mixture dominance holds: location-scale families z = (Sigma_0 + Sigma(theta)) z_0 + mu_0 + mu theta with linear Sigma(.), mu "satisfy mixture dominance if the loss l is convex" (Proposition A.4: D(alpha theta + (1-alpha) theta') <=_cx alpha D(theta) + (1-alpha) D(theta')); "Mixture dominance can also hold in discrete settings, e.g. D(theta) = Bernoulli(a^T theta + b) satisfies this condition for any loss." - Drusvyatskiy & Xiao (Math. - Narang, Faulkner, Drusvyatskiy, Fazel & Ratliff (JMLR 2023): multi-player performative prediction; "performatively stable equilibria" found by repeated retraining / stochastic gradient under mild assumptions; Nash equilibria require (strong) monotonicity of the game, with "transparent sufficient conditions." — [arXiv 2201.03398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03398)
- Mandal 2026: Li & Wai (2024) and Hikima & Takeda (2025) "study stationary points of possibly non-convex performative risk under a decision dependent, distributionally robust formulation"; stationarity is the operative target when convexity is unavailable. — [arXiv 2609.06467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06467)

### Inferences
- **Affine p satisfies mixture dominance with equality.** For D(y) = sum_s p_s(y) delta_{xi_s} with p affine, E_{D(alpha y + (1-alpha) y')} l(.; y_0) = sum_s p_s(alpha y + (1-alpha) y') Q_s(y_0) = alpha sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y_0) + (1-alpha) sum_s p_s(y') Q_s(y_0), which is the right-hand side of (A4). This is the S-scenario version of the Bernoulli(a^T theta + b) remark. For multilinear p (e.g. p_s(y) = prod_j q_{sj}(y_j)) on the relaxed box, p_s(alpha y + (1-alpha) y') differs from the mixture by cross terms whose sign depends on Q_s(y_0), so (A4) fails in general; on binary y the condition is not meaningful (no segments).
- **But Theorem 3.1 needs gamma > 2 eps beta, and gamma = 0 here.** Q_s is convex piecewise-linear, not strongly convex, so lambda = -2 eps beta: F is 2 eps beta-weakly convex at best (matching the Hessian bound in KQ2). Instantiating the constants on a discrete scenario space with the discrete metric: W_1 = TV = (1/2)||p(y) - p(y')||_1 <= eps ||y - y'||_2 with eps = (1/2) ||A||_{2->1} (A the matrix of a_s); beta = max_{s,s'} ||T^T (pi_s - pi_{s'})|| over recourse duals. Counterexample confirming nonconvexity: S = 2, p_1 = y, p_2 = 1 - y on [0,1], Q_1(y) = (1-y)^2 (convex), Q_2 = 0: F = y(1-y)^2 has F'' = 6y - 4 < 0 for y < 2/3.
- **Stability vs optimality for the facility problem.** The equilibrium / RRM device is "freeze the probabilities at the last solution and solve an exogenous two-stage SMIP": y_{t+1} in argmin_y f^T y + sum_s p_s(y_t) Q_s(y). Each step is a standard Benders-decomposable problem, so this is an attractive heuristic; but Theorem 3.5's contraction needs strong convexity (absent for binary y) and Theorem 4.3's gap bound 2 L_z eps / gamma is vacuous at gamma = 0; cycling is possible. Perdomo's Example 2.2 shows the stable and optimal points differ even for an affine Bernoulli model with squared loss.
- Narang et al.'s multiplayer theory is relevant only if several firms locate facilities and demand responds to all of them; for a single decision maker it adds nothing beyond the single-player conditions.

- Could not fetch Drusvyatskiy & Xiao's main convergence theorems with rates (only assumptions, lemmas and the equilibrium definition were extracted); rates are in their Table 1 / Sections 5-9.
- No literature was found that evaluates mixture dominance or weak convexity specifically for finite-scenario, affine-probability models in stochastic programming.

## KQ5. Robust MDPs and rectangularity (Iyengar 2005; Nilim & El Ghaoui 2005; Wiesemann, Kuhn & Rustem 2013): is there a "site-rectangularity" that decomposes the endogenous facility problem?

### Takeaway
Rectangularity makes the robust Bellman recursion valid by letting nature pick transition laws independently per state (or state-action), so the inner problem is a small LP in the probabilities for a fixed value vector; non-rectangular sets make even policy evaluation strongly NP-hard. The facility analogue ("site-rectangularity") would require both p(y) and Q_s to factor across sites, which the coupled recourse LP violates. What does transfer is the *fixed-y linearity in p* that underlies decision-dependent DRO with y-affine ambiguity sets, where dual-variable x binary products are linearized exactly (Basciftci et al.).

### Cited Findings
- (s,a)-rectangular sets: "an (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty set is of the form P-hat = x_{s in S, a in A(s)} P-hat_{s,a}," i.e. "the realization of the transition probabilities can be chosen independently for each state-action pair" (attributed to Iyengar / Nilim & El Ghaoui); s-rectangular (Wiesemann et al.): "the transition probabilities can be selected independently for each state" while "transition probabilities for different actions in the same state may be dependent," P-hat = x_s P-hat_s. — [arXiv 2412.12879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12879)
- For s-rectangular sets "an optimal randomized policy can be computed in polynomial time" by "a robust value iteration approach"; for (s,a)-rectangular sets randomization does not improve the worst case, "this is no longer true for" s-rectangular sets. Wiesemann et al. "consider convex, otherwise arbitrary uncertainty sets ... determining the worst-case realization for a given policy is already strongly NP-hard." — [arXiv 2412.12879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12879); corroborated by [arXiv 2508.07790](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07790) ("NP-hard for general uncertainty sets, even convex ones"; "deterministic policies may not be optimal under s-rectangularity")
- Bibliographic record: Wiesemann, Kuhn & Rustem, "Robust Markov Decision Processes," Math. OR 38(1):153-183, 2013, DOI 10.1287/moor.1120.0566. — [EPFL Infoscience](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/handle/20.500.14299/100036)
- Recent s-rectangular algorithms (Ho, Petrik & Wiesemann, Feb 2026) for 1-norm, 2-norm and phi-divergence ambiguity sets. — [arXiv 2602.05591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05591)
- Decision-dependent DRO facility location: inner problem max over pi in U(y) of E[h(y, d(y))] with y-dependent moment functions; Proposition 1 gives a closed form by decomposing "with respect to each location"; Theorem 1 dualizes to an objective with products delta_j^h mu_j(y), gamma_j^h (sigma_j^2(y) + mu_j(y)^2) of dual variables and y-affine functions; Theorem 2 linearizes them exactly into MILP (16) via McCormick sets for binary x continuous and binary x binary x continuous products. The literature review notes that Noyan et al. and Luo & Mehrotra "provide nonconvex reformulations for DRO problems under various forms of decision-dependent ambiguity sets." — [arXiv 1912.05577](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05577)
- Multimodal decision-dependent ambiguity sets (2024): "given binary valued first-stage decisions, exact reformulations for bilinear terms can be provided using McCormick envelopes." — [arXiv 2404.19185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19185)

### Inferences
- **Rectangularity = separability of nature's choice across the index that the Bellman recursion sums over.** In the facility problem the analogue index is the site j. "Site-rectangularity" would mean p(y) = prod_j p_j(y_j) (independent site-level scenarios) *and* Q_s(y) = sum_j Q_{s_j}(y_j); then F(y) = sum_j sum_{s_j} p_{s_j}(y_j) Q_{s_j}(y_j) and the problem splits into n one-dimensional problems. The recourse LP shares demand-assignment and capacity rows across sites, so the second condition fails; this is the "non-rectangular" regime where Wiesemann et al.'s hardness result lives, and nothing suggests decomposition.
- **What does transfer: fixed-y linearity in p.** For fixed y, sum_s p_s Q_s(y) is linear in p. In the DRO variant sup_{p in P(y)} sum_s p_s Q_s(y) with P(y) polyhedral and y-affine data, LP duality yields dual variables multiplied by y, which McCormick linearizes exactly for binary y (Basciftci Theorem 2). In the point-probability variant p = p(y), the same mechanism produces the product lift of KQ1. Either way the device is "binary x continuous product = exact MILP," not convexity.
- The Nilim/Iyengar inner LP ("nature best-responds to a fixed value vector") is the robust counterpart of the RRM/equilibrium iteration in KQ4: alternate between fixing y (linear in p) and fixing p (an exogenous SMIP in y). It is a heuristic without a contraction guarantee for binary y.

### Gaps
- The original Wiesemann-Kuhn-Rustem text (definitions, Proposition 1 on randomized policies, NP-hardness theorem) was not accessible; statements are via two 2024-25 arXiv papers that restate them. The 6-page PDF at optimization-online 2012/02/3352 turned out to be a different paper.

## KQ6. Stochastic shortest path / optimal stopping: recast facility opening as a finite-horizon MDP with a linear LP formulation — what is the state space?

### Takeaway
Yes, exactly and trivially: opening sites one at a time gives a finite-horizon MDP whose state is the set of opened sites; its Manne LP is exact (extreme points = deterministic opening sequences) but has Theta(2^n) states, and the terminal reward -sum_s p_s(S) Q_s(S) must be evaluated (or embedded as occupation-weighted recourse LPs) for every subset S. The LP "linearity" is enumeration; it compresses only when p depends on y through a small statistic and Q_s can be written against that statistic plus linearized products.

### Cited Findings
- "it is always possible to express a decision-dependent model as a decision-independent model with an expanded state space. We advocate using the decision-dependent policy graph because of the convenience and clarity that it provides the modeler." Example 6 uses a one-step transition matrix [(0.5 + 0.3y) (0.5 - 0.3y); ...] with y in {0,1}. — [Morton, Dowson & Pagnoncelli 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22981)
- Morton et al.'s SDDP for decision-dependent transitions pulls y back one period and carries it in the state, restricting to extreme points of the polytope Y "assumed to be modest in number"; subproblems "require binary variables to handle y'_j in ext(Y) and linearizations for bilinear terms involving binaries"; "we obtain a suboptimal policy because of the problem's non-convexity, but we can assess an optimality gap." — [arXiv 2509.22981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22981)
- In the BDD approach the decision-dependent scenario probabilities become a "flow of probability on the DAGs represented by the BDDs," shaped by binary first-stage decisions via linear inequalities, with the BDD size exponential in the worst case (UPP NP-hard). — [arXiv 1701.04055](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.04055)
- Pantuso & Hewitt's disjunctive formulation with finitely many distributions, exact L-shaped with distribution-specific cuts, finite convergence (Theorem 8); integer-second-stage case bounded by 2^{n_1} cuts under A4 (X = {0,1}^{n_1}). — [arXiv 2506.12753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12753)
- Zahavy's occupancy polytope K_gamma (KQ1) is the LP feasible set for any such finite MDP. — [arXiv 2106.00661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00661)

### Inferences
- **Construction.** Stages t = 0..n; state (S_t, t) with S_t the set opened so far (optionally only the sites with index > last opened, to remove ordering symmetry: then states are subsets with a "last index" marker, still Theta(2^n)); actions: open site j not in S, or stop; stopping (or reaching t = n) transitions to terminal state (S, s) with probability p_s(S); terminal cost Q_s(S). Occupation LP: variables d(S, a), flow constraints as in K_gamma with gamma = 1 and horizon n, objective sum_{S} d(S, stop) F(S) (+ opening costs). Exact; |states| <= 2^n (n+1), coefficient F(S) needs |S_scen| LPs per subset, or embed z_{S,s} = d(S,stop) p_s(S) x_{S,s} with W z_{S,s} >= d(S,stop) p_s(S)(h_s - T 1_S), still linear because p_s(S) and 1_S are constants per column. Total size O(2^n (n + |S_scen| dim x)) — the configuration master LP of KQ1.
- **When it compresses.** If p_s(y) = p_s(g(y)) with g a low-cardinality statistic (count opened, or status of k "anchor" sites), the MDP state can be the statistic (|K| states), but Q_s(y) still depends on the full y; the exact formulation then is Pantuso-Hewitt's disjunction delta_k = 1[g(y) = k] with products delta_k x_s linearized, size |K||S_scen| recourse copies, or their cutting-plane variant. If moreover the recourse LP decomposes by site (site-rectangular, KQ5) the chain reduces to a per-site DP. Neither condition holds for generic affine p with coupled recourse.
- A "stochastic shortest path" view adds nothing beyond this: the SSP LP is the same occupancy LP with the terminal set absorbing; optimal stopping structure (open until marginal value negative) only gives an index policy if F is separable/submodular, which is not implied.

### Gaps
- No published MDP/SSP reformulation of endogenous facility location was found; the construction is mine. Morton et al. is the nearest (multi-stage, SDDP, action-dependent Markov chain).

## KQ7. Direct work 2018-2026 on facility location / network design / combinatorial stochastic programs with decision-dependent probabilities via RL or MDP reformulations

### Takeaway
No paper was found that uses RL or an MDP reformulation to solve a combinatorial two-stage program with decision-dependent scenario probabilities. The 2018-2026 OR literature instead converges on: exact MILP via binary x continuous linearization (Basciftci-Ahmed-Shen 2021; Bhuiyan-Medal-Harun 2020; Bazotte et al. 2024/25), exact combinatorial encodings of product-form probabilities (Haus et al. BDD), distribution-specific L-shaped cuts (Pantuso-Hewitt 2025), successive refinement for product-form probabilities (Medal-Affar 2024), and SDDP with convex relaxations for action-dependent Markov chains (Morton et al. 2026). RL for two-stage programs exists only with exogenous probabilities.

### Cited Findings
- Hellemo, Barton & Tomasgard (Comput. Manag. Sci. 15:369-395, 2018), abstract: "a taxonomy of stochastic programming recourse models with decision-dependent uncertainty"; "Two-stage models are formulated where prior probabilities are distorted through an affine transformation or combined using a convex combination of several probability distributions"; distributions "incorporated in the model using the exact expression or by using a rational approximation"; "Test instances for each formulation are solved with a commercial solver, BARON, using selective branching." — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10287-018-0330-0); [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/117361)
- Basciftci, Ahmed & Shen, "Distributionally robust facility location problem under decision-dependent stochastic demand," EJOR 292(2):548-561, 2021: piecewise-linear moment functions of y; "exact mixed-integer linear programming reformulation ... through duality and convex envelopes"; valid inequalities (Proposition 3); "decision-dependent DRO has not been studied within the facility location context to derive tractable reformulations" before this work. — [arXiv 1912.05577](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05577)
- Bhuiyan, Medal & Harun (EJOR 2020), "A stochastic programming model with endogenous and exogenous uncertainty for reliable network design under random disruption": facility capacity depends probabilistically on protection decisions; "a reformulation that linearizes the nonlinear, non-convex model into a mixed-integer linear stochastic program," solved by an accelerated L-shaped algorithm. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037722172030134X) (abstract-level, via search summary)
- Haus, Michini & Laumanns, "Scenario aggregation using binary decision diagrams for stochastic programs with endogenous uncertainty" (2017): applies to "stochastic network design, network reliability, or stochastic network interdiction problems"; earlier approaches to the type-2 endogenous class "involve products or other non-linear expressions of decision variables"; [1] (Ahmed 2000 thesis) and [17] (Flach & Poggi 2010) "used convexification techniques to deal with these polynomials, while [37] have relied on linear approximations." — [arXiv 1701.04055](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.04055)
- Bazotte, Carvalho & Vidal (2024, v4 July 2025), "Solving two-stage programs with endogenous uncertainty via random variable transformation": covers discrete selection, Bernoulli, binomial, general discrete, uniform/exponential, location-scale families; SAA on the transformed program gives "an over-estimated optimality gap about 24 percentage points better, on average, than solving the mixed-integer deterministic equivalent for NDFPP-Selection." — [arXiv 2402.15486](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15486)
- Pantuso & Hewitt, "The L-shaped method for stochastic programs with decision-dependent uncertainty," Math. - Medal & Affar (2024), successive refinement within branch-and-cut for decision-dependent random capacities (product-form probabilities). — [arXiv 2409.08403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08403)
- Morton, Dowson & Pagnoncelli, "MDP modeling for multi-stage stochastic programs," Math. - RL for two-stage programs with *exogenous* probabilities: Yilmaz & Buyuktahtakin's multi-agent DRL (one agent for first stage, one for recourse; reported 6-10% gaps) and HGCN2SP (2025, RL for scenario reduction); neither has decision-dependent probabilities. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371175133_A_deep_reinforcement_learning_framework_for_solving_two-stage_stochastic_programs); [arXiv 2511.16027](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16027)
- Performative RL family (policy-dependent transitions, not combinatorial first stage): Mandal et al. ICML 2023; Rank et al. 2024 (gradually shifting environments, arXiv 2402.09838); Mandal & Radanovic 2024 (linear MDPs, arXiv 2411.05234); Mandal 2026 (mixtures, local vs global stability). — [arXiv 2207.00046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00046); [arXiv 2609.06467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06467)

### Inferences
- The field's consensus device for binary first stage is the exact binary x continuous linearization (McCormick/big-M), yielding MILPs whose LP relaxations are the only convex objects available; every "exact" method found (BDD flows, distribution-specific cuts, successive refinement, SDDP with relaxations) is a way of organizing enumeration or branch-and-bound over the induced distributions, consistent with KQ1 and KQ6.
- The performative-RL literature is the closest RL analogue to the facility problem and it does *not* restore convexity; it retreats to performative stability with regularization (Mandal 2023) or to local stationarity (Mandal 2026). This is strong evidence that an RL reformulation would not change the complexity class of the facility problem.

- Bhuiyan et al. (2020) details come from a search summary of the abstract, not the paper.
- Ahmed (2000) thesis chapter on decision-dependent probabilities and Flach & Poggi (2010) are cited only through Haus et al.'s related-work paragraph.
