# Experimental design, active learning, VOI and information acquisition: structural results for action-dependent distributions, applied to sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y)

Scope note. The target object is the two-stage facility-location objective c'x + sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y) where the first-stage decision y (facility opening, or sampling effort) changes the scenario probabilities p_s. Two variants: (a) mean-shifting (opening a facility moves nearby mean demand), (b) variance-reducing (first-stage sampling/market research sharpens the demand distribution; "endogenous demand learning"). Everything below is catalogued by the exact structural property it delivers: convexity/concavity in a belief, concavity in an "amount of information", submodularity in a set of experiments, or a linear (LP/MIP) reformulation in a lifted variable.

Notation used throughout: mu = belief (prior/posterior) over demand scenarios; tau = distribution over posteriors; W(mu) = min_x [c'x + E_mu Q(x, xi)] (the recourse-optimal cost at belief mu); V = analogous max-form value.

The arXiv identifier 2609.37489 given in the brief could not be located by any search (see Gaps in Q0). All findings about "endogenous demand learning" below are therefore drawn from the closest located literature (Ma–Kim–Linderoth–Luedtke–Matthews 2024 "Probing-Enhanced Stochastic Programming", which has exactly the "probe customers' demand before locating facilities" structure).

## Q0. Status of the target paper (arXiv 2609.37489) and nearest OR precedents

### Takeaway
The identifier 2609.37489 returned nothing in any search; the nearest located precedents are (i) Ma, Kim, Linderoth, Luedtke, Matthews (2024) "Probing-Enhanced Stochastic Programming", whose computational study is a stochastic facility location and sizing problem "with the option to probe customers to learn about their demands before building facilities" (variant (b)), and (ii) Pantuso (2025) and Basciftci–Ahmed–Shen (2021) for variant (a).

### Cited Findings
- Ma et al. 2024 model: "a two-stage stochastic decision problem where the decision-maker has the opportunity to obtain information about the distribution of the random variables ξ ... through a set of discrete actions that we refer to as probing. Probing components of a random vector η that is jointly-distributed with ξ allows the decision-maker to learn about the conditional distribution of ξ given the observed components of η." Three-stage model: stage 1 choose which components of η to observe; later decisions must be consistent with the obtained information. Computational study: "a stochastic facility location and sizing problem with the option to probe customers to learn about their demands before building facilities." — [Ma, Kim, Linderoth, Luedtke, Matthews, arXiv 2407.10669 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10669)
- Ma et al. classify decision-dependent uncertainty following Tarhan et al. 2009 and Hellemo et al. 2018 into "decision-dependent probabilities" (distribution of ξ is a function of decisions) and "decision-dependent information structure" (timing of revelation is decision-dependent). — [arXiv 2407.10669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10669)
- Pantuso (2025), "Solution of Stochastic Facility Location Problems with Combinatorially many Decision-Dependent Distributions": distribution of demand is a function of the set of open facilities (via zone activation), giving up to 2^|Z| candidate distributions; demand mean and standard deviation are linear in zone-activation indicators (μ_jd = μ_j + Σ_n (−1)^{s(n)} α_nj μ_j 1_{Z_d}(...), σ_jd = σ_j − Σ_n (−1)^{s(n)} β_nj σ_j 1_{Z_d}(...)); solved exactly by a decision-dependent L-shaped method with distribution-specific optimality cuts; instances with up to 2^17 potential distributions and hundreds of thousands of scenarios solved within minutes. — [arXiv 2509.09816](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09816v1); same paper on [Optimization Online (Sept 2025)](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/preprint.pdf)
- Basciftci, Ahmed, Shen (EJOR 2021): distributionally robust facility location where demand moments are functions of location decisions; "Exact mixed-integer linear programming reformulations and valid inequalities are derived for piecewise linear means and variances." — [IDEAS/EJOR 292(2) 548-561](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v292y2021i2p548-561.html)
- Pantuso 2025 states that the facility-location-with-decision-dependent-uncertainty literature "counts, to our knowledge, only the recent article by Basciftci et al." — [Optimization Online preprint](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/preprint.pdf)

## Q1. Value of information: convexity in the belief, Blackwell ordering, and submodularity in the set of experiments (Howard; Raiffa–Schlaifer; Blackwell; Krause–Guestrin 2005/2009)

### Takeaway
The decision value is convex (max-form) / concave (min-form) in the belief, so expected cost under any information structure is linear in the induced distribution over posteriors and improves with Blackwell-more-informative experiments; but decision-theoretic value of information is provably NOT submodular in the set of experiments (Krause–Guestrin 2009, Prop. 9, even in Naive Bayes), and only entropy/information-gain surrogates are submodular, under a conditional-independence condition.

### Cited Findings
- Blackwell's theorem (as stated by Gentzkow–Kamenica): "E_{⟨π|μ⟩} H(μ_s) ≤ H(μ) for all π and μ if and only if H is concave." A signal π with prior μ induces a distribution of posteriors τ = ⟨π|μ⟩ ∈ Δ(Δ(Ω)). — [Gentzkow & Kamenica, "Costly Persuasion", AEA 2014 PDF, Sec. 2](http://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2014/retrieve.php?pdfid=417)
- Blackwell equivalence (three conditions): σ′ yields weakly lower expected utility than σ in every decision problem ⟺ σ′ is a garbling of σ ⟺ the feasible-strategy set under σ′ is contained in that under σ. — [Wikipedia, Blackwell's informativeness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwell%27s_informativeness_theorem); convex-function formulation ("for every continuous convex v, E v(posterior) is at least as large under the more informative experiment") — [Tamuz lecture notes](https://tamuz.caltech.edu/teaching/info.pdf)
- LP-valued payoff is convex in the belief: Ryzhov–Powell (SIAM J. Optim. 2012) Proposition 2.2: "For all n and all knowledge states k^n = (c^n, Σ^n), V(c^n) ≤ E^n V(c_true) almost surely." Proof: "The optimal value function V is convex in c (concave for minimization problems ...). Applying Jensen's inequality together with the definition of c^n, we obtain V(c^n) = V(E^n c_true) ≤ E^n V(c_true)." Corollary 2.3: E^π V(c^N) ≤ E V(c_true) for every policy π (perfect-information bound). — [Ryzhov & Powell 2012, Princeton PDF](https://optimallearning.princeton.edu/Papers/Ryzhov%20Powell%20-%20Information%20collection%20for%20Linear%20Programs_SIAM_October2012.pdf)
- Submodularity of information gain requires conditional independence. Krause & Guestrin (UAI 2005): information gain I(Z;·) "is not submodular" in general (XOR counterexample: Z = X XOR Y, H(Z)=H(Z|X)=H(Z|Y)=1 but H(Z|X,Y)=0). Proposition 2: "Let S, U be disjoint subsets of V, such that the variables in S are independent given U. Let information gain be F(A) = H(U) − H(U|A) ... for any W ⊆ S ∪ U. Then F is submodular and non-decreasing on W, and F(∅)=0." Greedy achieves F(A) ≥ (1−1/e) OPT (Theorem 6) and the problem is not approximable better than (1−1/e) unless P=NP (Theorem 9). — [Krause & Guestrin, arXiv 1207.1394](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.1394)
- Decision-theoretic VOI is not submodular. Krause & Guestrin (JAIR 35, 2009): "Unfortunately, decision theoretic value of information does not satisfy submodularity. Proposition 9 (Non-submodularity of value of information). Decision-theoretic value of information is not submodular, even in Naive Bayes models. Intuitively, value of information can be non-submodular, if we need to make several observations in order to 'convince' ourselves that we need to change our action." — [Krause & Guestrin, JAIR 2009 / arXiv 1401.3474](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3474)
- Hardness: "just computing decision theoretic value of information objective functions ... is a #P-complete problem even on Naive Bayes models"; optimizing it is NP^PP-complete even for discrete polytrees; exact DP algorithms exist for chain graphical models (Theorems 1, 2). — [arXiv 1401.3474](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3474)
- Sensor placement confirmation: Malings & Pozzi (RESS 2019) "Submodularity issues in value-of-information-based sensor placement": VoI "lacks the property of submodularity"; "greedy optimization approaches using value of information can lead to sub-optimal solutions"; they propose a submodular surrogate (conditional entropy). — [IDEAS/RESS 183:93-103](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reensy/v183y2019icp93-103.html)
- Adaptive submodularity (Golovin & Krause, JAIR 2011): Definition 2 (adaptive monotone: conditional expected marginal benefit Δ(e|ψ) ≥ 0), Definition 3 (adaptive submodular: marginal benefit of a fixed item does not increase as more items are selected/observed); Theorem 5 gives greedy (1 − e^{−1/α}) for α-approximate greedy, i.e. (1−1/e) for exact greedy. Stated limits: "not directly applicable" with synergistic effects between items conditioned on observations; does not cover settings where "items selection can alter the underlying realization"; "pointwise submodularity is not sufficient to establish adaptive submodularity." — [Golovin & Krause, ar5iv 1003.3967](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1003.3967)
- Monotonicity of sampling-value in the allocation (ranking & selection): "for any sampling allocations Z1 and Z2, if Z1 ⊆ Z2 then v(Z1) ≤ v(Z2)". — [Finite-time analysis of KG, arXiv 1606.04624](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.04624)
- Monotonicity in the probe set for a two-stage SP: Ma et al. Lemma 1: "If S ⊆ T ⊆ [n], then F(S) ≤ F(T)" where F(S) = E_{η_S}[R(η_S)], R(η_S) = max_y E[β'y + Q(y,ξ) | η_S]; F([n]) is the perfect-information bound. No submodularity of F is claimed. — [arXiv 2407.10669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10669)

### Inferences
- For the facility problem, W(mu) = min_x [c'x + Σ_s mu_s Q_s(x)] is a minimum of finitely many linear functions of mu, hence concave piecewise-linear in mu; Jensen gives E_tau[W(mu_s)] ≤ W(mu_0): sampling can only reduce expected cost. This is the exact min-form of Ryzhov–Powell Prop 2.2. It gives CONCAVITY IN THE BELIEF, not in y.
- If y_j selects an experiment, the expected recourse cost G(y) = E_{tau(y)}[W(mu_s)] is monotone in Blackwell order (and in set inclusion of probes), but by Krause–Guestrin Prop 9 / Malings–Pozzi it is NOT submodular/supermodular in the set of experiments in general. Greedy guarantees exist only for surrogates (entropy, mutual information) under conditional independence (KG05 Prop 2). For variant (b) with independent demands across customers (demands conditionally independent given site-specific parameters) the conditional-independence condition of Prop 2 is natural, so the entropy surrogate IS submodular there—but the surrogate is not the recourse cost.
- Variant (a) (opening changes the realization's distribution) is exactly the case Golovin–Krause flag as outside adaptive submodularity ("item selection can alter the underlying realization").

### Gaps
- No source found stating a sufficient condition under which decision-theoretic VOI for an LP recourse (as opposed to entropy) is submodular; the literature only gives negative results and surrogates.
- Howard 1966 and Raiffa–Schlaifer were not fetched directly; the convexity-in-belief claim is sourced via Ryzhov–Powell and Blackwell statements.

## Q2. Bayesian optimal experimental design (Lindley; Chaloner–Verdinelli; Foster et al.; Rainforth et al. 2024): bounds and reformulations for the design-dependent marginal

### Takeaway
BED treats the design-dependent marginal p(y|ξ) by always sampling from the joint p(θ)p(y|θ,ξ), so the design enters only the integrand; the resulting EIG is "doubly intractable", estimated by biased NMC or by variational/contrastive lower and upper bounds that are optimized by stochastic gradient; no source claims concavity of EIG in the design, and the decision-theoretic version (Decision Utility Gain) adds a bi-level max inside the expectation with no structural claims either.

### Cited Findings
- EIG definition (Rainforth et al., Stat. Sci. 2024, Sec 2.1, Eqs 2–3): EIG(ξ) is the expectation over the marginal p(y|ξ) = E_{p(θ)} p(y|θ,ξ) of the KL from posterior to prior; equivalently E_{p(θ)p(y|θ,ξ)}[log p(y|θ,ξ) − log p(y|ξ)]; equivalently the mutual information between y and θ given ξ. — [arXiv 2302.14545 (HTML v2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.14545v2)
- "the EIG is a doubly-intractable quantity that is difficult to estimate, let alone optimize" (Sec 3.1); NMC estimator (Eq 7) is "in general, biased for any finite value of M" because "nonlinear mappings of unbiased estimators are themselves biased estimators"; asymptotic MSE O(a/N + b/M^2); NMC "cannot achieve a better mean squared error rate than O(C^(−2/3))". — [arXiv 2302.14545](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.14545v2)
- Bounds (Sec 3.3.1, 3.4.1): variational upper bound (Eq 12) EIG ≤ E[log p(y|θ,ξ) − log q(y|ξ)] for any normalized q, equality at q = p(y|ξ); Barber–Agakov lower bound (Eq 13) EIG ≥ E[log q(θ|y,ξ) − log p(θ)]; NMC-based upper bound (Eq 14) "can be arbitrarily tightened by increasing M"; contrastive lower bound (Eq 15, attributed to Foster et al. 2020, = PCE) "recovers the true EIG when q(θ|y,ξ) matches the true posterior." — [arXiv 2302.14545](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.14545v2)
- Foster et al. 2019/2020 unified stochastic-gradient approach: three lower bounds I_BA, I_ACE, I_PCE; "ACE generally performed about as well as the better of BA and PCE"; BA works when the inference network approximates the posterior, PCE when the prior is a reasonable proposal. — [arXiv 1911.00294](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.00294)
- Gradients: multilevel Monte Carlo gives "fully unbiased estimates of the EIG and its gradients"; stochastic gradient ascent on variational lower bounds w.r.t. variational and design parameters; gradient methods "introduce bias into the optimization due to the bias in their underlying estimators" when using NMC; for discrete designs "gradient methods have a problem when the design space or likelihood function is not continuous", relaxation schemes may help. — [arXiv 2302.14545](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.14545v2)
- Concavity: the review "makes no statement about whether the EIG is concave, convex, or nonconvex in ξ". — [arXiv 2302.14545](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.14545v2)
- Decision-theoretic generalization: BED is "any approach that optimizes an objective of the form E[U(ξ,θ,y)]" with U "a functional of the posterior"; expected Fisher information gain is used because it "can be easily estimated without evaluating either the posterior or marginal likelihood". — [arXiv 2302.14545](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.14545v2)
- Decision-aware BED (Huang, Guo, Acerbi, Kaski, NeurIPS 2024): Decision Utility Gain DUG(ξ_t, y_t) = max_{a∈A} E_{p(y_Ξ | h_{1:t−1} ∪ {(ξ_t,y_t)})}[u(y_Ξ,a)] − max_{a∈A} E_{p(y_Ξ | h_{1:t−1})}[u(y_Ξ,a)]; EDUG(ξ_t) = E_{p(y_t|ξ_t,h)}[DUG]. Difficulty attributed to "the inherent bi-level optimization problem and the need to evaluate two layers of expectations"; no concavity or bound claims; estimated by replacing the predictive with an amortized q. — [arXiv 2411.02064 (HTML v2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.02064v2)

### Inferences
- The variance-reducing facility objective is literally an EDUG: Σ_s p_s(y) Q_s(y) with p_s(y) induced by sampling plan y equals E_{obs ~ p(·|y)}[ min_x E[c'x + Q(x,ξ) | obs] ] when x is chosen after observing. The BED "trick" is the joint-sampling reparameterization: write E_{obs|y}[·] = E_{ξ~p(ξ)} E_{obs ~ p(·|ξ, y)}[·] so that y enters only through the likelihood of the observation. For Gaussian sampling noise this is a reparameterization: obs = ξ + σ_ε/√n(y) · ε, with ε ~ N(0,1) independent of y, so the sampling measure is y-independent and the objective is a plain expectation of a y-dependent function. This removes the decision-dependent measure but does NOT make the integrand concave/convex in y (the inner min_x is a concave function of the posterior mean, composed with a nonlinear map of y).
- All the BED bounds are bounds on EIG (an entropy functional), not on an LP-valued utility; the only transferable device is the contrastive/importance-weighting structure and MLMC debiasing of the nested expectation.
- The absence of any concavity claim in the review and in decision-aware BED is itself evidence: the BED community optimizes EIG/DUG by nonconvex stochastic gradient and amortization, not by convex reformulation.

### Gaps
- Chaloner & Verdinelli 1995 and Lindley 1956 not fetched; classical results on concavity of Bayesian D-optimality in the design measure (log det of information matrix is concave in the design weights) were not verified here and should be checked in Chaloner–Verdinelli if the facility variant has a linear-Gaussian demand model (then "amount of information" = posterior precision is linear in sample counts).

## Q3. Knowledge gradient / expected improvement with an LP-valued payoff (Frazier–Powell–Dayanik 2008; Ryzhov–Powell 2012; Frazier–Powell 2010)

### Takeaway
The knowledge gradient for an LP with uncertain objective is computable exactly as the expectation of a piecewise-linear parametric LP in a scalar normal variable and is asymptotically optimal, but the marginal value of information in sampling allocations is NOT concave in general (Frazier–Powell 2010, "eventually concave" only), and no work on KG for facility location or on ranking and selection with a downstream facility-location recourse was found.

### Cited Findings
- Frazier, Powell, Dayanik 2008: KG policy "myopically maximizes the expected increment in the value of information in each time period, where the value is measured according to the terminal utility function"; optimal for a single-period horizon and in the infinite-horizon limit. — [Frazier & Powell KG policy PDF](https://optimallearning.princeton.edu/Papers/Frazier%20Powell%20-%20Knowledge%20gradient%20policy.pdf)
- Ryzhov & Powell 2012 (SIAM J. Optim. 22:1344–1368): information collection for LPs with Bayesian normal prior on objective coefficients; the future problem "can be written as a parametric LP with a single stochastic parameter"; "The quantity (c^n + zΔc^n_j)' x(c^n + zΔc^n_j) is a piecewise linear function of z", KG = expected value of this piecewise-linear function under z ~ N(0,1), computed by finding breakpoints via a sequence of LPs; Theorem 4.1: KG policy asymptotically optimal; Prop 2.2/Cor 2.3 convexity-based perfect-information bound. — [Ryzhov & Powell 2012 PDF](https://optimallearning.princeton.edu/Papers/Ryzhov%20Powell%20-%20Information%20collection%20for%20Linear%20Programs_SIAM_October2012.pdf)
- Frazier & Powell, "Paradoxes in Learning and the Marginal Value of Information", Decision Analysis 7(4) 2010: in Bayesian ranking and selection "the marginal value of information is not concave, leading to algorithmic difficulties and apparent paradoxes"; with many identical alternatives "it is often better to ignore some completely and focus on a smaller number"; value of information is "eventually concave", i.e. concave "when the number of measurements of each alternative is large enough". — [IDEAS/Decision Analysis](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ordeca/v7y2010i4p378-403.html); [INFORMS DOI 10.1287/deca.1100.0190](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/deca.1100.0190)
- Radner–Stiglitz nonconcavity: "the marginal value of a small amount of information is zero" under their conditions, implying increasing returns over a range; Chade & Schlee 2002 generalize beyond finite states/signals; De Lara & Gilotte 2007 give a tight sufficient condition (infinitesimal information distance variation = 0). — [De Lara & Gilotte, HAL](https://hal-enpc.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00716396); [Chade & Schlee](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/another-look-at-the-radner-stiglitz-nonconcavity-in-the-value-of-)
- Whitmeyer 2024 "Call the Dentist! A (Con-)Cavity in the Value of Information": Proposition 2.3 "The efficient value of information, W, is weakly concave in η, strictly so if φ is strictly convex", where W(η) = max expected value over Bayes-plausible τ with D(τ) = φ[∫ c(x,μ) dτ(x)] ≤ η (c a convex divergence, φ convex strictly increasing, φ(0)=0); Lemma 2.2: the constraint always binds; Corollary 2.4: marginal value at 0 strictly positive with ≥2 undominated actions; Proposition 2.6: the "inefficient" (worst-case experiment of given amount) value "is almost never concave" (finite states/actions); Proposition 3.3: max-min value also weakly concave. — [arXiv 2404.01190](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01190v1)

### Inferences
- Ryzhov–Powell's computation transfers directly to facility location with uncertain demand only if the uncertainty is in the recourse objective coefficients; demand usually enters the right-hand side of the recourse LP, where the value is piecewise-linear convex (min-form) in the RHS rather than concave, so the single-parameter parametric-LP computation still works (parametric RHS LP) but with the opposite curvature. This is standard parametric LP theory, not a result stated by Ryzhov–Powell (they treat objective coefficients only).
- The Frazier–Powell nonconcavity means one cannot expect the variance-reducing facility objective to be concave in sampling counts y; at best "eventually concave" for large y. Whitmeyer's result shows concavity IS restored if one optimizes over the best Bayes-plausible experiment of a given cost (the posterior-based relaxation, Q4), i.e. nonconcavity is an artifact of the parametrization y ↦ experiment, not of the value functional itself.

### Gaps
- No paper found on "knowledge gradient for facility location" or "ranking and selection with a downstream optimization problem" beyond Ryzhov–Powell (LP objective coefficients) and the shortest-path special case they cite. No KG result with uncertain RHS was found.
- Frazier's 2018 BO tutorial (arXiv 1807.02811) was not fetched; it is not expected to contain structural results for LP-valued payoffs.

## Q4. Rational inattention / posterior-based reformulation (Sims; Caplin–Dean; Matějka–McKay; Caplin–Dean–Leahy)

### Takeaway
Writing the problem over state-contingent choice probabilities (equivalently over induced posteriors) makes the expected payoff LINEAR and the Shannon cost CONVEX (mutual information is convex in the channel for fixed prior), so the information-acquisition problem is a concave maximization; with any posterior-separable cost the problem is a concavification. This is the exact mechanism that would make the product term linear—but only if the "cost of inducing a posterior distribution" is expressible as an expectation over posteriors, which is not generally true of a sampling-count cost.

### Cited Findings
- Matějka & McKay (AER 2015) Lemma 1: under an optimal strategy "each action is selected in at most one posterior ... This behavior follows from the convexity of the entropy-based cost function"; footnote 12: "Convexity is a natural property of the cost function. For instance, it is implied by any cost function for which an information structure that is more informative in the sense of Blackwell (1953) is more costly." — [Matějka & McKay PDF](https://home.cerge-ei.cz/matejka/logit_FM_AM.pdf)
- Corollary 1 (state–action model): the conditional probabilities P = {P_i(v)} solve max_P Σ_i ∫ v_i P_i(v) G(dv) − c(P,G) s.t. P_i(v) ≥ 0, Σ_i P_i(v) = 1 for all v, where c(P,G) = λ[−Σ_i P_i^0 log P_i^0 + ∫ Σ_i P_i(v) log P_i(v) G(dv)] is λ times the mutual information between states and actions (Eq 9). Theorem 1: optimal conditional choice probabilities have the generalized logit form. — [Matějka & McKay PDF](https://home.cerge-ei.cz/matejka/logit_FM_AM.pdf)
- Mutual information is a convex function of p(y|x) for fixed p(x) (Cover & Thomas Theorem 2.7.4) and concave in p(x) for fixed p(y|x). — [arXiv 2106.14646 (citing C&T Thm 2.7.4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14646); [Georgia Tech ECE587 lecture 3](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~yxie77/ece587/Lecture3.pdf)
- Caplin, Dean & Leahy (JPE 2022): three classes—posterior separable, uniformly posterior separable, invariant posterior separable—"As with the Shannon cost function, all can be solved using Lagrangian methods"; posterior-separable costs "depend on the expectation of a strictly convex function of posterior beliefs so that standard Lagrangian optimization methods are available"; the PS class "allow standard tools of convex analysis to be used for purposes of solution"; the two axioms together "pinpoint the Shannon cost function". — [NBER w23652](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23652.pdf); [Columbia WP 21](https://sites.asit.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2018/06/Working_Paper_21.pdf)
- Denti: "When cost of information is posterior separable, the information acquisition problem can be formulated as a concavification problem." — [AER appendix / Denti](https://aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20211252.appx)
- Sims (2003) imposes "a constraint on the expected reduction in entropy"; Gentzkow–Kamenica take cost proportional to expected reduction in entropy. — [Gentzkow & Kamenica, Costly Persuasion](http://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2014/retrieve.php?pdfid=417)
- Dean & Neligh experiments reject Shannon but support posterior-separable costs. — [Columbia Experimental tests of RI](https://econ.columbia.edu/working-paper/experimental-tests-of-rational-inattention/)

### Inferences
- Exact posterior-based reformulation of the variance-reducing facility problem (my construction from the cited results): let Ω be the demand-scenario set, mu_0 the prior over Ω, W(mu) = min_{x∈X} [c'x + Σ_s mu_s Q_s(x)]. Choosing a sampling plan y induces τ(y) ∈ Δ(Δ(Ω)) with E_{τ(y)}[mu] = mu_0. The planner's cost is E_{τ(y)}[W(mu)] + κ(y). Over τ directly: min_τ { ∫ W(mu) dτ(mu) + C(τ) : ∫ mu dτ(mu) = mu_0 }. The objective's first term and the Bayes-plausibility constraint are LINEAR in τ (this is the Gentzkow–Kamenica Eq. (1) structure, see Q5). If C is posterior separable, C(τ) = λ ∫ [H(mu_0) − H(mu)] dτ(mu) with H concave, then C is also LINEAR in τ, and the whole problem is an (infinite-dimensional) LP in τ whose value is the lower convex envelope of W(mu) + λ[H(mu_0) − H(mu)] at mu_0 (min-form of concavification). With finitely many x, W is piecewise-linear concave, so W + λ(H(mu_0) − H) is a sum of a concave PL and a convex function; the envelope is computable by the standard Lagrangian/concavification machinery of Caplin–Dean–Leahy. This makes the "product of decision-dependent probability and recourse cost" linear—in τ, at the price of (i) replacing y by τ and (ii) assuming the cost of information is posterior separable.
- Equivalent finite-dimensional "channel" form (Matějka–McKay Corollary 1 min-analogue): because each x is optimally associated with at most one posterior (Lemma 1, for strictly convex cost), the problem can be written over conditional probabilities P(x | ω) for x ∈ X (finite), objective Σ_ω mu_0(ω) Σ_x P(x|ω)[c'x + Q_ω(x)] + λ I(ω; x), which is linear plus convex in P → convex program over a polytope of size |X|·|Ω|. For facility location |X| is exponential (2^|I|), so this is a column-generation-sized convex program, but the bilinearity is gone.
- The obstruction for a true sampling model: if y_j = number of samples at site j with per-sample cost, the induced τ(y) is a specific family (e.g. Gaussian posterior means), and the cost is κ(y), not a function of τ alone. Posterior separability holds only if κ(y) can be written as λ·E_{τ(y)}[H(mu_0) − H(mu)]; for Gaussian demand with Shannon H this would require per-site cost proportional to ½ log(1 + y_j σ_j^2/σ_ε^2), i.e. concave—not linear—in y_j. So the posterior-based LP is an exact reformulation only under a (possibly unrealistic) cost assumption, and otherwise a RELAXATION (every achievable τ(y) is feasible, so the τ-LP value lower-bounds the true optimum when C(τ) ≤ κ(y) for all y). Whitmeyer's Prop 2.3 shows this relaxation's value is concave in the information budget.

### Gaps
- No paper found that applies the posterior-separable reformulation to a stochastic program with LP recourse; the construction above is an inference from Matějka–McKay, Caplin–Dean–Leahy, and Gentzkow–Kamenica.
- Caplin & Dean 2013/2015 originals not fetched; the posterior-separable concept is sourced via CDL 2022 and secondary descriptions.

## Q5. Bayesian persuasion / information design (Kamenica–Gentzkow 2011; Gentzkow–Kamenica 2014; Bergemann–Morris 2019): choosing a distribution subject to a linear feasibility constraint plus a cost of inducing it

### Takeaway
The sender's problem is max over τ with E_τ[mu_s] = mu_0 of E_τ[v̂(mu_s)]—linear objective, linear (Bayes-plausibility) constraint, value = concavification at the prior; Gentzkow–Kamenica show the same holds with a cost of inducing τ provided the cost is an expected reduction of a concave uncertainty measure (posterior separable), which is the exact condition under which "cost of inducing distribution τ" is linear (hence trivially convex) in τ.

### Cited Findings
- Gentzkow–Kamenica restating KG 2011: "for any τ such that E_τ[μ_s] = μ_0, there exists a π s.t. τ = ⟨π|μ_0⟩. Hence, we can express Sender's problem as max_{τ s.t. E_τ[μ_s]=μ_0} [E_τ v̂(μ_s)] (1)"; "Let V denote the concavification of v̂ – the smallest concave function that is everywhere weakly greater than v̂ ... the value of an optimal signal is V(μ_0) and Sender benefits from persuasion if and only if V(μ_0) > v̂(μ_0)." Here v̂(μ_s) = E_{μ_s}[v(a*(μ_s), ω)] with a* the receiver's optimal action. — [Gentzkow & Kamenica, Costly Persuasion (Dec 2013), Sec 4](http://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2014/retrieve.php?pdfid=417)
- Costly case: Assumption 1: "There exists an interior belief μ̄ and a measure of uncertainty H such that for all signals π: c(π) = E_{⟨π|μ̄⟩}[H(μ̄) − H(μ_s)]" with H concave ("A function H ... is a measure of uncertainty if it is concave"); examples: entropy, residual variance H(μ) = Σ_ω μ(ω)(1−μ(ω)). Proposition 1: "Suppose the cost function satisfies Assumption A1. The value of an optimal signal is V_c(μ_0)" where V_c is the concavification of v̂_c(μ_s) = v̂(μ_s) − [H(μ̄) − H(m(μ_s|μ_0, μ̄))] and m(·) re-maps posteriors between priors. Without such a cost, "the concavification approach cannot be used" because "Sender's payoff at a posterior may depend on the signal that induced this belief." — [Costly Persuasion](http://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2014/retrieve.php?pdfid=417)
- Secondary restatement: KG 2011 show "a committed sender's problem can be written as choosing a Bayes-plausible distribution of posteriors and, in the binary-state case, solved by concavifying a reduced-form posterior payoff"; Gentzkow–Kamenica 2014 extend "to costly persuasion by subtracting a posterior-separable convex information cost from the payoff" so the optimum concavifies "an effective payoff u(p) − k_P κ(p)". — [arXiv 2602.10821](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10821)
- Coarse communication caveat: with at most k messages the k-concavification can fall short of the full concave hull. — [arXiv 1910.13547](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.13547)

### Inferences
- Direct analogue for the planner: replace the receiver's action by the facility decision x ∈ X and v̂ by −W(mu); Bayes plausibility is the linear constraint; "cost of inducing τ" is convex in τ iff it is a convex functional on Δ(Δ(Ω)); it is linear iff posterior separable. A cost that is a convex function of the Blackwell order (e.g. any cost that is Blackwell-monotone and convex in mixtures of experiments) keeps the problem convex in τ but loses the concavification characterization.
- Conditions for the cost of inducing p to be convex in p (collected): (i) posterior separable (expectation of a convex function of the posterior) → linear in τ (Gentzkow–Kamenica Assumption 1; CDL); (ii) mutual information with fixed prior → convex in the channel P(signal|ω) (Cover–Thomas 2.7.4), which is the channel-side version of (i); (iii) a sampling technology with cost κ(y) gives a cost on τ only through the inverse image, convex only if τ ↦ min{κ(y): τ(y) = τ} is convex, which is not generally true (Frazier–Powell nonconcavity is the symptom).
- For variant (a) (mean shift), the induced distribution is not Bayes plausible relative to a fixed prior (the mean genuinely moves), so the persuasion machinery does not apply: there is no linear "plausibility" constraint, only the decision-indexed distribution family of Pantuso/Hellemo.

### Gaps
- Kamenica–Gentzkow AER 2011 and Bergemann–Morris JEL 2019 were not fetched directly (abstract-only pages); all statements are from Gentzkow–Kamenica 2013/2014 and secondary sources quoting them. Bergemann–Morris's LP formulation of information design over "obedient" joint distributions (which is the finite-dimensional linear program version) is not cited here—report writer should treat that as unverified in these notes.

## Q6. Decision-dependent information in multistage SP (Goel–Grossmann 2006; Apap–Grossmann; Vayanos–Kuhn–Rustem 2011; Ma et al. 2024; Hellemo et al. 2018)

### Takeaway
The decision-dependent-timing literature handles the decision-dependence through conditional non-anticipativity constraints (disjunctive/big-M MIP, Lagrangean relaxation, decision rules) or through a nested conditional-expectation formulation with information-relaxation bounds; these give LINEAR (MIP) reformulations of measurability, not convexity, and the decision-dependent-probability case (Hellemo et al.) is handled with global (BARON) solvers or finite indexing with L-shaped cuts (Pantuso), i.e. no convex reformulation of Σ p_s(x)Q_s(x) is claimed anywhere.

### Cited Findings
- Goel & Grossmann (Math. Prog. 108:355–394, 2006): "disjunctive programming formulation" with "binary variables to model the time at which each endogenous uncertain parameter is observed"; "a Lagrangean duality based branch and bound algorithm"; the conditional NACs "form a very large class, with cardinality in the order of the square of the number of scenarios"; Boland et al. 2016 show "only very few of these constraints may be required". — [Springer Math Prog](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10107-015-0970-6); [Ma et al. summary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10669)
- Follow-ups: Tarhan et al. 2009 Lagrangian relaxation; Colvin & Maravelias 2009/2010 branch-and-cut; Gupta & Grossmann 2014 Lagrangean decomposition that keeps some NACs explicit and dualizes the rest, with dual bound at least as tight; Apap & Grossmann: NAC reduction properties plus a "sequential scenario decomposition heuristic" that gives upper and lower bounds but "is not exact", and Lagrangean decomposition with a possibly persistent duality gap. — [AIChE 2016 abstract](https://proceedings.aiche.org/conferences/aiche-annual-meeting/2016/proceeding/paper/635g-efficient-branch-and-bound-strategy-multistage-stochastic-programs-endogenous-and-exogenous); [arXiv 1908.01792 (graph-theoretic NAC generation)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.01792)
- Vayanos, Kuhn, Rustem (CDC-ECC 2011): problems "can be formulated as mixed-binary multi-stage stochastic programs with decision-dependent non-anticipativity constraints"; "severely computationally intractable"; "conservative approximation in the form of a mixed-binary linear program by restricting the spaces of measurable binary and real-valued decision rules to those that are representable as piecewise constant and linear functions of the uncertain parameters". NAC enforced by big-M: "|Y_ij| ≤ M z_j ... if ξ_j was not observed in the first decision stage, then the affine decision rule y(ξ) = Yξ must be independent of ξ_j"; Proposition 3.1 gives multistage NACs |z^s_{t,j'} − z^{s'}_{t,j'}| ≤ z^s_{t−1,j} and |Y^s_{t,ij'} − Y^{s'}_{t,ij'}| ≤ M z^s_{t−1,j} for s, s' differing only in coordinate j. Jonsbråten et al. 1998 "studied decision problems in which the control actions can impact both the distribution of the uncertainties as well as the timing"; problems with decision-dependent discovery "are perceived as particularly hard even if the distribution of the uncertainties is unaffected by the decisions". — [Vayanos–Kuhn–Rustem CDC 2011 PDF](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/cdc-ecc-2011/data/papers/2090.pdf)
- Vayanos, Georghiou, Yu (Management Science) "Robust Optimization with Decision-Dependent Information Discovery" extends this to robust optimization. — [arXiv 2004.08490](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.08490)
- Ma et al. 2024 (Probing-Enhanced SP): nested formulation (PESP): max_S F(S) − α(S) with F(S) = Σ_{η_S} P(η_S) R(η_S), R(η_S) = max_y E[β'y + Q(y,ξ) | η_S]; α monotone, optionally modular; "we do not necessarily assume that taking a probing action gives us complete information"; nested formulation "allows us to forgo the modeling of conditional non-anticipativity constraints" and handles continuous η; branch-and-bound on probe inclusion/exclusion with information-based relaxation bounds (Lemma 2: for fixed-in S1 and fixed-out S0, F(S1) − α(S1) ≤ ... ≤ F([n]\S0) − α(S1)); Lemma 3 sharper under modular α; internal/external sampling give statistical bounds; greedy heuristic. On finite support "scales significantly better than the MIP formulation" and gives bounds that "improve upon the perfect information bounds". — [arXiv 2407.10669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10669)
- Hellemo, Barton, Tomasgard (Comput. Manag. Sci. 15:369–395, 2018): taxonomy of two-stage recourse models where first-stage decisions change the distribution "directly or indirectly": (i) prior probabilities "distorted through an affine transformation", (ii) "several probability distributions are combined using convex weights", (iii) "parameters of the distribution are first-stage decision variables"; distributions "included either exactly or via a rational approximation"; instances "solved with BARON using selective branching". — [MIT DSpace record](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/117361); [IDEAS/CMS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/comgts/v15y2018i3d10.1007_s10287-018-0330-0.html)
- Pantuso 2025 (variant (a) exact MIP structure): objective min F'x + Σ_d 1_d(x) E_{P_d}[Q(x,ξ)]; extensive form min Σ_i F_i x_i + Σ_d δ_d E_{P_d}[Q]; "the scenario probabilities π_sd belong to each distribution d and do not vary with x within that distribution. The decision dependence enters only through the choice of d"; δ_d linked to zone indicators by linear constraints; bilinear δ_d · w^{sd}_{ij} replaced by h^{sd}_{ij} with bounding constraints; decision-dependent L-shaped method (extending Van Slyke–Wets via Pantuso & Hewitt 2025) with cut μ ≥ Σ_s π_sd ρ_sd'(h_sd − T_sd x) − U(|Z_d| − Σ_{z∈Z_d} y_z + Σ_{z∉Z_d} y_z); Prop 3 finite convergence; Prop 4 Jensen valid inequality μ ≤ Σ_i Σ_j R_ij min{C_i, max_d E_{P_d}[ξ_j]} x_i "using concavity of Q in ξ"; "The paper does not claim convexity of the full problem, submodularity, or other global structural properties". — [arXiv 2509.09816](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09816v1)

### Inferences
- Transfer to decision-dependent probabilities: the NAC/decision-rule/Lagrangean machinery addresses "which σ-field may y depend on", i.e. measurability; it leaves the objective as a scenario sum with FIXED probabilities. Hence it transfers to variant (b) only in the nested/probing form (Ma et al.), where probabilities never appear as functions of y—the decision-dependence is absorbed into the conditioning and the resulting F(S) is monotone but otherwise unstructured, hence solved by B&B over probe sets. It does not transfer to variant (a) at all; there the only linearization is finite indexing of distributions + big-M/L-shaped cuts (Pantuso) or MILP via piecewise-linear moments (Basciftci et al.).
- The "information-based relaxation" bounds of Ma et al. (Lemma 1–3) are the SP analogue of Blackwell monotonicity: more probing never hurts, so relaxing to "probe everything in the undecided set" gives a valid bound, and the perfect-information value is the global bound (same content as Ryzhov–Powell Cor. 2.3).
- If one insists on writing p_s(y) explicitly for sampling (variant (b)), Hellemo's class (iii) (distribution parameters as first-stage decisions) is the matching taxonomy entry and the located literature treats it as a nonconvex global-optimization problem.

## Q7. Submodularity route for the variance-reducing variant (Krause, Singh, Guestrin 2008; Golovin–Krause)

### Takeaway
Mutual information / entropy reduction is submodular under conditional independence (Krause–Guestrin 2005 Prop 2) and gives greedy (1−1/e) guarantees, but the expected reduction in recourse cost (decision-theoretic VOI) does NOT inherit submodularity (Krause–Guestrin 2009 Prop 9; Malings–Pozzi 2019), so greedy guarantees for the variance-reducing facility variant are available only for an entropy surrogate, not for the true objective.

### Cited Findings
- Krause–Guestrin 2005 Prop 2 (submodularity of information gain under conditional independence of S given U) and the (1−1/e) greedy guarantee; Theorem 9 hardness of better approximation. — [arXiv 1207.1394](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.1394)
- Krause–Guestrin 2009 Prop 9: decision-theoretic VOI not submodular even in Naive Bayes; computing it is #P-complete there. — [arXiv 1401.3474](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3474)
- Malings & Pozzi 2019: VoI for sensor placement "lacks the property of submodularity"; greedy "can lead to sub-optimal solutions"; propose conditional-entropy surrogate. — [IDEAS/RESS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reensy/v183y2019icp93-103.html)
- Golovin–Krause adaptive submodularity: Theorem 5 greedy guarantee; coverage under stochastic failures, version-space reduction, and adaptive influence maximization are adaptive submodular; framework "not directly applicable" with synergies or when "items selection can alter the underlying realization"; "pointwise submodularity is not sufficient to establish adaptive submodularity". — [ar5iv 1003.3967](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1003.3967)
- Ma et al. 2024 prove only monotonicity (Lemma 1) of the probe-set value F(S) and use branch-and-bound plus a greedy heuristic without an approximation guarantee. — [arXiv 2407.10669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10669)
- Singh, Guillory, Bilmes (AISTATS 2012) bisubmodular formulations for richer VOI problems in sensor placement/feature selection with constant-factor approximation. — [PMLR v22 singh12](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v22/singh12.html)

### Inferences
- For the facility problem with customer demands conditionally independent given site parameters and sampling noise independent across sites, the Krause–Guestrin Prop 2 condition holds (S = observation variables independent given U = latent demands), so the entropy/MI surrogate in the set of sampled sites is submodular and greedy-optimizable with (1−1/e). The gap between the surrogate and the true expected recourse-cost reduction is unquantified in the literature.
- The Krause–Guestrin intuition for non-submodularity ("need several observations to convince ourselves to change our action") maps onto facility location: the optimal set of open facilities changes only when posterior demand crosses thresholds, so the value of the k-th sample at a site can exceed the value of the first (complementarity), which is also the Frazier–Powell "paradox".

### Gaps
- No paper located that proves submodularity, supermodularity, or an approximation ratio for expected recourse-cost reduction in two-stage stochastic programs as a function of the sampled set; no "EVSI submodular" result found.

## Q8. Synthesis: what the catalogue gives for Σ_s p_s(y) Q_s(y)

### Takeaway
Across all seven fields the only exact linearization of the decision-dependent-probability × recourse-cost product is the lift to a distribution over posteriors (or to a channel P(x|ω)), under which the objective and Bayes plausibility are linear and a posterior-separable information cost is linear too (variant (b) only); every other field either (i) shows the natural y-parametrized objective is nonconcave (Radner–Stiglitz, Frazier–Powell), non-submodular (Krause–Guestrin), and doubly intractable (Rainforth), or (ii) linearizes via finite distribution indexing and big-M/L-shaped cuts (variant (a): Pantuso, Basciftci et al.; Hellemo via global solver).

### Cited Findings
- (All sources above; no new citations.)

### Inferences
- Property table (decision variable → property, with the condition):
  1. Belief mu → W(mu) concave (min-form); E_tau[W] ≤ W(mu_0). Condition: W is a min of functions linear in mu (finite x, or LP in x with mu entering only the objective). [Ryzhov–Powell Prop 2.2; Blackwell]
  2. Distribution over posteriors tau → objective linear; Bayes plausibility linear; posterior-separable cost linear; value = convex envelope at mu_0. Condition: cost is E_tau of a convex function of the posterior (Gentzkow–Kamenica A1 / CDL). [Q4, Q5]
  3. Channel P(x|ω) → expected cost linear, Shannon cost convex → convex program. Condition: fixed prior, Shannon/mutual-information cost. [Matějka–McKay Cor. 1; Cover–Thomas 2.7.4]
  4. Information budget η (best experiment of cost ≤ η) → value concave in η. Condition: cost = φ(expected convex divergence), φ convex increasing. [Whitmeyer Prop 2.3]
  5. Sample counts / sampling allocation y → NOT concave in general; eventually concave; monotone in set inclusion. [Frazier–Powell 2010; Radner–Stiglitz; finite-time KG lemma]
  6. Set of experiments/probes S → true VOI not submodular (even Naive Bayes); entropy/MI submodular iff observations conditionally independent given the target; probe value monotone. [KG05 Prop 2; KG09 Prop 9; Ma et al. Lemma 1]
  7. Continuous design ξ in BED → no concavity claimed; optimize via biased NMC / BA, PCE, ACE bounds / MLMC gradients; joint-sampling reparameterization removes the design from the sampling measure. [Rainforth et al. 2024; Foster et al. 2019]
  8. Facility vector x with x-indexed distribution family (variant (a)) → exact MIP via indicator δ_d(x) and big-M/L-shaped cuts; no convexity. [Pantuso 2025; Basciftci et al. 2021; Hellemo et al. 2018 use BARON]
- Variant (a) vs (b): the posterior-based linearization (rows 2–4) requires Bayes plausibility, i.e. the mean of the induced distribution equals the prior; variant (a) violates this by construction (the action moves the mean), so none of the information-acquisition reformulations apply to (a); only row 8 applies. Variant (b) is Bayes plausible and all of rows 1–7 apply; the exact reformulation is available if the sampling cost can be written as posterior separable, otherwise the τ-LP is a relaxation (lower bound on cost) with concave value in the budget.
- Practical reformulation suggestion for (b) that uses only verified results: enumerate (or column-generate) candidate facility plans x ∈ X'; the planner's relaxed problem is min_{τ} Σ_k τ_k W(mu_k) + λ Σ_k τ_k [H(mu_0) − H(mu_k)] s.t. Σ_k τ_k mu_k = mu_0, τ ≥ 0, over a finite grid of posteriors mu_k; this is an LP whose optimal support has at most |Ω| points (Carathéodory, implicit in the concavification literature); then map the optimal τ back to a sampling plan y if one exists (feasibility of the sampling technology is the unverified step).

### Gaps
- No source provides an exact map from a Gaussian/precision-based sampling plan y to a posterior-separable cost, nor bounds on the gap between the τ-relaxation and the y-constrained problem.
- The brief's framing "expected value of information is convex in the prior / concave in the information structure": convexity in the prior is verified (row 1); "concave in the information structure" is verified only in the Whitmeyer efficient-quantity sense (row 4) and refuted in the sampling-count sense (row 5).
