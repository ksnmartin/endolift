# Change-of-measure and gradient-estimation ideas for sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y) with binary y

Setting used throughout: y in {0,1}^n (facilities to open), scenario set s = 1..S fixed, p_s(y) >= 0 with sum_s p_s(y) = 1, and Q_s(y) = min{ q_s' z : W z = h_s - T y, z >= 0 } (a convex piecewise-linear LP value function of y). Objective F(y) = c'y + sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y). Items marked "(derived)" in Inferences are identities I worked out from the cited definitions; they are not quoted from a source.

## Q1. Likelihood ratio / importance sampling and exponential-family p_s(y)

### Takeaway
Rewriting E_{p(y)}[Q] = E_q[(p(y)/q) Q] is exact and removes the decision from the measure, but it moves the decision into the integrand as a multiplicative factor; the literature (Dupačová; Hellemo et al.) states explicitly that this costs the convexity of the original integrand, and no source I found claims an exponential-family parameterization restores convexity. For binary y with theta(y) affine, exp(theta(y)·T(s)) is exactly multilinear in y, which turns the tilted objective into a 0-1 hyperbolic (ratio-of-multilinear) program, the class Ahmed (2000) treats with convex extensions.

### Cited Findings
- Dupačová: "Assume that there exist densities p(x, ω) of probability distributions P_x with respect to a common probability measure Q. Then the objective function in (2) can be rewritten as F(x) = ∫ f(x, ω) p(x, ω) Q(dω) ... However, it is obtained at the cost of losing convenient properties of the original random objective function f(x, ω)." — [Dupačová, Optimization under Exogenous and Endogenous Uncertainty (MME 2006)](https://www2.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~kopa/papers/MME06sty.pdf)
- Dupačová: "if f(x, ω) is a convex function of x for each ω, then so is F(x; P), whereas the convexity property of F(x) := F(x; P_x) may be lost." Suggested remedies: "recursive optimization methods and search techniques ... numerical enumeration techniques including branch-and-bound method and disjunctive programming." — [same source](https://www2.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~kopa/papers/MME06sty.pdf)
- Dupačová's lecture slides give the parametric density version: solve ∫ f(x, ξ) δ(θ(x), ξ) dG(ξ), with the exponential example min_x ∫_0^∞ f(x, ξ) λ(x) e^{−λ(x) ξ} dξ, and note "the function f̃ need not preserve convenient properties of f; search for special cases." They separate "Decision dependent probabilities: fixed set of scenarios, decisions only influence probabilities: min sum_s p_s(x) f(x, ξ_s)" from "Decision dependent scenarios: fixed probability structure, decisions shape scenarios: min sum_s p_s f(x, ξ_s(x))", calling the latter "arguably the most important case in practice." — [Dupačová slides, Charles University](https://karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~racko/NMEK615/download/year2425/Stochastic_programming_problems_with_endogeneous_randomness.pdf)
- Hellemo, Barton and Tomasgard formulate exactly the scenario-weight model: min c_x'x + c_y'y + sum_s p_s(y) q_s' z_s, with (i) linear scaling p_s(y) = p_0s·y on a subset of scenarios and renormalization of the rest, (ii) convex combination p_s = sum_i p_{i,s} y_i with sum_i y_i = 1, (iii) Kumaraswamy and normal-cdf parametric forms with the parameters as first-stage variables (rational Abramowitz-Stegun approximation with branch-selecting binaries). They state the models are "nonlinear and non-convex", that "Global optimization techniques must be applied to guarantee an optimal solution", solve with BARON, and report that selective branching on the y that determine p(y) improved times "dramatically" because once y is fixed the problem is an ordinary stochastic program. — [Hellemo, Barton, Tomasgard, Comput. Manag. Sci. 15 (2018) 369–395](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10287-018-0330-0)
- Shapiro: "Within simulation models, a variant of SAA based on likelihood ratio transformations was suggested in Rubinstein and Shapiro (1993)." — [Shapiro, WSC 2001](https://informs-sim.org/wsc01papers/056.PDF). The 1990 precursor constructs "a stochastic counterpart to the deterministic program using a probability measure transformation, so one simulation run can estimate the expected-performance objective." — [Rubinstein & Shapiro, Math. Comput. Simul. 32(4), 1990](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/matcom/v32y1990i4p373-392.html)
- Rubinstein & Kroese's textbook (3rd ed.) covers "the transform likelihood ratio method, the score function method for sensitivity analysis, the stochastic approximation method and the stochastic counterpart method for Monte Carlo optimization." — [Wiley page](https://www.wiley-vch.de/de/fachgebiete/mathematik-und-statistik/simulation-and-the-monte-carlo-method-978-1-118-63216-1)
- Ahmed's 2000 thesis: stochastic programs "with discrete first-stage decisions and decision-dependent uncertainty" are "cast as 0-1 hyperbolic programs, and the thesis uses convex extension theory to build a reformulation scheme and an exact solution method"; Noyan et al. (2018) cite Ahmed (2000) for the result that such programs "can be reformulated as 0-1 hyperbolic programs." — [Ahmed, UIUC thesis 2000](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/88389); [Noyan, Rudolf, Lejeune, arXiv 1806.09215](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.09215)
- A cross-entropy/KL dual identity is stated in a 2026 preprint: Λ_1(w; θ) = −θ log E_q[exp(−w/θ)], with the optimal tilt given by the Gibbs weighting, and KL is the only power divergence yielding a log-sum-exp value function. — [arXiv 2609.26552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26552)

### Inferences
- (derived) Exact identity: sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y) = sum_s q_s L_s(y) Q_s(y), L_s(y) = p_s(y)/q_s, for any q with q_s > 0 wherever p_s(y) > 0. With exponential-family p_s(y) = q_s exp(θ(y)·T(s) − A(θ(y))), A(θ) = log sum_s q_s exp(θ·T(s)), the objective is sum_s q_s exp(θ(y)·T(s)) Q_s(y) / exp(A(θ(y))). A is convex in θ; exp(θ·T(s)) is log-convex; Q_s(y) is convex but not log-convex (it is piecewise-linear and can be zero), so the product is not convex or log-convex in general. Taking logs where Q_s > 0: log F = log sum_s exp(θ(y)·T(s) + log q_s + log Q_s(y)) − A(θ(y)) is a difference of two convex functions only if θ is affine in y AND log Q_s is convex in y; the latter fails for LP value functions in general. So the exponential-family route gives at best a DC structure under an extra log-convexity assumption that the facility-location recourse does not satisfy. No source I found claims otherwise.
- (derived) For binary y and θ(y) = sum_i a_i y_i: exp(sum_i a_i·T(s) y_i) = prod_i (1 + (e^{a_i·T(s)} − 1) y_i) exactly on {0,1}^n. Hence p_s(y)/q_s = M_s(y)/sum_{s'} q_{s'} M_{s'}(y) with M_s multilinear, i.e. a ratio of multilinear polynomials: the 0-1 hyperbolic program of Ahmed (2000). Normalization-free (unnormalized tilt) versions are linearizable by the multilinear tools in Q8, but the denominator sum_{s'} q_{s'} M_{s'}(y) must be handled by fractional-programming (Charnes–Cooper/Li-type) or by enumerating the finitely many values of the denominator, which is what Hellemo's "selective branching on y" and Pantuso–Hewitt's δ_d indicator variables (Q4/Q8) effectively do.
- (derived) For a finite scenario set, importance sampling itself buys nothing computationally: the "sample" is the whole scenario set, the likelihood ratios are bounded (max_s p_s(y)/q_s < ∞), and the weighted sum is already exact. The only gain is notational: fixing q makes the measure decision-free and isolates all y-dependence in L_s(y)·Q_s(y), which is the bilinear/product structure one is trying to remove, not a cure for it.
- The cross-entropy method optimizes the sampling parameter v of q_v to minimize KL to the zero-variance density; that is an inner convex problem in v for exponential families (A(v) convex), but it does not change the outer decision problem in y. I found no source applying CE to the decision-dependent two-stage objective (see Gaps).

- I could not fetch Asmussen & Glynn (Stochastic Simulation, Ch. VII) or Rubinstein & Shapiro (1993) text; the statements above about the stochastic counterpart are from Shapiro (2001) and the 1990 abstract, not the books.

## Q2. Score function (REINFORCE), IPA, and the stochastic counterpart

### Takeaway
The gradient identity ∇_y E_{p(y)}[Q(y)] = E_{p(y)}[∇_y Q] + E_{p(y)}[Q ∇_y log p(y)] is the standard split used in the performative-prediction papers; the stochastic counterpart replaces the expectation by a fixed-sample LR-weighted average and yields one deterministic problem in y, but the resulting sample objective is a weighted sum of Q_s(y) with y-dependent weights, i.e. exactly the original bilinear structure with p_s replaced by its sample analogue. For binary y the gradient identity has no direct use; on a relaxation, the score term is what destroys convexity.

### Cited Findings
- Miller, Perdomo & Zrnic write the gradient of the performative risk as ∇_θ PR(θ) = E_{z∼D(θ)} ∇_θ ℓ(z; θ) + E_{z∼D(θ)} ℓ(z; θ) ∇_θ log p_θ(z) and note "gradients are unavailable since they depend on the density of D(θ), denoted p_θ, which is typically unknown." — [arXiv 2102.08570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)
- Their convexity proof shows the score term is controlled by mixture dominance: E_{z∼D(θ)}[ℓ(z; θ) ∇_θ log p_θ(z)]'(θ' − θ) ≤ E_{z∼D(θ')} ℓ(z; θ) − E_{z∼D(θ)} ℓ(z; θ) (Appendix C). — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)
- A 2024 paper "separates the gradient of performative risk into the classical risk minimization term and a second term, the performative gradient", proposes a reparameterization estimator and compares it with the score-function estimator of Izzo et al. — [arXiv 2411.02023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02023). PerfGD (Izzo, Ying, Zou 2021) "explicitly captures how changes in the model affects the data distribution" and is claimed as "the first algorithm which provably converges to the performatively optimal point"; its theory is for a one-dimensional Gaussian case. — [arXiv 2102.07698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07698)
- Lecture notes (UvA) on derivative estimation: "For FD and IPA, we only assume the function h to depend on θ (structural dependence). For LR, we only assume the distribution of X to depend on θ." — [Dorsman, Stochastic Simulation slides](https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/j.l.dorsman/Downloads/StochSim1819/Slides_Stoch_Sim_JLD_derivative_estimation.pdf)
- Glasserman's IPA framework gives sufficient conditions for unbiasedness by establishing that finite-horizon performance measures are a.s. continuous in the parameter. — [Glasserman, Structural conditions for perturbation analysis](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/4253/glasserman_structural_conditions.pdf)
- SAA optimal value is a downward-biased estimator of the true optimum (minimization); Dentcheva & Lin propose smoothed estimators with lower MSE. — [arXiv 2103.07553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07553). Banholzer, Fliege & Werner quantify the asymptotic bias of SAA optimal-value estimators. — [Southampton eprint](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/430479/1/ratesofconvergence_saa_v48.pdf)
- Rubinstein & Glynn (2009): "do not use IS in high dimensional problems because of the degeneracy properties of likelihood ratios"; their screening method shrinks the LR to a low-dimensional "bottleneck parameter vector." — [Stanford page](https://web.stanford.edu/~glynn/papers/2009/RubinsteinG09.html)
- Shapiro notes that "for smooth unconstrained problems" the stochastic counterpart and stochastic approximation give asymptotically equivalent estimators. — [IAOR abstract](https://iaorifors.com/paper/29156)

### Inferences
- (derived) Stochastic counterpart for this problem: draw ξ^1..ξ^N ∼ q once, then solve min_y c'y + (1/N) sum_j [p(ξ^j; y)/q(ξ^j)] Q(y, ξ^j). This is one deterministic problem in y for the fixed sample. Pointwise it is unbiased for F(y); its minimum value is downward-biased as for SAA. Its variance at y is (1/N)(E_q[L(y)^2 Q^2] − F(y)^2), which blows up when L(y) = p(y)/q is far from 1 on scenarios where Q is large; this is the q-far-from-p failure. For a finite scenario set with q_s > 0 for all s, N = S with the whole support "sampled" reproduces the exact objective, so no bias and no variance, but also no structural simplification.
- (derived) In the facility-location model the dependence on y is both structural (Q_s(y) via T y) and distributional (p_s(y)), so neither pure IPA nor pure LR applies; the mixed estimator is the two-term identity. On a continuous relaxation y ∈ [0,1]^n, the first term is a subgradient of a convex function; the second (score) term has no sign and is the source of nonconvexity (Perdomo et al. Prop. 4.2 shows PR can be concave with ε-sensitive maps, see Q6).
- The stochastic counterpart does not help with binary y at all: gradients are meaningless on {0,1}^n and the LR-weighted sample objective is still sum_j w_j(y) Q(y, ξ^j) with y-dependent weights.

### Gaps
- No source gives an explicit bias/variance formula for the LR-SAA (stochastic counterpart) as a function of the distance between q and p(y); the formula above is standard but derived here.
- Could not access the Rubinstein–Shapiro 1993 book text.

## Q3. Exponential tilting / Esscher transform and log-sum-exp

### Takeaway
If p_s(y) = q_s exp(a(y)·ξ_s − log Z(y)), then sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y) = [sum_s q_s e^{a(y)·ξ_s} Q_s(y)] / [sum_s q_s e^{a(y)·ξ_s}], a ratio of two exponential sums; log Z(y) is convex (log-sum-exp composed with affine a) and exponential-cone representable, but the numerator is not log-convex unless each Q_s is log-convex, so the geometric-programming route needs Q_s to be a posynomial-like (log-convex, positive) function of y, which an LP value function is not. No published source was found making this reformulation for decision-dependent two-stage programs.

### Cited Findings
- Log-sum-exp terms "can be written exactly with exponential cone constraints, which are supported by conic solvers such as MOSEK." — [arXiv 2510.16886](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.16886)
- Worst-case expectation over a KL neighborhood has maximizer "given by exponential tilting, which is the continuous analogue of softmax weights." — [arXiv 2605.02850](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02850). Tilted ERM reweights losses by exponential tilting and connects to CVaR and DRO. — [NSF PAR 10493706](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10493706)
- Miller et al. show that for the location-scale family (2) and squared/absolute loss the performative risk is convex "for any g and µ" (Example 3.2), and that for D(θ) = N(µ, ε²θ²) the risk is (ε − 1)²-convex (Example 3.5). — [arXiv 2102.08570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)

### Inferences
- (derived) Geometric-programming form: if Q_s(y) > 0 and log Q_s(y) is convex in y (not the case for an LP value function in general), then log numerator = log sum_s exp(a(y)·ξ_s + log q_s + log Q_s(y)) is convex when a is affine, and log F = log numerator − log Z(y) is DC. A convex program results only if log Z(y) is constant on the feasible set (e.g. tilts constrained to preserve normalization), or if one minimizes an upper bound obtained by replacing −log Z(y) with its affine minorant at a reference point (a convex surrogate / majorization-minimization step). Each MM step is a convex problem in a continuous relaxation; none of this is exact for binary y.
- (derived) The Donsker–Varadhan form log Z(a) = sup_p { sum_s p_s a·ξ_s − KL(p‖q) } (the KL-dual cited above) exhibits the tilted p(y) as the argmax of a concave problem in p; the objective sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y) is then the value of a bilevel problem (inner max over p, outer min over y with p evaluated inside Q's weights). This is an MPEC-type structure, not a convex one.
- For binary y and affine a(y), exp(a(y)·ξ_s) is exactly multilinear (see Q1), so the tilted model is a 0-1 hyperbolic program; this is the only exact route for binary y and it lands back in Q8's linearization machinery plus a fractional denominator.

### Gaps
- No source found that treats Esscher/exponential tilting as a convexification device for decision-dependent stochastic programs; the searches returned only KL/DRO uses of tilting.

## Q4. Radon–Nikodym derivatives, measure-valued and relaxed-control reformulations

### Takeaway
Optimizing over the measure directly makes the objective linear in the measure, but here the measure is pinned to a curve p(y) inside the simplex and y also enters Q_s; the only exact "linear-in-measure" version for binary y is enumeration over decision patterns (or distribution patterns), which is what the Pantuso–Hewitt and 2025 facility-location papers do with indicator variables δ_d, and it is a Dantzig–Wolfe-type reformulation with up to 2^n columns.

### Cited Findings
- Relaxed controls / Young measures: "Young measures are also called generalized controls or relaxed controls", classical controls being Dirac measures. — [arXiv 1404.4699](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.4699). Lifting to occupation measures turns a nonconvex trajectory problem into a linear program over measures, solved via moment-SOS hierarchies when data are polynomial. — [arXiv 1309.3112](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.3112); [arXiv 1412.2278](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1412.2278)
- Pantuso & Hewitt: problem min c'x + sum_d 1_d(x) E_{P_d}[Q(x, ξ)], where "the non-trivial relationship between x and the resulting distribution is piecewise-constant" over a finite partition {X_d} of X; the problem "can be viewed as a type of disjunctive program"; distribution-specific optimality cut µ ≥ sum_s π_{s d} (ρ_s)'(h_{s d} − T_{s d} x) − U(1 − 1_d(x)); bilinear terms q'y δ replaced by McCormick envelopes τ ≤ y, τ ≤ Y δ, τ ≥ y + Y δ − Y for an LP bound. Enumeration "is viable only when the number of distributions is small." — [arXiv 2506.12753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12753)
- Facility location with up to 2^|Z| distributions (one per active-zone pattern; |Z| = |I| gives 2^|I|): extensive form min sum_i F_i x_i + sum_d δ_d E_{P_d}[Q], δ_d·w linearized with auxiliary h giving a MILP of size O(2^|Z|); Benders-type decomposition with master of size O(|Z|), distribution-specific cuts with big-M U, finite convergence, Jensen-based valid inequality; extensive-form CPLEX gaps > 10^3 % on most instances, decomposition solved all 1,008 instances per demand type, |Z| = 17 reached <0.5% gap on 74/84, |Z| = 20 none to optimality. Demand mean in the case study is linear in the zone indicators. — [arXiv 2509.09816](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09816v1)
- Dupačová: with a finite number of distributions "indexed by a finite number of indices d ... one may apply a full enumeration with respect to d, or a version of the branch-and-bound method, or disjunctive programming techniques." — [MME 2006](https://www2.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~kopa/papers/MME06sty.pdf)

### Inferences
- (derived) Measure-valued lift: let ν be a probability measure on {0,1}^n (a randomized first-stage decision) and µ(s, y') = p_s(y') ν(y'). Then the objective is sum_{y'} ν(y') [c'y' + sum_s p_s(y') Q_s(y')], linear in ν. Because it is linear and ν ranges over a simplex, an extreme point (a Dirac ν) is optimal, so the randomized relaxation is exact; the price is 2^n columns, i.e. the Dantzig–Wolfe / column-generation form of complete enumeration. Pantuso–Hewitt's δ_d with |D| ≪ 2^n is the same idea when p depends on y only through a coarse partition (zones).
- (derived) Occupation-measure/moment-SOS relaxations require polynomial data; p_s(y) multilinear is polynomial, but Q_s(y) (LP value function) is piecewise-linear, so one would need to embed the second-stage LP via its dual (max over dual extreme points), producing a polynomial-in-(y, dual) bilinear program; the first-level moment relaxation of y_i ρ products is exactly the RLT/McCormick linearization of Q8. I found no paper doing this for decision-dependent probabilities.

### Gaps
- No source connects Young/occupation measures or relaxed controls to decision-dependent scenario probabilities; the lift above is my construction.

## Q5. Variance reduction, common random numbers, coupling p_s(y) and Q_s(y)

### Takeaway
CRN/coupling is the push-in idea: represent decision-dependent demand as ξ_s(y) = ξ_s^0 + A y on a fixed probability space instead of as reweighted fixed scenarios. For a location-family (mean-shift) model this removes the probability weights entirely, and because the LP value function is convex in its right-hand side, the objective becomes an ordinary two-stage stochastic program with technology matrix T − A, convex in a continuous relaxation and MILP-exact for binary y. The two representations are not equivalent for a fixed finite support, so this is a modelling choice, not an identity.

### Cited Findings
- Dupačová: if P_x(B) = Q(B ⊕ H x), "changing variables ... transforms the objective function to ∫ f(x, ζ − H x) Q(dζ), whose properties depend on properties of f(x, u) viewed as a function of (x, u) jointly"; this is the "Push-in technique" that removes the decision from the measure "under specific assumptions." — [MME 2006](https://www2.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~kopa/papers/MME06sty.pdf)
- Dupačová's slides contrast "decision dependent probabilities" (min sum_s p_s(x) f(x, ξ_s)) with "decision dependent scenarios" (min sum_s p_s f(x, ξ_s(x))). — [slides](https://karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~racko/NMEK615/download/year2425/Stochastic_programming_problems_with_endogeneous_randomness.pdf)
- Miller et al.: location-scale family z_θ = (Σ_0 + Σ(θ)) z_0 + µ_0 + µ θ with z_0 ∼ D_0 fixed; for squared/absolute loss the performative risk is convex irrespective of ε (Example 3.2, Theorem 3.3); the proof of Prop. A.4 is an explicit coupling (eq. 4) with E[z'|z] = z. — [arXiv 2102.08570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)
- The 2025 facility-location paper's case study has µ_{jd} = µ_j + sum_n (−1)^{s(n)} α_{nj} µ_j 1_{Z_d}(z^(n)(j)), i.e. the mean is linear in active-zone indicators, but the paper nonetheless models the dependence as a choice among 2^|Z| distributions. — [arXiv 2509.09816](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09816v1)
- A UMD thesis comparing IPA, LR and weak derivatives on stochastic activity networks found weak derivatives with common random numbers beat the other estimators in nearly every configuration. — [UMD DRUM](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/handle/1903/12391)
- Basciftci, Ahmed & Shen model demand moments as piecewise-linear functions of y: µ_j(y) = min{ µ̄_j (1 + sum_{j'} λ^µ_{jj'} y_{j'}), µ^UB_j }, σ²_j(y) = max{ σ̄²_j (1 − sum_{j'} λ^σ_{jj'} y_{j'}), (σ^LB_j)² }. — [arXiv 1912.05577](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05577)

### Inferences
- (derived) Push-in for the facility model: if the scenario demand under y is d_s(y) = d_s^0 + A y with fixed weights p_s, then Q_s(y) = min{ q_s' z : W z = h_s + A y − T y, z ≥ 0 } and sum_s p_s Q_s(y) is convex in y (LP value function convex in RHS, composed with affine map), with no bilinear term; the binary problem is a standard two-stage MILP. This is the discrete analogue of Miller et al.'s location family and of Dupačová's eq. (3).
- (derived) Reweighting a fixed support {ξ_s} by p_s(y) is a different distribution map from shifting the atoms; both can match a given mean function µ(y), but they are not equal as distributions, and only the shift version preserves convexity. Whether the facility-location demand response is better modelled as a shift (CRN-coupled) or as a reweighting is a modelling decision; if only the mean (or mean and variance) is to be matched, the Basciftci-style moment-based DRO or the shift model avoids the bilinear weights.
- CRN in the simulation sense (same U for all y) is automatic in scenario-based models; its value here is conceptual (fix the base randomness, push y into the integrand).

### Gaps
- No source explicitly states the "LP value function convex in RHS ⇒ push-in preserves convexity" argument for decision-dependent two-stage programs; this is my derivation from standard LP sensitivity.
- Glasserman's book text was not accessible; only his structural-conditions paper abstract was.

## Q6. Performative prediction conditions (Perdomo et al. 2020; Drusvyatskiy & Xiao 2023)

### Takeaway
Their conditions (ε-sensitivity in W1, β-joint smoothness, γ-strong convexity of the loss in the decision) give convergence of repeated minimization to a performatively stable point when εβ/γ < 1 and a bound ‖θ_PO − θ_PS‖ ≤ 2 L_z ε/γ; they explicitly do not give convexity of the decision-dependent objective (Perdomo Prop. 4.2: it can be concave). For the scenario facility model ε is computable, but γ = 0 (piecewise-linear Q) and β-smoothness in the scenario variable fails, so the theorems do not apply without regularization and smoothing; nothing is stated for binary decisions.

### Cited Findings
- Perdomo et al. Def. 3.1: D(·) is ε-sensitive if W_1(D(θ), D(θ')) ≤ ε‖θ − θ'‖_2 for all θ, θ'. Def. 2.1: θ_PO = argmin_θ PR(θ), PR(θ) = E_{Z∼D(θ)} ℓ(Z; θ). Def. 2.3: θ_PS = argmin_θ E_{Z∼D(θ_PS)} ℓ(Z; θ). Theorem 3.5: with ℓ β-jointly smooth and γ-strongly convex, the RRM map satisfies ‖G(θ) − G(θ')‖ ≤ (εβ/γ)‖θ − θ'‖, and if ε < γ/β the iterates converge linearly to a unique θ_PS. Theorem 4.3: with ℓ L_z-Lipschitz in z, γ-strongly convex, D ε-sensitive, ‖θ_PO − θ_PS‖_2 ≤ 2 L_z ε/γ. Proposition 4.2: PR(θ) "can be concave in θ, even if the loss ℓ(z; θ) is β-jointly smooth (A1), γ-strongly convex (A2), and the distribution map D(·) is ε-sensitive with ε < γ/β." — [Perdomo, Zrnic, Mendler-Dünner, Hardt, ICML 2020 (text)](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICML/2020/performative_prediction__a562bc64.txt) (arXiv 2002.06673)
- Drusvyatskiy & Xiao (Math. ### Inferences
- (derived) Mapping: θ ↔ y, z ↔ ξ_s (demand vector), ℓ(z; θ) ↔ Q(y, ξ) + c'y, D(y) = sum_s p_s(y) δ_{ξ_s}. ε-sensitivity is checkable: W_1(D(y), D(y')) ≤ diam(Ξ)·TV = (diam(Ξ)/2)·‖p(y) − p(y')‖_1; for affine p_s(y) = a_s + b_s'y this gives ε ≤ (diam(Ξ)/2)·max_i sum_s |b_{si}| (with ‖·‖_1 on y) and an analogous ‖B‖ bound for ‖·‖_2. Strong convexity: Q(·, ξ) is piecewise-linear, so γ = 0 and every bound degenerates (εβ/γ = ∞, 2L_zε/γ = ∞); one must add (γ/2)‖y‖² on the relaxation, which changes the problem. β-joint smoothness: Q(y, ξ) is piecewise-linear in ξ (LP value in RHS), so ∇_y Q is piecewise-constant in ξ and not Lipschitz; smoothing of Q in ξ (or a quadratic recourse) would be needed. Conclusion: the performative conditions cannot be met by the raw scenario facility model; they can be met only for a regularized, smoothed continuous relaxation, and even then they certify a stable point, not convexity.
- (derived) The analogue of repeated risk minimization for binary y is the fixed-point iteration y^{k+1} ∈ argmin_y c'y + sum_s p_s(y^k) Q_s(y), each step an ordinary two-stage MILP (Hellemo's remark that fixing y leaves an ordinary SP). No contraction argument exists on {0,1}^n; cycling is possible, and Miller et al.'s Prop. 2.1 shows the stable point can be the maximizer of the true objective even in the continuous strongly convex case.

### Gaps
- Neither paper treats discrete/binary decisions; no decision-dependent-distribution convergence result for integer first stages was found.
- Drusvyatskiy–Xiao contains no convexity statement for the decision-dependent objective itself (confirmed by grep of the full text: no "mixture dominance", no convexity-of-PR theorem).

## Q7. Mixture dominance (Miller, Perdomo, Zrnic 2021), stated exactly

### Takeaway
Mixture dominance (A4): E_{z∼D(αθ+(1−α)θ')} ℓ(z; θ_0) ≤ E_{z∼αD(θ)+(1−α)D(θ')} ℓ(z; θ_0) for all θ, θ', θ_0 ∈ Θ and α ∈ (0,1); with γ-strong convexity in θ, β-smoothness in z and ε-sensitivity it gives (γ − 2εβ)-convexity of the performative risk, and ε = γ/(2β) is a sharp threshold. For affine p_s(y) on a fixed scenario set, mixture dominance holds with equality for any loss, so the only obstacles for the facility model are γ = 0 and non-smooth Q.

### Cited Findings
- Exact statement: "a distribution map, loss pair (D(·), ℓ) satisfies mixture dominance if the following condition holds for all θ, θ', θ_0 ∈ Θ and α ∈ (0, 1): E_{z∼D(αθ+(1−α)θ')} ℓ(z; θ_0) ≤ E_{z∼αD(θ)+(1−α)D(θ')} ℓ(z; θ_0). (A4)" — [arXiv 2102.08570, Sec. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)
- Interpretation: "mixture dominance (A4) essentially posits convexity of DPR in the first argument. Importantly, assuming convexity in each argument separately does not directly imply that the performative risk is convex." — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)
- Theorem 3.1: "Suppose that the loss function ℓ(z; θ) is γ-strongly convex in θ (A3a), β-smooth in z (A2), and that D(·) is ε-sensitive (A1). If mixture dominance (A4) holds, then the performative risk is λ-convex for λ = γ − 2εβ." Threshold: "γ/(2β) is a sharp threshold for convexity of the performative risk ... for ε > γ/(2β) the performative risk is strictly concave in that example." — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)
- Discrete case: "Mixture dominance can also hold in discrete settings, e.g. D(θ) = Bernoulli(a'θ + b) satisfies this condition for any loss." Example A.2: when "the probability of a positive outcome of a binary variable is linear in θ ... D(αθ + (1 − α)θ') = αD(θ) + (1 − α)D(θ')." — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)
- Corollary A.1: convex order D(αθ+(1−α)θ') ≤_cx αD(θ)+(1−α)D(θ') plus ℓ convex in z suffices for (A4); Proposition A.4: location-scale families (2) with Σ_0 + Σ(θ) full rank satisfy the convex-order condition (proof by coupling, Lemma A.3: z ≤_cx z' iff a coupling with E[z'|z] = z exists). — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)
- Theorem 3.3 (location-scale): λ = γ − 2εβ + γ_z σ_min(·)² type bound; Prop. 2.1: for any γ, Δ > 0 there is an instance where the unique stable point maximizes PR with gap ≥ Δ. — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)
- Two-stage approach: estimate the distribution map (OLS for the location parameter under random deployments θ_i ∼ N(0, I)), then optimize the plug-in risk; sample complexity O(d/Δ) versus Õ(d²/Δ²) for derivative-free methods (Theorem 4.1). — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)

### Inferences
- (derived) For a fixed scenario set with D(y) = sum_s p_s(y) δ_{ξ_s}: if p(y) is affine in y then D(αy + (1−α)y') = αD(y) + (1−α)D(y') exactly (same mechanism as Example A.2), so (A4) holds with equality for every loss. If p(y) is multilinear or otherwise nonlinear, (A4) is a condition on the vector-valued function y ↦ (E_{p(y)}[ℓ(·; θ_0)])_{θ_0}, i.e. convexity in y of sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y') for every fixed y'; this is checkable scenario-by-scenario for a given p (e.g. it holds if each p_s(y) is convex in y and Q_s(y') ≥ 0, or if p_s(y) Q_s(y') summed is convex).
- (derived) With (A4) holding and γ = 0 (LP recourse), Theorem 3.1 gives λ = −2εβ < 0: no information. Adding a quadratic regularizer (γ/2)‖y‖² to a relaxation gives convexity if ε < γ/(2β), where β would have to come from a smoothed Q; the condition is then an explicit trade-off between the strength of demand response (ε) and how much the relaxation is distorted (γ). This is a continuous-relaxation statement only; it says nothing about {0,1}^n beyond providing a convex lower-bounding relaxation.

### Gaps
- No paper applies mixture dominance to scenario-based stochastic programs or to integer decisions; the affine-p observation is mine, by analogy to Example A.2.

## Q8. Linear reformulations when p(y) is affine or multilinear in binary y (tighter than McCormick)

### Takeaway
For binary y and p_s(y) ≥ 0 affine or multilinear in y, the objective is exactly linearizable: pull the second-stage minimization out (valid because p_s(y) ≥ 0), introduce w_{is} = y_i z_s (or w_{Ms} = (prod_{i∈M} y_i) z_s), and linearize each product of a binary (or binary monomial) with the bounded continuous z_s by McCormick, which is exact at integer y; the RLT/Sherali–Adams device of multiplying the recourse constraints W z_s = h_s − T y by y_i and (1 − y_i) gives the tighter LP relaxation of Adams–Sherali, and for the pure-binary monomials in p(y) the standard linearization is exact (equals the multilinear polytope) iff the monomial hypergraph is Berge-acyclic.

### Cited Findings
- Basciftci, Ahmed & Shen: for w = η z with η ∈ [η_lo, η_hi] and z ∈ {0,1}, the McCormick set M = {(w, η, z): η − (1 − z) η_hi ≤ w ≤ η − η_lo(1 − z), η_lo z ≤ w ≤ η_hi z} is exact: "Because variable z is binary valued, we have an exact reformulation in (13)". For the trilinear w = η z_1 z_2 with η ≥ 0 bounded and z_1, z_2 binary, Proposition 2 (citing [27]) states the given inequality set equals conv{(w, η, z_1, z_2): w = η z_1 z_2, η ∈ [η_lo, η_hi], z ∈ {0,1}²}. They obtain "an exact mixed-integer linear programming reformulation" via "duality and convex envelopes" when moments are piecewise linear in y, linearizing products Y_lm = y_l y_m, Δ = δ y, Γ = γ y, Ψ = γ y_l y_m. — [arXiv 1912.05577](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05577)
- The 2025 facility-location paper linearizes δ_d·w (distribution indicator times second-stage cost) with auxiliary h, yielding a MILP; its LP-relaxation-based Jensen valid inequality "is not tight in general." — [arXiv 2509.09816](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09816v1)
- Pantuso & Hewitt use McCormick envelopes τ ≤ y, τ ≤ Y δ, τ ≥ y + Y δ − Y on q'y δ for a distribution-independent LP lower bound. — [arXiv 2506.12753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12753)
- Adams & Sherali (1990, Oper. Res. 38(2)) give a linearization of mixed binary-continuous products whose continuous relaxation is tighter than the alternatives in the literature. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/oropre/v38y1990i2p217-226.html). Fortet's inequalities are the classical binary-product linearization. — [Mallach, OJMO](https://ojmo.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/ojmo.34.pdf)
- Del Pia & Khajavirad (2025): "the widely-used standard linearization ... describes the multilinear polytope if and only if the hypergraph is Berge-[acyclic]" (snippet truncated at "Berge-"); their complete edge relaxation "is stronger than ... the standard linearization, the flower relaxation, and the intersection of all possible recursive McCormick relaxations" and is an extension of the multilinear polytope iff the hypergraph is α-acyclic. — [Lehigh ISE TR 25T-011](https://engineering.lehigh.edu/sites/engineering.lehigh.edu/files/_DEPARTMENTS/ise/pdf/tech-papers/25/25T_011.pdf); polynomial-size extended formulations for acyclic hypergraphs — [arXiv 2501.04805](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04805v1). For cycle hypergraphs the multilinear polytope needs standard-linearization, flower and odd β-cycle inequalities. — [NSF PAR 10081429](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10081429)
- Minimum-size recursive McCormick linearizations of multilinear programs are NP-hard in general, FPT when monomials have at most three variables. — [MERL TR2025-098](https://merl.com/publications/docs/TR2025-098.pdf)
- Adjustable-robust literature: when uncertainty depends only on binary variables the problem "can become an MILP by expressing each integer variable as the difference between two binary variables and linearizing the bilinear terms." — [arXiv 2007.00247](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.00247)
- Hellemo et al. note the affine-scaling and convex-combination models have bilinear objectives and solve them with BARON (which builds McCormick relaxations internally) rather than by explicit linearization. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10287-018-0330-0)

### Inferences
- (derived) Exact MILP for affine p: with p_s(y) = a_s + b_s'y ≥ 0 on {0,1}^n, F(y) = c'y + min_z sum_s (a_s + b_s'y) q_s'z_s subject to W z_s = h_s − T y, z_s ≥ 0, because a nonnegative weighted sum of minima of independent LPs equals the minimum of the weighted sum. Products y_i z_s are replaced by w_{is} with the Basciftci-type (binary × bounded continuous) McCormick set, which is exact at integer y given bounds Z on z_s. Size: S·n·dim(z) extra continuous variables. For multilinear p (e.g. the exact binary expansion of an exponential tilt, Q1), each monomial y_M z_s is a (binary monomial) × continuous product; linearize y_M by its standard-linearization variable Y_M and then Y_M z_s by McCormick (Y_M ∈ {0,1} at integer points), or use the trilinear convex-hull set of Basciftci Prop. 2 for |M| = 2.
- (derived) Tighter than plain McCormick: level-1 RLT multiplies W z_s = h_s − T y by y_i and (1 − y_i), giving W w_{is} = h_s y_i − T Y^{(i)} and W (z_s − w_{is}) = h_s(1 − y_i) − T(y − Y^{(i)}), with Y^{(i)}_k = y_i y_k linearized (Y^{(i)}_i = y_i). This is the Adams–Sherali mixed-binary construction; it is equivalent to the disjunctive "two copies of the recourse" formulation (z_s = w_{is} if y_i = 1, w_{is} = 0 if y_i = 0) and its LP relaxation dominates McCormick on the products y_i z_s. The relaxation is still not the convex hull of the whole problem because the products y_i y_k across different i interact (the multilinear polytope issue): exactness of the p-part holds iff the monomial hypergraph is Berge-acyclic (Del Pia–Khajavirad), which an affine p satisfies trivially and a product-form p (full tilt over all facilities) violates.
- (derived) Alternative exact scheme when p depends on y only through K ≪ n "patterns" (zones): enumerate distributions d ∈ D with indicator δ_d, link δ to y linearly, and linearize δ_d·(q_s'z_s) (the 2509.09816 extensive form); Benders with distribution-specific cuts scales to |D| = 2^17 but not 2^20 in their tests.
- Which of these works for binary y: all of them (they rely on y ∈ {0,1}); none give convexity of a continuous relaxation beyond the LP relaxation of the linearized MILP.
