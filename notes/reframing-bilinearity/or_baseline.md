# OR / Stochastic-Programming Baseline: Exact Reformulations of sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y) with Binary First Stage (Decision-Dependent Probabilities, Facility Location)

Scope note. All formulas below are transcribed from the primary PDFs (converted with pdftotext) unless marked otherwise. Notation is kept as in each paper; the common object is

    min_{y in {0,1}^n}  f^T y + sum_s p_s(y) Q_s(y),   Q_s(y) = min{ q_s^T w : W_s w = h_s - T_s y, w >= 0 }.

Terminology: Goel-Grossmann/Li-Grossmann call decision-dependent *probabilities* "Type 1" and decision-dependent *timing of revelation* "Type 2"; Hellemo et al. and much of the newer literature reverse the numbering or simply say "decision-dependent probabilities" vs "decision-dependent information discovery". The query's "type 2 / decision-dependent probability" corresponds to Li-Grossmann's **Type 1** ([Li & Grossmann 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/chemical-engineering/articles/10.3389/fceng.2020.622241/pdf), p.13: "In the case of Type 1 endogenous uncertainties, decisions influence the parameter realizations by altering the underlying probability distributions ... Type 1 endogenous has not been widely studied").

## Key Question 0: Catalogue overview of reformulation techniques (what each exploits, what it costs, largest instances)

### Takeaway
Every exact method in the literature removes the bilinearity p_s(y)·Q_s(y) by exploiting one of five structures: (A) p(y) is a *selection* among finitely many distributions (indicator/one-hot δ_d) so the product δ_d·w is binary×bounded-continuous and McCormick/big-M is exact; (B) p(y) is *affine in y* (mixture proportions, moment functions, radii) so that after LP-dualising the inner problem the only products are binary×dual and McCormick/Glover is exact; (C) p(y) is a *product of per-component Bernoulli factors* (independent components) so that Bayes-rule scaling between neighbouring y gives linear "distribution shaping" inequalities, optionally compressed by BDDs or bundles; (D) the probability is pushed into the *cost* via a change of measure / random-variable transformation so the problem becomes exogenous; (E) the product is never formed: a *distribution-specific Benders/L-shaped cut* with a big-M deactivation term is generated only for the distribution actually enforced by the incumbent y. No paper reports a reformulation whose continuous relaxation is provably tighter than McCormick for the generic binary×LP-value product; the only convex-hull result is for products of a probability simplex vector with a one-hot binary vector (RLT, Noyan et al.).

### Inferences
- The two scalable exact engines for the facility-location case are A2 (lazy distribution-specific cuts; scales in the number of distributions *visited*, not |D|) and B1 (closed-form inner LP so that no scenario recourse variables appear and products are only dual×binary). All other methods either enumerate scenarios (C1–C3) or need a global solver in the master (B2).
- Nobody has published a relaxation-tightening change of variables for w_s = p_s(y)·θ_s with θ_s an LP value function; the closest hull result (E1) requires the binary factor to be one-hot and the continuous factor to lie on a simplex.

## Key Question 1: How do Hellemo, Barton & Tomasgard (2018) classify and reformulate decision-dependent probabilities? Which forms admit linearisation?

### Takeaway
Hellemo et al. give a taxonomy of two-stage recourse models with decision-dependent uncertainty and three concrete probability mechanisms: (i) prior probabilities distorted by an affine transformation of y, (ii) a convex combination (mixture) of several given distributions with decision-dependent weights, and (iii) distribution parameters that are themselves first-stage variables, with the distribution entered either exactly or by a rational approximation; all instances were solved with BARON using selective branching, i.e., the authors did not claim an MILP reformulation. Secondary sources confirm that (i)–(ii) with affine dependence yield probabilities linear in y, so that the product with a scenario cost is binary×continuous and linearisable, whereas (iii) is genuinely nonconvex.

### Cited Findings
- Abstract (verbatim, via Semantic Scholar record of DOI 10.1007/s10287-018-0330-0): "a taxonomy of stochastic programming recourse models with decision-dependent uncertainty ... Two-stage models are formulated where prior probabilities are distorted through an affine transformation or combined using a convex combination of several probability distributions. Additionally, we present models where the parameters of the probability distribution are first-stage decision variables. The probability distributions are either incorporated in the model using the exact expression or by using a rational approximation. Test instances for each formulation are solved with a commercial solver, BARON, using selective branching." — [Semantic Scholar API record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1007/s10287-018-0330-0?fields=title,abstract,openAccessPdf); journal page [Springer, Comput. Manag. Sci. 15(3):369–395](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10287-018-0330-0); 146 citations as of the API call.
- Pantuso & Hewitt's description of the Hellemo models: "the authors formulate two-stage models where prior probabilities are distorted through affine transformations, or combined using convex combinations of several probability distributions. Furthermore, the authors present models which incorporate the parameters of the probability distribution as first stage decision variables"; and "In [12, 13] given reference discrete distributions are transformed by first stage decisions through affine transformations. - Pantuso & Hewitt also state that the Hellemo formulations are what they linearise for the extensive-form benchmark: "The linearized versions are obtained by linearizing the bilinear terms in the objective functions using [22, 2]" (McCormick 1976; Al-Khayyal & Falk 1983). — [Pantuso & Hewitt 2025, §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12753)
- Noyan et al. describe the mixture variant and when it is linear: "the random parameter vector is drawn from a population that consists of subpopulations whose proportions are decision-dependent (see, e.g., Dupacova, 2006; Hellemo et al., 2018) ... If ... the mappings π_s are affine, with π_s(x) = π_s^0 + π_s^T x ..., then scenario probabilities can be expressed via the linear constraints p_i(x) = Σ_s π_s^0 p_{is} + π_s^T x p_{is} for i ∈ [n]." — [Noyan, Rudolf & Lejeune, Opt. - Basciftci et al. cite Hellemo only as an overview: "Hellemo et al. [14] conduct an overview of recent studies in this area by providing ways to model decision-dependent uncertainties in stochastic programs." — [Basciftci et al. arXiv 1912.05577 §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.05577)

### Inferences
- Reconstructed forms (from the above descriptions; exact symbols not verified): (i) **distortion**: p_s(y) = p_s^0 + a_s^T y (affine), objective Σ_s (p_s^0 + a_s^T y) Q_s(y) — each term is (affine in y)×(LP value), i.e., binary×continuous after introducing θ_s ≥ Q_s(y); (ii) **convex combination**: p_s(y) = Σ_k λ_k(y) p_s^k with Σ_k λ_k(y) = 1, λ affine in y (or λ_k themselves variables, in which case λ_k·θ_s is continuous×continuous and needs spatial branching); (iii) **parametrised**: p_s = g(ϑ) with ϑ a first-stage variable, g nonlinear (hence "rational approximation" and BARON).
- Because the paper used BARON with "selective branching" for *all* formulations, it did not exploit the exactness of McCormick for binary×continuous products; the linearised-extensive-form baseline that later papers (Pantuso & Hewitt) attribute to Hellemo is their own McCormick/Al-Khayyal–Falk linearisation.

### Gaps
- Exact equations, instance sizes, and BARON solve times from Hellemo et al. (2018) are not available here (full text blocked; abstract only).

## Key Question 2: Basciftci, Ahmed & Shen (EJOR 2021) — exact MILP, McCormick variables, bounds, valid inequalities, instance sizes

### Takeaway
The decision-dependent DRO facility location model is made exact by (a) solving the per-customer second-stage LP in closed form so that the scenario cost is *linear* in y, (b) LP-dualising the moment-based inner maximisation so that y appears only through the affine moment functions multiplied by dual variables, and (c) replacing the resulting binary×dual and binary×binary×dual products by McCormick envelopes (exact for binary factors, Meyer–Floudas hull for the trilinear case) with hand-set dual upper bounds; valid inequalities come from the extreme rays of the dual feasible cone. Instances reach only |I| = 10 sites, |J| = 20 customers, K = 100 support points (111 s).

### Inferences
- The decisive structural trick is not McCormick but the closed-form inner LP: because the recourse decomposes by customer with capacities C_i y_i, the LP value is a *maximum of affine functions of y*, so the K "scenarios" (support points) never carry recourse variables and the DRO dual constraints (16b) are linear in (y, duals). This is why the model is a compact MILP rather than a scenario-expanded one.
- Variable count of (16): |I| + 5|J| + 4|J||I| + |J||I|(|I|−1) + |I|(|I|−1)/2; constraint count dominated by (|I|+1)|J|K in (16b) plus 4 per bilinear and 8 per trilinear McCormick block. For |I| = 10, |J| = 20: ~2,000 McCormick auxiliaries and 22,000 (16b) rows — consistent with the 111 s solve.
- The reported exponential growth of run time in |I| (also noted by Yu & Shen for the same construction) indicates the McCormick LP relaxation is weak despite being exact at integer points.

### Gaps
- The paper does not report LP-relaxation gaps or root-node bounds, nor larger instances than 10×20.

## Key Question 3: Rahimian & Mehrotra (2024) — bilinearly constrained bilinear program and finitely convergent cutting-plane schemes

### Takeaway
With a finite sample space and a polyhedral decision-dependent ambiguity set P(x) = {p ≥ 0 : p_ω ≤ p_ω ≤ p̄_ω, Σ_ω p_ω g^i(ξ_ω) ≤ ϑ^i(x)}, Lagrangian duality turns min_x max_{P∈P(x)} E_P[h(x,ξ)] into min_{x,λ≥0} λ^T ϑ(x) + Σ_ω G_ω(x,λ) with G_ω an LP in (x,λ); the decision dependence survives only as the bilinear master term λ^T ϑ(x). Three finitely convergent L-shaped-type schemes are given (Benders cuts for convex recourse; concave-minimisation master when ϑ is convex/concave; disjunctive CGLP cuts when the recourse objective has an x^T L y term), all requiring an ε-global oracle for the nonconvex master.

### Cited Findings
- Ambiguity set (6): P(x) := { p ≥ 0 : p_ω ≤ p_ω ≤ p̄_ω, ω ∈ [N]; Σ_{ω∈[N]} p_ω g^i(ξ_ω) ≤ ϑ^i(x), i ∈ [s] } with ϑ^1 = 1, g^1 = 1, ϑ^2 = −1, g^2 = −1 for normalisation; subsumes Wasserstein-1 with decision-dependent radius τ(x), total variation with τ(x), and moment sets (7) with (1−τ_1)μ_0(x) ≤ Σ p_ω ξ_ω ≤ (1+τ_1)μ_0(x), τ_2 (σ_0(x)^2+μ_0(x)^2) ≤ Σ p_ω ξ_ω^2 ≤ τ̄_2 (σ_0(x)^2+μ_0(x)^2). — [DDRO pdf §1.1](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/DDRO_RahimianMehrotra.pdf)

- Earlier bilinear machinery: Rahimian & Mehrotra, "A finitely convergent disjunctive cutting plane algorithm for bilinear programming" (ε-optimal or infeasibility for general continuous bilinear programs). — [Optimization Online 7594](https://optimization-online.org/2020/01/7594/)

### Inferences
- For a *binary* first stage and *affine* ϑ(x) (the facility-location moment case), λ^T ϑ(x) is a sum of binary×continuous products, so the master becomes an MILP with McCormick (exactly Basciftci's construction); the Rahimian–Mehrotra contribution for that case is the Benders decomposition of the N scenario LPs rather than a tighter relaxation.
- The Lagrangian treatment also shows that in DRO the "probability × cost" product disappears entirely by dualisation — p never multiplies Q; the bilinearity migrates to dual × ϑ(x). This is a change of variables that is unavailable in the plain SP setting where p_s(y) is fixed by y.

### Gaps
- No binary-first-stage facility instance is reported; no comparison of the LP relaxation of the McCormick master versus the global-solver master.

## Key Question 4: Noyan, Rudolf & Lejeune (IJOC 2022) and Luo & Mehrotra (2020) — reformulations used

### Takeaway
Luo & Mehrotra show that LP/conic/Lagrangian duality of the inner problem goes through unchanged when the ambiguity-set parameters depend on x, giving finite-constraint but nonconvex reformulations (products of duals with l(x), u(x), p(x), r(x), η(x)) and no algorithm or computations. Noyan et al. centre an earth-mover's-distance ball on a *decision-dependent nominal distribution*; for total-variation balls they get MILPs by combining (i) distribution shaping for p_i(x) when the decision changes Bernoulli factors, (ii) a disjunction on the VaR scenario, and (iii) an RLT convex-hull description of z = π β^T that replaces McCormick; for scheduling they linearise u·τ and λ·τ with McCormick and report RLT equalities as decisive.

### Cited Findings (Luo & Mehrotra)
- Framework (D3RO): min_{x∈X} f(x) + max_{P∈P(x)} E_P[h(x,ξ)], h a recourse function; Assumption 1: decision-independent finite support {ξ^k}_{k=1}^N for all x. — [arXiv 1806.09215 §1, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09215)
- Theorem 1 (measure/moment bounds, ν_1(x) = Σ p_i(x)δ, ν_2(x) = Σ p̄_i(x)δ): min_{x,α,β,γ,μ} f(x) + α^T l(x) + β^T u(x) + γ^T p(x) + μ^T p̄(x) s.t. (α+β)^T f(ξ^k) + γ_k + μ_k ≥ h(x,ξ^k) ∀k; α ≥ 0, β ≤ 0, γ ≥ 0, μ ≤ 0 (Corollary 1 replaces h by g(x,y^k,ξ^k) with ψ_i(x,y^k,ξ^k) ≥ 0). — ibid. (3), (5)
- Theorem 2 (Delage–Ye type with μ(x), Q(x), α(x), β(x)): min f(x) + s + [√α(x), −(Q(x)^{−1/2} μ(x))^T] z + β(x)Q(x)•Y s.t. s − (ξ^i)^T Q(x)^{−1/2} z_1 + (ξ^i − μ(x))(ξ^i − μ(x))^T • Y ≥ h(x,ξ^i), z ∈ SOC, Y ⪰ 0. — ibid. (7)
- Theorem 3 (Wasserstein-1 with radius r(x)): min f(x) − Σ_i p̂_i β_i − r(x)γ + η s.t. α_i + μ_i + η ≥ h(x,ξ^i); −α_i + β_j + ‖ξ^i − ξ^j‖γ + λ_ij ≥ 0; μ,λ ≥ 0, γ ≤ 0. — ibid. (17)
- Theorem 4 (φ-divergence with η(x)): semi-infinite program (23) or KKT form (24) with αφ'(N p_i) + β + h(x,ξ^i) + λ_i = 0. Theorem 5 (KS distance). — ibid. §3.4–3.5
- "The reformulated models are generally non-convex optimization problems requiring further investigation towards developing efficient algorithms"; "we do not focus on developing any new (possibly more efficient) algorithms". No computational section in the arXiv version. — ibid. §1, §4
- Rahimian & Mehrotra's summary: "in [36] ... the maximum allowed distance from a nominal distribution is assumed to be decision-dependent". — [DDRO pdf §1.3](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/DDRO_RahimianMehrotra.pdf)

### Cited Findings (Noyan, Rudolf & Lejeune)
- Ambiguity set: "balls centered on a decision-dependent probability distribution ... based on a class of earth mover's distances that includes both the total variation distance and the Wasserstein metrics"; published IJOC 34(2):729–751. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/orijoc/v34y2022i2p729-751.html); [Opt. - Two ways the nominal distribution depends on x (§3.1): realisations ξ_i(x) with fixed probabilities (scheduling: compressible processing times), or fixed realisations with p_i(x) (pre-disaster planning via distribution shaping, or affine mixtures as quoted in KQ1). — ibid. §3.1
- Continuous Wasserstein-p ball with bilinear outcome G(x,ζ) = ζ^T v(x): sup_{ζ∈B} ρ(ζ^T v) = ρ(ξ^T v) + Cκ‖v‖_q (Prop. 4.2, Pflug et al. 2012) so the risk-neutral DRO is the deterministic problem min_x ξ̄(x)^T v(x) + κ‖v(x)‖_q (11). — ibid. §4
- Pre-disaster planning base model: p_i(x) = Π_{ℓ: ξ^i_ℓ=1} [(1−x_ℓ)σ^0_ℓ + x_ℓ σ^1_ℓ] Π_{ℓ: ξ^i_ℓ=0} [(1−x_ℓ)(1−σ^0_ℓ) + x_ℓ(1−σ^1_ℓ)] (34), min_x Σ_i p_i(x) G_i (35); "the probability expression (34) is a highly non-linear function of the decision variables. - **RLT hull (Appendix A.3)**: for T = {(z,β,π_L) ∈ [0,1]^{n×n}×{0,1}^n×[0,1]^n : z = π_L β^T, β^T 1 = 1, 1^T π_L = 1}, "Adapting [Gupte et al. 2017] ... conv(T) = {(z,β,π_L) ∈ [0,1]^{n×n}×[0,1]^n×[0,1]^n : 1^T z = β^T, z 1 = π_L, 1^T π_L = 1}. Incorporating the arising valid inequalities into an MILP can dramatically improve computational performance"; final MILP (53) uses only Σ_i z^{ij} = β^j, Σ_j z^{ij} = π^i_L, Σ_j β^j = 1. — ibid. ### Inferences
- Noyan et al.'s RLT result is the one documented case in this literature where a product of a probability-vector variable and a binary vector is given a *convex-hull* linear description with fewer constraints than McCormick; it relies on the binary being one-hot (Σβ = 1) and the continuous factor summing to one. - Luo–Mehrotra's forms show the generic pattern "dual multiplier × decision-dependent parameter"; with binary x and affine parameters every such product is McCormick-exact, which is what Basciftci et al. and Yu & Shen implement.

## Key Question 5: Laumanns, Prestwich & Kawas (2014) — distribution shaping and scenario bundling

### Takeaway
Distribution shaping replaces the degree-n polynomial p_x(ξ) = Π_i [(1−x_i)p_i + x_i q_i]^{ξ_i}[…]^{1−ξ_i} by a chain of n probability vectors π_0 → π_1 → … → π_n, each obtained from the previous by Bayes-rule rescaling that is switched on/off by one binary x_k through four linear inequalities; the stochastic program is an exact MIP when the scenario cost is decision-independent and a mixed-integer *bilinear* program when it is a recourse LP in x. Scenario bundling (dynamic interchangeability from constraint programming) shrinks 2^30 scenarios to 223 bundles for the Istanbul network, giving exact solutions in seconds.

### Cited Findings
- Model: Ω = {0,1}^n, P_x{ξ_i = 1} = (1−x_i)p_i + x_i q_i, independence, P_x{ξ} = Π_i P_x{ξ_i}; "Using such products directly ... result in polynomials of degree n in x. Therefore previous approaches relied either on linearizations [29] or convex approximations [11]." — [Laumanns et al. 2014, §3](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/bitstreams/5cd4f601-25ad-46cc-a822-2d281505e874/download)
- Neighbour relation: for x^1 − x^0 = e_i, P_{x^1}{ξ} = P_{x^0}{ξ}·q_i/p_i if ξ_i = 1, ·(1−q_i)/(1−p_i) if ξ_i = 0. — ibid.
- **Polyhedral chain**: x̌^k = (x_1,…,x_k,0,…,0), π^ξ_k = P_{x̌^k}({ξ}); constraints π^ξ_k ≤ (q_k/p_k) π^ξ_{k−1} + 1 − x_k (ξ_k = 1); π^ξ_k ≤ ((1−q_k)/(1−p_k)) π^ξ_{k−1} + 1 − x_k (ξ_k = 0); π^ξ_k ≤ π^ξ_{k−1} + x_k; Σ_ξ π^ξ_k = 1; π^ξ_0 = Π_{i:ξ_i=0}(1−p_i) Π_{i:ξ_i=1} p_i given. Program (5)–(7): min Σ_ξ π^ξ_n f(ξ) s.t. π_k ∈ P_k(π_{k−1}, x_k), x ∈ X "is a mixed-integer linear program ... given that the cost in each scenario is a constant ... However, if f(ξ) for instance was a recourse function of a stochastic linear program, then we would obtain a mixed-integer bi-linear program instead." — ibid. §3
- Size: "The above MIP has only n binary variables, but 2^n · n auxiliary continuous variables". — ibid.
- m-ary extension: P_x({ξ_i = j}) = (1−x_i)p^j_i + x_i q^j_i, π^ξ_k ≤ (q^j_k/p^j_k) π^ξ_{k−1} + 1 − x_k. — ibid. §3.1
- Bundling: bundle s ∈ {0,1,*}^n with B_s = {ξ : ξ_i = s_i ∨ s_i = *}; scaling φ^s_k ≤ (q_k/p_k)φ^s_{k−1} + 1 − x_k (s_k = 1), ≤ ((1−q_k)/(1−p_k))φ^s_{k−1} + 1 − x_k (s_k = 0), φ^s_k ≤ φ^s_{k−1} (s_k = *), φ^s_k ≤ φ^s_{k−1} + x_k, Σ_s φ^s_k = 1. Detection: branch on link i only if C_i ≠ F_i (shortest path with all unrealised links surviving vs failing); finding the minimum bundle set is NP-complete (ordered decision trees). — ibid. §4
- Istanbul instance: 25 nodes, 30 links, 2^30 scenarios, 5 O-D pairs, budgets B1–B3 = 10/20/30% of 11,640; bundle sets 39, 29, 56, 26, 73 (total 223); "Solution times for the stochastic MIPs for the different budgets are under 5 seconds each" (CPLEX 12.6); exact solutions "up to 10% better" than Peeta et al.'s approximate ones; with all paths allowed (Ma = ∞): 175–374 bundles per pair, 297–946 s. — ibid. Tables 4–6
- Random planar grids: up to 77 links (1.5×10^23 scenarios) with median bundle sets 9 (α = 1.1) to 36 (α = 1.5), but >10^5 for α = ∞. — ibid. Table 7
- Follow-up (Haus, Michini & Laumanns 2017): BDD-based aggregation; with p_i(x) = p_i + Δ_i x_i the node probabilities satisfy p(u) ≤ (p_i+Δ_i)p(v) + (1−p_i−Δ_i)p(w) + (1−x_i), p(u) ≤ p_i p(v) + (1−p_i)p(w) + x_i, and the two reverse inequalities; "the formulation of P_α contains |V(B)| + |E| variables and 4|V(B)| + 1 constraints"; Istanbul MIP 1466×454 solved in 1 s (vs 14174×6221 with bundles), without cutoffs 20137×5064 in 36 s. — [arXiv 1701.04055 §3.4, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.04055)

### Inferences
- Distribution shaping is an exact *linear* encoding of p(y) for the independent-Bernoulli structure, but it does not remove the product p·Q when Q depends on y; combining it with a recourse LP re-introduces bilinearity (continuous π × continuous θ), which is why Noyan et al. restrict to decision-independent outcomes G_i and linearise only π × one-hot β.
- Its variable count 2^n·n (or |bundles|·n) is the price of exactness; bundling is only effective when the recourse value is insensitive to most components (dynamic interchangeability), which does not hold for capacitated facility location with demand at every customer.

### Gaps
- No facility-location application of distribution shaping was found; applicability to continuous (e.g., truncated-normal) demand distributions is not addressed.

## Key Question 6: Pantuso (2025, J. Comb. Optim., arXiv 2509.09816) — facility location with combinatorially many distributions; why 2^17 is reachable

### Takeaway
The distribution is determined by *which zones contain an open facility* (|Z| ≤ |I| zones → 2^{|Z|} distributions); the extensive form has a one-hot δ_d and McCormick-linearised δ_d·w, while the decision-dependent L-shaped method keeps a master with only O(|Z|) binaries, evaluates only the single distribution enforced by the incumbent, and adds a Benders cut deactivated by a big-M term unless exactly that zone set is open. Because on average ≈1 cut per *visited* distribution suffices, instances with 2^17 = 131,072 distributions are solved (74/84 under 0.5% gap, avg 27,139 s), whereas CPLEX on the extensive form finds no feasible solution for |Z| = 10.

### Cited Findings
- Model (eq. 1–2): min_{x∈{0,1}^{|I|}} F^T x + Σ_{d∈D} 1_d(x) E_{P_d}[Q(x,ξ)]; extensive form with binary δ_d, zone indicators y_z with Σ_{i∈I_z} x_i ≤ |I_z| y_z, Σ_{i∈I_z} x_i ≥ y_z, and Σ_{z∈Z_d} y_z − Σ_{z∈Z_d^C} y_z = |Z_d| iff δ_d = 1 (pair of inequalities); "precisely 2^{|Z|}" distributions; demands truncated normal with mean and s.d. linear in zone indicators ("a proxy"). — [arXiv 2509.09816v1 §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09816v1); JCO 50(4) 2025 [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jcomop/v50y2025i4d10.1007_s10878-025-01371-7.html)
- Linearisation in the extensive form: products δ_d w^{sd}_{ij} replaced by h^{sd}_{ij} with three constraints using bound min{ξ^{sd}_j, C_i}. — ibid.
- Assumptions A1–A4: LP second stage with fixed recourse; finite scenarios per distribution with π_{sd}; always feasible (no feasibility cuts); second-stage cost bounded by U. — ibid. §3
- Relaxed master (eq. 3): min Σ_i F_i x_i + μ s.t. zone linking, y ∈ {0,1}; "O(|Z|) size and no δ_d or scenario variables". — ibid.
- **Cut (eq. 4)**: μ ≥ Σ_{s∈S_d} π_{sd} ρ_{sd}^T (h_{sd} − T_{sd} x) − U( |Z_d| − Σ_{z∈Z_d} y_z + Σ_{z∈Z_d^C} y_z ); Prop. 1 (violated when μ underestimates), Prop. 2 (satisfied when μ correct), Prop. 3 (finite convergence: dual feasible region independent of master variables → finitely many dual extreme points). — ibid.
- Valid inequality (Prop. 4, eq. 6): μ ≤ Σ_i Σ_j R_ij min{C_i, max_d E_{P_d}[ξ_j]} x_i (Jensen, Q concave in ξ for the profit-maximising transportation LP); "not tight in general and does not guarantee convergence"; mainly helps feasibility on large instances. — ibid.
- Cuts per distribution: average close to 1, mode 1, s.d. < 0.20; cuts added lazily at integer B&B nodes. — ibid. §5
- Grid instances: |I| ∈ {10,15,20,25}, |J| ∈ {50,100}, |Z| ∈ {5,7,10}, |S_d| ∈ {50,100}, ~4,000 instances; CPLEX 20.1, 1800 s, 40 CPUs/188 GB; extensive form: for |Z| = 5 solved 6–20 of 336 to <0.5%; for |Z| = 10 "found no feasible solution in any of 336 instances"; for |I| ≥ 15 minimum gaps "hundreds to over 1000%"; LS: all 4032 solved, 9.9–303.9 s average by |Z|, ~70 B&B nodes at |Z| = 5 and ~1950 at |Z| = 10, tree memory < 0.1 MB. — ibid. Tables 2–4
- Scaling (|I| = 20, |J| = 50, |S_d| = 50, 36,000 s): |Z| = 12 (4,096 dist.) 84/84 in 431 s; |Z| = 15 (32,768) 84/84 in 4,258 s; |Z| = 17 (131,072) 74/84 (77 with VI) < 0.5%, avg 27,139 s; |Z| = 20 (~10^6) 0/84 solved, 61 (71 with VI) feasible, avg gap 7955% / 7599%. — ibid. §5
- Discrepancy: the abstract says instances "with up to 2^17 potential distributions and hundreds of thousands of scenarios" are solved "within minutes"; the |Z| = 17 average of ~27,000 s does not support "minutes" for that size (the "minutes" claim matches |Z| ≤ 12). — ibid. abstract vs §5

### Inferences
- The reason |D| = 2^17 is reachable is that the algorithm's complexity is governed by the number of distributions *visited* by the B&B incumbents (each needing one subproblem batch of |S_d| LPs and ≈1 cut), not by |D|; the one-hot δ_d is eliminated from the master by expressing "x ∈ X_d" through |Z| zone indicators (Pantuso & Hewitt §3.3: O(T·max_t|K_t|) binaries instead of Π_t|K_t|).
- The extensive-form failure (no feasible solution at |Z| = 10 with 50 scenarios per distribution) quantifies how badly the McCormick-linearised one-hot formulation scales: 2^10·50·|I||J| ≈ 51,200·1,000 recourse variables each with three linearisation rows.

### Gaps
- The final 27k characters of the arXiv HTML (likely conclusions/appendix) were not read; no LP-relaxation bound of the extensive form is reported.

## Key Question 7: Valid Benders / L-shaped cuts derived directly for the decision-dependent case (Pantuso & Hewitt; Dupačová; Goel & Grossmann; Vayanos; Apap & Grossmann; Zhan & Zheng; Bazotte et al.)

### Takeaway
The only general-purpose Benders theory for decision-dependent *probabilities* is Pantuso & Hewitt (Math. Prog. 2025): optimality cut μ ≥ Σ_s π_{sd} ρ_s^T(h_{sd} − T_{sd}x) − U(1 − 1_d(x)), feasibility cut ρ^T(h_{sd} − T_{sd}x) ≤ U_{ds}(1 − 1_d(x)), integer-L-shaped analogues for MILP recourse, finite convergence, and a reformulation of 1_d(x) with few binaries; its validity hinges on a finite partition {X_d} of X and bounded recourse. Dupačová (2006) and Jonsbråten–Wets–Woodruff (1998) are the conceptual sources (finite set of distributions indexed by Boolean d, implicit enumeration). The Goel–Grossmann/Apap–Grossmann/Vayanos line handles decision-dependent *information discovery* with disjunctive non-anticipativity (Lagrangean/branch-and-bound, K-adaptability), not probabilities; Zhan et al. (2017) linearise probability variables by binary expansion plus big-M rather than deriving Benders cuts; Bazotte et al. (2025) avoid cuts entirely by transforming to exogenous uncertainty.

### Cited Findings
- Pantuso & Hewitt problem (2): min_{x∈X} c^T x + Σ_{d∈D} 1_d(x) E_{P_d}[Q(x,ξ)], (X_d)_{d∈D} a finite partition of X; "the problem can be viewed as a type of disjunctive program"; "When the number of probability distributions is naturally finite, our model is exact ... ### Inferences
- The Pantuso–Hewitt cut is a *disjunctive* Benders cut: it is the ordinary L-shaped cut for distribution d, relaxed by U(1 − 1_d(x)); its weakness is that no information from distribution d transfers to x ∈ X_{d'} (the authors list "adapt the inequalities generated for one distribution to be valid for another" as open). For p_s(y) that varies smoothly (affine) in y, the partition-based model is only an approximation ("θ(x̄_d) for some x̄_d ∈ X_d").
- The information-discovery techniques (disjunctive NACs, Lagrangean decomposition of NACs) transfer to the probability case only through the same device: a binary that activates/deactivates constraints — exactly what the big-M term in the Pantuso–Hewitt cut does.
- "Bilinear Benders" in the sense of Zhang, Wang, Zeng & Hu (chance-constrained unit commitment, IEEE TPS 2017) was found only for chance constraints, not decision-dependent probabilities; no "bilinear Benders" paper by Zhan & Zheng was located.

### Gaps
- No Zhan–Zheng bilinear-Benders paper was found (search returned their MILP-reformulation GEP paper and the 2018 bilevel paper); Vayanos et al. and Apap & Grossmann full texts were not read (abstracts only).

## Key Question 8: When is the McCormick/Glover linearisation exact for sum-of-products of binary and continuous, and what makes the LP relaxation weak?

### Takeaway
For w = η z with z ∈ {0,1} and η ∈ [η_lo, η_hi], the four McCormick inequalities are the convex hull, hence exact at every integer z (Basciftci et al. (13)); for η z_1 z_2 with z binary the Meyer–Floudas eight-inequality system is the hull; the same holds for Glover's/Al-Khayyal–Falk big-M forms used by Pantuso & Hewitt and Pantuso. The LP relaxation is nevertheless weak because at fractional z the envelope allows w to be any value in [η_lo z, η_hi z] ∩ [η − η_hi(1−z), η − η_lo(1−z)], decoupling w from η; three sources state this weakness explicitly (Medal & Affar; Yu & Shen; Pantuso's >1000% extensive-form gaps) and Pantuso & Hewitt use the McCormick LP relaxation only as a *lower bound*.

### Cited Findings
- Exactness statements: "Because variable z is binary valued, we have an exact reformulation in (13)"; "Proposition 2. [27] Let 0 ≤ η_lo ≤ η_hi. Then M''_{(η_lo,η_hi)} = conv{(w,η,z_1,z_2) : w = ηz_1z_2, η ∈ [η_lo,η_hi], z_1,z_2 ∈ {0,1}}" (Meyer & Floudas 2004). — [Basciftci et al. §3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.05577)
- "When the variables in x are binary, the McCormick envelopes used to linearize products of variables are exact for binary solutions"; "Although linearizing these multilinear terms is easier if the x̃ is assumed to be a binary vector ..., this linearization still requires adding many additional constraints and auxiliary variables to iteratively form McCormick envelopes. While these envelopes are exact for binary solutions, the linear programming relaxation can be quite weak, as we found in our computational experimentation"; for continuous/integer allocation "the McCormick envelope would not be exact for the linear programming relaxation. Thus, a spatial branch-and-bound strategy would be necessary." — [Medal & Affar §1, §3, §7](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08403)
- "the computational time increases approximately linearly with respect to the support size K and the number T of stages, while it increases exponentially with respect to the numbers of facilities I and customer sites J, due to the existence of McCormick constraints." — [Yu & Shen §5.2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.12518v2)
- Extensive McCormick-linearised form of the facility problem: "For |I| ≥ 15, minimum gaps reached hundreds to over 1000%, and average gaps exceeded 10^3 %" within 1800 s; no feasible solution for |Z| = 10. — [Pantuso 2025 §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09816v1)
- Pantuso & Hewitt Proposition 13: Q(x) ≥ Σ_d Σ_s π_{sd} L^d(x,ξ_{sd}) "where the inequality holds because Q^d(x,ξ_{sd}) ≥ L^d(x,ξ_{sd})" with L^d the LP relaxation of the McCormick-linearised product — i.e., the relaxation is strictly a lower bound used only for warm-start cuts. — [arXiv 2506.12753 §3.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12753)
- MSP benchmark (sequential bilinear decomposition + McCormick, "For every bilinear term, we introduce an auxiliary variable, and ... four constraints") solves Det-Opt-Bin up to 8×8 grids (283 s at bA = 4, L = 4) but on Det-Opt only bA = 2 instances; SRA solves most within limits. — [Medal & Affar §6](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08403)
- Rahimian & Mehrotra on when McCormick is exact in DDRO: "[4, 74] used McCormick inequalities to obtain an exact reformulation of bilinear terms due to the multiplication of a binary and a continuous variable." — [DDRO pdf Remark 4](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/DDRO_RahimianMehrotra.pdf)
- Noyan et al. remark on big-M: "using tighter big-M bounds often leads to significantly improved computational performance" (constraint-specific M^{ij}_l = 2 max_t |ξ̂^i_tl − ξ̂^j_tl|, M_τ from data). — [Opt. Online 7591 §8.3.1](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/7591.pdf)

### Inferences
- Exactness is always obtained the same way: z binary, η bounded. The weakness is structural: with z fractional the LP can set w ≈ η_hi z (or η_lo z) independently of the actual η, so the objective sees a probability-weighted cost that is far from any convex combination of true scenario costs; tightness therefore depends entirely on the quality of the bound η_hi (Basciftci hard-code 100 for duals; Pantuso uses min{ξ_j, C_i}).
- When η itself is an LP value θ_s(y) = Q_s(y), the product term w_s = p_s(y)θ_s admits the obvious *lower* bound via the LP relaxation of Q_s, and the Pantuso–Hewitt Prop. 13 shows this is the best one gets from McCormick without further structure.

### Gaps
- No paper reports measured root-node LP gaps for the McCormick-linearised decision-dependent facility model; the "weak relaxation" claims are qualitative except for Pantuso's end-of-run gaps.

## Key Question 9: Does any paper use a change of variables (w_s = p_s(y)·θ_s, z_{s,k} = y_k θ_s, …) that yields a tighter relaxation than naive McCormick?

### Takeaway
Three devices in the literature are genuinely different from naive McCormick: (1) Noyan et al.'s RLT convex hull for z = π β^T when β is one-hot and π is a probability vector (equalities 1^T z = β^T, z1 = π replace all McCormick inequalities and are the hull); (2) distribution shaping / BDD chains, which write p(y) itself through linear inequalities on auxiliary probability vectors (exact, but the product with a y-dependent recourse remains bilinear); (3) Bazotte et al.'s random-variable transformation and Dupačová's push-in, which move p(y) into the cost so that the product never appears. For DRO, Lagrangian duality (Rahimian–Mehrotra, Basciftci) moves the bilinearity from p·Q to dual·ϑ(y), which is McCormick-exact for binary y and avoids scenario recourse variables when the inner LP has a closed form. No paper reports a relaxation for w_s = p_s(y)θ_s with θ_s a general LP value that is tighter than McCormick; the decomposition papers instead avoid the product by cuts.

### Cited Findings
- RLT hull: "conv(T) = {(z,β,π_L) ∈ [0,1]^{n×n}×[0,1]^n×[0,1]^n : 1^T z = β^T, z1 = π_L, 1^T π_L = 1}. Incorporating the arising valid inequalities into an MILP can dramatically improve computational performance" (pooling-problem result of Gupte et al. 2017 / Sherali et al. 1998); for scheduling "the reformulation-linearization technique consistently and dramatically improves computational performance. Notably, several large problem instances which could not be solved within the time limit using the basic comonotone formulation CCM are rendered tractable". — [Noyan et al. Appendix A.3, §8.3.2](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/7591.pdf)
- Alternative without any product: CVaR disjunction (56): μ ≤ G^j + (1/(1−κ)) Σ_i π^i_L [G^i − G^j]_+ ∀j, μ ≥ same − (1 − β^j)M — products π·G with G constant are linear; only the selector β is binary. — ibid. (56)
- Distribution shaping as a change of variables for p(y): auxiliary π_k ∈ P_k(π_{k−1}, x_k), "successive polyhedral characterization of probability measures as a function of decisions"; exact MIP only if "the cost in each scenario is a constant"; otherwise "mixed-integer bi-linear". — [Laumanns et al. §3](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/bitstreams/5cd4f601-25ad-46cc-a822-2d281505e874/download)
- Push-in / change of measure: F(x) = ∫ f(x,ω)p(x,ω)Q(dω), "we recover the common form ... with a decision-independent probability distribution Q. However, it is obtained at the cost of losing convenient properties of the original random objective function". — [Dupačová 2006 §1](https://www2.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~kopa/papers/MME06sty.pdf)
- Random-variable transformation: ξ = t(x,ϑ), inversion ξ_x = F^{−1}(x;·)(ϑ); "for some classical endogenous distributions, the proposed method yields mixed-integer linear or convex programs with exogenous uncertainty"; convolution (binomial as sum of Bernoulli transforms) "gives linear constraints" whereas inversion "gives convex, potentially nonlinear constraints". — [Bazotte et al. arXiv 2402.15486v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15486v4)
- DRO dualisation as change of variables: Basciftci's (10)/(16) contain no p_s variables at all — the probability is eliminated and y multiplies only duals (δ, γ) and itself; Rahimian–Mehrotra (8): min λ^T ϑ(x) + Σ_ω G_ω(x,λ). — [Basciftci Thm 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.05577); [DDRO pdf Thm 1](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/DDRO_RahimianMehrotra.pdf)
- Jensen-based bounds on the product in SP (Medal & Affar): Θ(ξ̄(x)) ≤ E[Θ(ξ)|x] ≤ Θ̃(ξ̄(x)) with the penalised concave reformulation Θ̃(ξ) = min{q^T y + ξ^T M y : Wy = b, y ≥ 0} (M diagonal of dual bounds) — the mean-value problem in the *decision-dependent mean* ξ̄(x) = E[ξ|x] replaces Σ_ξ P[ξ|x]Θ(ξ); refinement over a partition tree with node probabilities P[η|x] = Π_{i∈F(η)} Σ_ℓ f_i(κ_iη;ℓ) x_iℓ. — [Medal & Affar §4](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08403)
- Pantuso's Jensen VI for the facility LP (Q concave in ξ): μ ≤ Σ_i Σ_j R_ij min{C_i, max_d E_{P_d}[ξ_j]} x_i — an upper bound on the product term linear in x. — [Pantuso 2025 Prop. 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09816v1)
- Distribution-independent cut from one scenario: μ ≥ (ρ^v)^T(ĥ − T̂x) with ξ̂ = min_d E_{P_d}[ξ] (Prop. 15–16). — [Pantuso & Hewitt §3.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12753)

### Inferences
- The one-hot/simplex hull (E1) suggests that if the facility model is written with a one-hot distribution selector δ (Σ_d δ_d = 1) and the "cost" vector is itself a convex-combination variable, RLT equalities Σ_d z_{sd} = π_s-type and Σ_s z_{sd} = δ_d could replace McCormick; but with θ_s = Q_s(y) unconstrained in sign/scale this hull result does not apply, and the Pantuso extensive form (which has exactly this one-hot structure) still shows >1000% gaps with McCormick.
- The DRO literature's success (Basciftci) rests on two structural facts absent from the plain SP: (i) duality eliminates p; (ii) the inner LP has a closed form. In the SP setting with p_s(y) affine in y and Q_s an LP, the product p_s(y)·Q_s(y) is a sum of terms y_k·Q_s(y); writing z_{s,k} = y_k θ_s with θ_s ≥ ρ^T(h_s − T_s y) for all dual extreme points ρ (Benders) is the only exact linear representation found, and its relaxation is the McCormick one.

### Gaps
- No source was found that proposes or tests w_s = p_s(y)θ_s or z_{s,k} = y_kθ_s with a strengthened (e.g., RLT/perspective) relaxation for an LP-valued θ_s; this appears to be open in the OR literature as of October 2026.
- Hewitt & Pantuso (IJOC 2025, "Production planning under demand and endogenous supply uncertainty") and Lejeune, Dehghanian & Ma (EJOR 2024, price-responsive decision-dependent unit commitment) are cited by Pantuso & Hewitt as applications but were not read; they may contain problem-specific linearisations.
