# Bound-free lifted cuts beat McCormick on endogenous demand

Of every reformulation device catalogued across operations research, probability and simulation, statistical physics and variational inference, reinforcement learning, global optimization, economics and information economics, exactly one cross-field combination both survived independent verification and produced a relaxation tighter than McCormick for the bilinear objective Σ_s p_s(y) Q_s(y): **multiply each Benders cut of Q_s by the nonnegative literal factors (1, y_j, 1−y_j) of an affine p_s(y), lift the products into the Boolean-quadric space, and give each factor its own epigraph variable.** The researchers proved this "per-factor" formulation is the Balas disjunctive hull of each single product y_j·Q_s(y) and therefore dominates the Basciftci-type McCormick MILP for every valid bound U while needing no bound on Q_s at all; on the three toy instances it closed the root gap to **1.96 / 1.54 / 0.97 %** against McCormick's best **1.98 / 1.77 / 1.96 %**, and its explicit level-1 RLT "two-copy" LP was **integral (0.00 % gap) on all three instances**. The three other derived candidates are all mathematically correct and exact at binary y, but their relaxations are far weaker than naive McCormick: the Gibbs/KL exponential-cone program (statistical physics) shows **33–42 %** gaps against **3.6–7.1 %**, with Slater's condition failing at every binary point; Charnes–Cooper on the whole two-stage program (economics) shows **6.8–7.5 %** against **2.8–4.9 %**; and the Hammer–Rubin weak-convexity shift (RL/performative prediction) shows **38–53 %**. The published literature confirms the reader's prior: no paper reports a relaxation tighter than McCormick for a generic binary × LP-value product ([Pantuso & Hewitt 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12753)), every exact method rides one of five structural assumptions on p_s(y), and the one published convex-hull result requires a one-hot binary times a simplex vector ([Noyan, Rudolf & Lejeune](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/7591.pdf)). The caveat that governs everything below is scale: all gap numbers come from n = 3 sites, where rankings transfer but magnitudes do not, and whether the RLT-1 LP stays integral for larger n is untested. For a CPU-parallel attack, the winning formulation is the friendliest one: its separation problem is one LP per (scenario, factor) pair, all independent, and its extended LP decomposes by (s, j) blocks.

## Every published exact method rides one of five structures, none tighter than McCormick

The OR literature on decision-dependent probabilities (Li and Grossmann's "Type 1", which they note "has not been widely studied" ([Li & Grossmann 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/chemical-engineering/articles/10.3389/fceng.2020.622241/pdf))) removes the product p_s(y)·Q_s(y) in exactly five ways, and recognizing which structure a paper exploits is the fastest way to see why none of them generalizes. Structure A is *selection*: p(y) is piecewise constant over a finite partition of the feasible set, so a one-hot indicator δ_d times a bounded recourse variable is a binary × continuous product and McCormick or big-M is exact at integer points. This is the Pantuso–Hewitt extensive form and its L-shaped descendant, where the optimality cut μ ≥ Σ_s π_{sd} ρ_s^T(h_{sd} − T_{sd}x) − U(1 − 1_d(x)) is the ordinary Benders cut relaxed by a big-M term that switches it off unless the incumbent enforces distribution d ([Pantuso & Hewitt 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12753)). The facility-location instantiation reaches **2^17 = 131,072 distributions** (74 of 84 instances under 0.5 % gap, average 27,139 s) because complexity scales with distributions *visited*, roughly one cut each, not with |D|; the McCormick-linearised extensive form, by contrast, found **no feasible solution for |Z| = 10** and showed gaps of "hundreds to over 1000 %" for 15 or more facilities ([Pantuso 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09816v1)). Structure B is *affinity*: p_s(y), or the moments that define an ambiguity set, are affine in y, so after LP-dualising the inner problem the only products are binary × dual, which McCormick linearises exactly (Basciftci's set M′ with "Because variable z is binary valued, we have an exact reformulation") and Meyer–Floudas hull for the trilinear case ([Basciftci, Ahmed & Shen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.05577)). The decisive trick there is not McCormick but a closed-form inner LP: because the per-customer recourse decomposes with capacities C_i y_i, the value is a maximum of affine functions of y, so no scenario recourse variables appear and the model is a compact MILP that reaches only **|I| = 10, |J| = 20, K = 100 in 111 s**; Yu and Shen report for the same construction that time "increases exponentially with respect to the numbers of facilities I and customer sites J, due to the existence of McCormick constraints" ([Yu & Shen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.12518v2)). Rahimian and Mehrotra show the same Lagrangian move for polyhedral decision-dependent ambiguity sets, where the bilinearity migrates entirely to the master term λ^T ϑ(x) and the DEF solved by a nonconvex solver shows gaps of "on average between 8.64–28.58 %" versus 0 % for their decomposition ([Rahimian & Mehrotra 2024](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/DDRO_RahimianMehrotra.pdf)).

Structure C is *independence*: p(y) is a product of per-component Bernoulli factors, so Bayes-rule rescaling between neighbouring decisions gives Laumanns' linear "distribution shaping" chain, an exact MIP with n binaries but **2^n·n continuous variables**, and, as the authors state, "if f(ξ) for instance was a recourse function of a stochastic linear program, then we would obtain a mixed-integer bi-linear program instead" ([Laumanns, Prestwich & Kawas 2014](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/bitstreams/5cd4f601-25ad-46cc-a822-2d281505e874/download)). The BDD compression of Haus, Michini and Laumanns cuts the Istanbul MIP to **1466 × 454, solved in 1 s**, but inherits the same caveat and is exponential in general ([Haus, Michini & Laumanns](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.04055)). Structure D is *push-in*: Dupačová's change of measure F(x) = ∫ f(x,ω) p(x,ω) Q(dω) makes the measure exogenous "at the cost of losing convenient properties of the original random objective function" ([Dupačová 2006](https://www2.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~kopa/papers/MME06sty.pdf)), and Bazotte, Carvalho and Vidal's random-variable transformation ξ = F^{-1}(x;·)(ϑ) yields convex continuous relaxations when the inverse CDF is convex or concave in x and MILPs otherwise ([Bazotte et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15486v4)). Structure E is *never forming the product*: the Pantuso–Hewitt lazy cut above. The single genuinely different relaxation result is Noyan et al.'s RLT hull: for z = π β^T with β one-hot and π on the simplex, conv(T) is described by the equalities 1^T z = β^T, z1 = π, 1^T π = 1 alone, with no McCormick inequalities, and they report that RLT "consistently and dramatically improves computational performance" ([Noyan, Rudolf & Lejeune](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/7591.pdf)). It does not extend to z_{s,k} = y_k θ_s with θ_s an unconstrained LP value, and Pantuso's extensive form, which has exactly the one-hot structure, still shows the >1000 % gaps. Hellemo, Barton and Tomasgard's taxonomy (affine distortion, convex combination, parameters as first-stage decisions) solved everything with BARON and selective branching and never claimed an MILP ([Hellemo et al. 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10287-018-0330-0)); the linearised baseline later attributed to them is Pantuso and Hewitt's own McCormick.

The reason McCormick is weak is structural and worth stating once, because every candidate below is an attempt to escape it. For w = η z with z ∈ {0,1} and η ∈ [L, U], the four inequalities are the convex hull over the box, so no relaxation in the same (η, z) space can be tighter for an isolated product; but at z = 1/2 the envelope lets w sit anywhere in a band of width (U − L)/2, decoupled from η, and when η is a Benders epigraph variable U must be a global a-priori bound on recourse cost ([Medal & Affar](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08403); [Basciftci et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.05577)). For a bilinear *function* rather than a term the ratio between the McCormick gap and the true envelope gap "cannot be bounded by a constant independent of n" ([Boland et al. 2017](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10107-016-1031-5)), and for separable bilinear programs the Shor SDP relaxation "provides no benefit over the McCormick relaxation" ([Gu, Dey & Richard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00345)). Tightening must therefore come from either a better U or from avoiding U altogether by lifting, which is what the winner does.

Three patterns in this table matter for what follows. First, the fields that do obtain convexity do so by making the *payoff* constant (MNL capture, assortment, persuasion), by making the *decision* the distribution itself (occupation measures, rational inattention, Gallego–van Ryzin intensity), or by making the *energy equal the cost* (deterministic annealing). None of these holds when the payoff is an LP value that depends on y through capacities, and the researchers found no source establishing convexity, submodularity, or valid OA cuts for the product of a decision-dependent probability and a value function; the bilevel survey's verdict that decision-dependent distributions are "another completely open field of research" ([Beck, Ljubić & Schmidt](https://opus4.kobv.de/opus4-trr154/files/491/bilevel-under-uncertainty-survey-preprint.pdf)) is the same verdict in every field. Second, the devices that *do* transfer all reduce to lifting a product of a 0/1 quantity and a continuous one into an extended space and then choosing a relaxation of that lift; the multilinear polytope theory says exactly when the cheap relaxation is the hull (standard linearisation iff the monomial hypergraph is Berge-acyclic ([Del Pia & Khajavirad](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04805v1))), which for affine p is Padberg's acyclic-graph condition and for a full product-form tilt over all sites is 2^n, that is, scenario enumeration. Third, the one device that yields convexity outright is a modelling choice rather than an algebraic identity: representing the demand response as a mean shift d_s(y) = d_s^0 + A y on a fixed probability space (Dupačová's push-in, Miller et al.'s location family) makes the objective an ordinary two-stage program convex in the relaxation, because the LP value is convex in its right-hand side composed with an affine map; reweighting a fixed support and shifting its atoms can match the same mean function but are different distributions, and only the shift preserves convexity (derived; push-in statement in [Dupačová](https://www2.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~kopa/papers/MME06sty.pdf), location-family convexity in [Miller et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)). The reader should treat that as the first question to settle with the domain: if only the mean, or mean and variance, of demand is meant to respond to openings, the Basciftci moment model or the shift model avoids the bilinear weights entirely.

## Verification: one of four derived candidates beats McCormick, three lose badly

The verification note tested the four candidates on one capacitated facility-location recourse (n = 3 sites, m = 4 customers, unmet-demand penalty, relatively complete recourse) under three probability models (affine, Gibbs, logit), on three instances each (S = 4 and 8 with one demand seed, S = 6 with another), with every cut loop run to convergence so that each reported bound is the bound of the formulation with the complete Benders cut family. Exact optima by enumeration were 54.20 / 56.10 / 61.07 (affine), 56.30 / 58.22 / 64.33 (Gibbs), 53.84 / 55.75 / 60.57 (logit), with y* = (1, 0, 1) in all nine cases (derived). One preliminary finding reshapes the baseline itself: the naive McCormick formulation "MC-P", which linearises ω_s = p_s(y)·θ_s with both p_s and θ_s continuous, is **not an exact reformulation at binary y**. With y fixed to a binary point it underestimates F(y) by up to **12.3 (affine), 13.9 (Gibbs) and 7.0 (logit)**, because p_s(y) sits strictly inside its bounds while θ_s is continuous; only the Basciftci-type "MC-B" on products y_j θ_s (binary × continuous) is exact, to 1.4·10^{-14} (derived). This is the structural point the global-optimization note makes once and the verification confirms numerically: at a binary y, p_s(y) is a fractional number, so exactness requires expanding p_s into monomials that are themselves 0/1 before linearising, which is what Medal and Affar's sequential decomposition does ([Medal & Affar](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08403)). All comparisons below use MC-B with the tightest valid bounds, which favours McCormick as much as possible.

Candidate 1 is the physics contribution. Its identity is the Gibbs optimality gap KL(r‖p(y)) = KL(r‖q) + β Σ_s r_s E_s(y) + log Z(y) ≥ 0, with equality iff r = p(y); with affine energy, the bilinear term r_s y_j is the only non-convexity, McCormick on w_{sj} = r_s y_j removes it exactly at binary y, and scaling the recourse by r_s (valid by positive homogeneity of the LP value in its right-hand side, with the r_s = 0 case handled by dual feasibility) produces a single-level program whose continuous relaxation is a convex exponential-cone program (derived, re-proved in verification; DV identity in [arXiv 2605.27253](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.27253)). Two things go wrong. The constraint G ≤ 0 is the optimal-value reformulation of a convex lower-level problem whose minimum is exactly 0, so no strictly feasible point exists at any binary y, the classical MFCQ failure of value-function reformulations ([Ye & Zhu 1995](https://doi.org/10.1080/02331939508844060), cited from memory in the notes and flagged for checking); CLARABEL returned `optimal_inaccurate` at every binary point on two of three instances with one-signed optimistic errors up to 0.134, and the Slater-restoring slack G ≤ 10^{-3} pushed errors to −2.54, 4 % of F, consistent with the Pinsker bound (max Q − min Q)√(ε/2) (derived). The remedy is to substitute r = p(y) and solve the plain LP at any node where all y are fixed and to use the KL program only at fractional nodes, where Slater holds. The deeper failure is tightness: at the strengthened relaxation optimum of the S4 instance the relaxation placed r = (0.331, 0.253, 0.216, 0.201) on the scenarios while p(y) = (0.133, 0.192, 0.277, 0.398), that is, most mass on the cheapest scenario, because the McCormick lower bound max(0, r_s + y_j − 1) is zero whenever y_j ≤ 1 − r_s, which is almost always the case for S ≥ 2 since r_s ≤ 1/2. The lift w = r ⊗ y has one factor confined to [0, 1/2], exactly where the McCormick box is loose, and the KL constraint then carries almost no information about y (derived). The "perspective half" of the formulation, the scaled recourse with no bound on Q, is fine; the probability half is what fails. Benders cuts transfer to the scaled recourse value-preservingly (same dual polyhedron, same bound 37.60 on S4 in 6 iterations), but the master is a MICP, so OA or nonlinear branch-and-bound is required.

Candidate 2 is the global-optimization contribution and the only winner. Validity is elementary: since p_s(y) ≥ 0 and Q_s(y) ≥ α_k^T d_s − g_k^T y for every dual vertex k, multiplying through gives p_s(y)Q_s(y) ≥ p_s(y)·cut_k(y) with equality for the optimal vertex, and in the lifted (y, Y) space with Y_{jl} = y_j y_l the right-hand side is linear, so cuts generated anywhere, fractional or binary, are globally valid; the standard linearisation forces Y = yy^T at binary points, the optimal vertex closes the gap, and finitely many vertices give finite convergence (derived; verified with value error 2.8·10^{-14} at all eight binary points and Benders from an empty master converging in 4 iterations). The tightness claim as originally stated was false, and the counterexample is instructive: on S4 the whole-p lift gave 51.49 (4.99 %) against MC-B's 53.12 (1.98 %), while on S6 it gave 1.83 % against 1.96 %, so neither dominates. The mechanism is that p_s(y) has mixed-sign coefficients, high-demand scenarios carry negative coefficients on Y in the cut and push Y to its McCormick upper bound, and a single max_k is applied to the whole mixed-sign combination, whereas MC-B keeps both the lower and upper envelopes of each product y_j θ_s, information the "multiply the whole cut" scheme discards (derived). The repair splits p_s(y) into its posiform b′_s + Σ_{a_{sj}>0} a_{sj} y_j + Σ_{a_{sj}<0} |a_{sj}|(1 − y_j), with b′_s = min over binary y of p_s(y) ≥ 0, and multiplies each cut by each nonnegative literal factor with its own epigraph: η^0_s ≥ cut_k(y), η^+_{sj} ≥ α_k^T d_s y_j − g_k^T Y_j, η^-_{sj} ≥ α_k^T d_s(1 − y_j) − g_k^T(y − Y_j). The dominance proof is a Balas disjunction: for fixed (s, j), the set {(y_j, y, v): y_j ∈ {0,1}, v ≥ y_j Q_s(y)} is the union of {y_j = 0, Y_j = 0, v ≥ 0} and {y_j = 1, Y_j = y, v ≥ Q_s(y)}, whose disjunctive hull with multiplier y_j is exactly the McCormick inequalities for Y_j = y_j y plus the per-factor cuts v ≥ max_k(α_k^T d_s y_j − g_k^T Y_j), that is, the Günlük–Linderoth perspective hull of a piecewise-linear function ([Günlük & Linderoth](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2009/09/2393.pdf)); MC-B's four inequalities are valid for the same two polyhedra and hence implied by their hull, for every valid U, with no bound at all (derived). The cost is S(1 + 2n) epigraph variables (only S(1 + nnz(a_s)) needed) and SK(1 + 2n) cuts. Keeping the recourse variables explicit and adding the two product copies z_{sj} = y_j x_s with A z_{sj} = y_j d_s, B z_{sj} ≤ U Y_j and the complementary copy for (1 − y_j), the Adams–Sherali level-1 construction ([Adams & Sherali](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/oropre/v38y1990i2p217-226.html)), dominates the per-factor cuts by weak duality (the cut version does not force one common x_s) and relaxed directly to y = (1, 0, 1) on all three affine instances (derived). The researchers are explicit that n = 3 integrality must not be extrapolated, since the standard linearisation of Y is not the Boolean quadric polytope for n ≥ 3 in general ([Padberg](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01589101)); what it does show is that the bound-free RLT family is where the strength lives, and that projecting cuts scenario-wise loses part of it, the same loss that appears in Candidate 3 (42.3 versus 50.2) and that Bodur et al. document for cut-and-project in exogenous stochastic integer programs ([Bodur et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/orijoc/v29y2017i1p77-91.html)).

Candidate 3 is the economics contribution, Charnes–Cooper applied to the whole two-stage program rather than to a constant-payoff ratio ([Charnes & Cooper](https://doi.org/10.1002/nav.3800090303); conic assortment in [Şen, Atamtürk & Kaminsky](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.09040)). Everything checks: with p_s(y) = N_s(y)/D(y), w = 1/D(y), u_j = y_j w, the normalisation D_0 w + Σ_j D_j u_j = 1 is linear, positive homogeneity gives w Q_s(y) = Q̃_s(w, wy), and the products N_s(y) x̃_s are exactly linearisable either by McCormick (given bounds on x̃) or by a bound-free RLT variant; both are exact to 2.8·10^{-14} and the MILP optimum equals enumeration (derived). The relaxation is weak for a reason that generalises: (w, u) decouple from y. At the S4 RLT optimum, y = (0.351, 0.186, 0.351) but w = 0.1001 while 1/D(y) = 0.1375, and u/w = (0.57, 0.46, 0.57), so the scaled recourse operates "more open" than y while the numerators stay at the fractional y; only the normalisation and McCormick boxes link (w, u) to y, and with w ranging over [0.066, 0.25] those boxes are loose. The McCormick-on-x̃ variant, where the bound x̄_s = w_max d_s acts as a big-M on Q, lands at 54–55 %. A tighter hull of {(w, u, y): w = 1/D(y), u = yw, y binary} is the known 0-1 hyperbolic-programming question, treated by convex extensions in Ahmed's thesis ([Ahmed 2000](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/88389)), which the researchers could not access.

Candidate 4 is the RL/performative-prediction contribution. Miller et al.'s Theorem 3.1 gives λ-convexity with λ = γ − 2εβ under mixture dominance ([Miller et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)); affine p satisfies mixture dominance with equality (the S-scenario version of their Bernoulli(a^T θ + b) remark, derived), but γ = 0 for a piecewise-linear recourse, so F is only (2εβ)-weakly convex, and the researchers proposed the Hammer–Rubin diagonal shift with ρ = 2Σ_s ‖a_s‖ L_s ([Hammer & Rubin](http://www.numdam.org/item/M2AN_1970__4_3_67_0/)). The modulus is correct, including the nonsmooth case: on any line, each linear piece of p_s Q_s has second derivative ≥ −2‖a_s‖L_s, and at a kink of Q_s the derivative jumps by p_s(t)(m_2 − m_1) ≥ 0, so kinks are convex and need no extra modulus (derived; 0.0 midpoint-convexity violation on 1500 random pairs). The bound is useless by construction: the shift equals −ρn/8 at the centre of the box, **32–38 against optimal values of 54–61**, and the measured relaxation values 25.54 / 32.67 / 37.70 (52.9 / 41.8 / 38.3 %) sit at interior points. The sampled modulus (31.4 on S4) shows ρ is at least 3× conservative, but even the invalid sampled value gives 6–13 %, and a Billionnet–Elloumi best-diagonal certificate would need an SDP for a non-quadratic F ([Billionnet & Elloumi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-005-0637-9)). A counterexample kills the extension to multilinear p: with p_s = y_1 y_2 and Q_s = M constant, the claimed modulus is 0 but the true modulus is M, so for multilinear p the modulus scales with the recourse cost *level*, not its Lipschitz constant (derived). Of the combinations, Candidate 1 + Benders is value-preserving; Candidates 2 + 3 in the monomial × 1/Z space give an exact bound-free MILP for Gibbs or logit p at 10.8–17.2 % (between naive McCormick and Candidate 1, weaker for the same mixed-sign reason as whole-p Candidate 2, and the per-factor repair there is the full Lagrange expansion, that is, enumeration); and adding Candidate 1's KL constraint, rewritten linearly in the lifted monomial variables, to that master changed the bound by nothing to four decimals on all instances, because the normalisation and monomial lift already carry everything the KL identity carries (derived).

## Ranking the cross-field combinations that survived

The ranking below weighs verified tightness, generality of the requirement on p_s(y), cost, and how much of the structure a CPU-parallel implementation can exploit. "New" means the researchers found no source stating the combination; "verified" means the verification note proved and numerically checked it.

**First: per-factor RLT-lifted Benders cuts, and their extended-space parent.** This fuses Adams–Sherali level-1 RLT and Günlük–Linderoth perspective hulls from global optimization with Benders cut generation and Bodur et al.'s cut-and-project from stochastic programming, applied to a decision-dependent weight. It is new (no source states the convex hull of {(y, w): w ≥ y_k Q(y)} for piecewise-linear convex Q; the Balas-hull derivation is the researchers' own), it is verified, and it is the only formulation that provably dominates the exact McCormick baseline while requiring no bound on Q_s. Requirement: p_s(y) ≥ 0 affine in y, or multilinear with a nonnegative literal-monomial representation of bounded degree whose monomial hypergraph is acyclic enough for a polynomial hull (standard linearisation exact iff Berge-acyclic; β-acyclic extended formulation of size (r−1)|V| + |E| variables ([Del Pia & Khajavirad](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04805v1))). What survives: linearity, exactness at binary y, an LP relaxation whose quality is governed by the Boolean-quadric relaxation of Y rather than by any recourse bound. Parallel structure is the best of any candidate: separation of the most violated lifted cut is one LP per (scenario, factor) with lifted right-hand side p_s(y)d_s and capacity U ỹ_s(y, Y), all S(1 + 2n) of them independent; the extended RLT-1 LP decomposes into (s, j) blocks of size 2m_x coupled only through y and Y, which is Dantzig–Wolfe or ADMM territory; and the master is a plain MILP whose branch-and-bound nodes parallelise. The known weakness is that scenario-wise projected cuts lose the two-copy coupling (verified on Candidate 3 at 21–22 % versus 6.8–7.5 %), so recovering the full RLT-1 bound by cuts requires separating on the joint two-copy polyhedron per (s, j), an LP with 2m_x variables, which is still independent across (s, j) and therefore still parallel. Padberg triangle inequalities changed nothing at n = 3 but are the natural next strengthening at larger n.

**Second: mean-shift (push-in) modelling, if the domain permits it.** Not a reformulation of the stated objective but the one route to genuine convexity: d_s(y) = d_s^0 + Ay with fixed weights gives an ordinary two-stage MILP with technology matrix T − A, convex in the relaxation, fully scenario-parallel under standard Benders. Requirement: that the demand response be representable as a location family rather than a reweighting of a fixed support. It is published in pieces ([Dupačová](https://www2.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~kopa/papers/MME06sty.pdf); [Miller et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08570)) and the LP-RHS convexity argument is derived; its promise is bounded only by modelling fidelity.

**Third: the Gibbs/KL exponential-cone MICP, used at fractional nodes only.** New and verified-exact, with a convex relaxation; weak in its current McCormick lift and numerically fragile at binary points. Its promise is as the only exact convex object for a *normalised, nonnegative* probability model (Gibbs with affine energy), where affine p cannot be guaranteed nonnegative without case analysis; and because, as the verification shows, Candidate 1 is the "r-variable version" of Candidate 2 (with Benders cuts its master is linear in (η, r, w) and for affine p reduces to Candidate 2 after r = p(y)), any hull strengthening of {(y, r, r ⊗ y): y binary, r ∈ Δ} beyond Σ_s w_{sj} = y_j, which the researchers identify as "the whole problem", would lift it directly. Parallel structure: S exponential cones and scenario-parallel cut generation; the MICP master is the serial bottleneck.

**Fourth: Charnes–Cooper on the whole program plus lifted cuts, for logit and Gibbs probabilities.** New, verified-exact, bound-free, and the only exact linear route when p is a ratio with a common denominator; weaker than naive McCormick on the toys, and its gap is intrinsic to the decoupling of w from y unless the 0-1 hyperbolic hull question is answered. Parallel structure as for the first candidate.

**Fifth: DC sandwich with CCCP as a primal heuristic and homotopy.** The bounds (1/t)[log Z_t − log Z] are explicit difference-of-convex functions with O(t·Var Q) gap, CCCP subproblems are exponential-cone programs, and annealing in t or β is the transferable content of deterministic annealing ([Rose 1998](https://www.ece.ucsb.edu/publications/rose/pubs/pub42-Proc11-98.pdf)); the alternative "freeze p at the last y, solve an exogenous SMIP" fixed point is Hellemo's selective-branching observation and the performative-prediction RRM map, cheap and scenario-parallel per step but with no monotonicity guarantee and possible cycling ([Drusvyatskiy & Xiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11173)). Both are heuristics and belong inside a branch-and-bound that gets its bounds from the first candidate.

**Sixth: posterior-based LP for the variance-reducing variant.** If the decision is a sampling or probing plan rather than a mean-shifting opening, the rational-inattention/persuasion lift makes the objective, Bayes plausibility and a posterior-separable cost all linear in the distribution over posteriors, and the value is a concavification ([Matějka & McKay](https://home.cerge-ei.cz/matejka/logit_FM_AM.pdf); [Gentzkow & Kamenica](http://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2014/retrieve.php?pdfid=417)); for a sampling-count cost it is a relaxation whose value is concave in the information budget ([Whitmeyer](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01190v1)). It does not apply to the mean-shift variant at all, which is why it ranks low for the stated problem.

**Dead ends, verified or sourced.** Hammer–Rubin convexification (verified useless and affine-only); SDP on the lifted products (no benefit over McCormick ([Gu, Dey & Richard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00345))); occupation-measure and menu-of-distributions LPs (exact but 2^n columns, their pricing problem being the original problem); submodular and adaptive-submodular guarantees (only in the activation model where Q is constant in y); MNL concavity, conic assortment, intensity control and OA cuts (all need a constant payoff); rectangular decomposition (needs site-separable recourse); gradient domination (no performance-difference lemma analogue); and importance sampling or the stochastic counterpart on a finite scenario set, which "buys nothing computationally" because the sample is the whole support (derived; [Shapiro](https://informs-sim.org/wsc01papers/056.PDF)).

## Open questions, source gaps, and where CPU parallelism lives

The verified result is strong but narrow, and the open questions are mostly about its reach. The first is scale: every gap number is for n = 3, where LP relaxations of good formulations are nearly integral, so the researchers' own statement is that rankings, not magnitudes, transfer; the relative advantage of RLT-type lifts over McCormick should grow with n, since McCormick's per-product error is O(U − L)/2 at y_j = 1/2 while the hull is bound-free, but this was not measured, and the RLT-1 integrality should be expected to break for larger n. The second is the hull itself: an exact convex description of {(r, w, y): w = r ⊗ y, y binary, r = p(y)}, or of the 0-1 hyperbolic set behind Candidate 3, is identified as the whole problem, and the single-coordinate hull {w ≥ 0, r − w ≥ 0, Σ_s w_s = y_j, Σ_s r_s = 1} (derived) has no known polynomial-size extension to all coordinates simultaneously. The third is degree: the posiform repair for multilinear p needs a nonnegative literal-monomial representation, which need not exist degree-preservingly, and the always-available Lagrange expansion has 2^n terms; no instance with a mixed-sign degree-2 p was run, so the size-versus-tightness trade-off of the repair at degree 2 is unmeasured, and the flower and running-intersection relaxations that dominate every recursive McCormick for bounded degree ([Khajavirad](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13034)) were not tried. The fourth is the exact weak-convexity modulus of F, bracketed only in [31.4, 101] on S4, which matters if anyone revisits convexification with an SDP-type certificate.

Source gaps are concentrated and should be closed before publication. Hellemo, Barton and Tomasgard's full text could not be retrieved from Springer or either institutional mirror, so their exact affine-distortion and convex-combination equations are reconstructed from the abstract and from descriptions in Pantuso & Hewitt and Noyan et al.; Ahmed's 2000 thesis chapter on 0-1 hyperbolic programs and convex extensions is restricted to UIUC and known only through its abstract and citations; the Del Pia–Khajavirad "iff Berge-acyclic" sentence was truncated in the snippet and should be confirmed in the full report; the Ye–Zhu MFCQ-failure reference and the Hammer–Rubin, Billionnet–Elloumi, Charnes–Cooper and Padberg citations were made from memory rather than re-fetched; Glover (1975), Frangioni–Gentile perspective cuts, Sherali–Adams' original papers, Puterman's LP chapter, Wiesemann–Kuhn–Rustem's original text, Kamenica–Gentzkow 2011 and Bergemann–Morris's LP form of information design were all read through secondary restatements; and the arXiv identifier 2609.37489 ("endogenous demand learning") supplied to the experimental-design researcher returned nothing in any search, so the variance-reducing variant was analysed against Ma et al.'s probing model ([Ma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10669)) instead. No paper was found that reports root-node LP gaps for the McCormick-linearised decision-dependent facility model, that compares RLT level-1 against McCormick on such programs, that applies multilinear-polytope results to a probability times a piecewise-linear value, or that evaluates mixture dominance or weak convexity for finite-scenario affine-probability stochastic programs; those absences are part of the evidence that the first-ranked combination is new.

For the reader's CPU-only plan, the exploitable parallelism is layered and almost entirely favourable to the winning formulation. At the outermost layer, branch-and-bound nodes of the per-factor master MILP are independent, and at any node where all y are fixed the problem collapses to S independent recourse LPs (this is also the remedy for Candidate 1's Slater failure). At the cut layer, separation is one small LP per (scenario, literal factor), S(1 + 2n) of them per round, with the dual box 0 ≤ β ≤ penalty giving valid truncated cuts when the lifted capacity has negative entries; these are embarrassingly parallel and the master-to-subproblem communication is a single (y, Y) vector. At the extended-formulation layer, the RLT-1 LP has (s, j) blocks of 2m_x variables coupled only through (y, Y), which is the structure that Dantzig–Wolfe pricing or consensus ADMM exploit across cores, and recovering its full bound by cuts requires separating on the joint two-copy polyhedron per (s, j), again independent across pairs. For the Pantuso–Hewitt enumerated-distribution route, which remains the published state of the art when p depends on y only through a few zone indicators, the parallelism is over the |S_d| scenario LPs of the single enforced distribution per iteration ([Pantuso 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09816v1)). For the physics candidate, the S relative-entropy cones and the scenario-parallel cut generation are parallel but the MICP master is not; for the DC/CCCP and RRM heuristics the outer loop is serial and each inner step is a fully scenario-parallel convex or exogenous problem. The one place where parallelism buys exactness outright is small n: the measure-valued and menu formulations are honest enumeration, and 2^n configurations each needing S LPs is trivially parallel up to roughly n = 20, which is also the scale at which Pantuso's decomposition stops solving to optimality.

## Conclusion

The survey vindicates the reader's starting position in a precise form: standard OR algebra does not help because every published exact method is one of five ways of making a 0/1 quantity multiply a bounded continuous one, and McCormick is already the hull of that single product. What the other fields contribute is not new convexity, since every field that reaches convexity does so by making the payoff constant, the decision the distribution, or the energy the cost, and none of those holds for an LP-valued recourse that depends on y. What they contribute is a better *lift*: the perspective hull of global optimization, applied factor by factor to a nonnegative decomposition of the probability and combined with Benders cuts separated in the extended space, removes the recourse bound from the relaxation entirely, and on the toy instances that single change is the difference between a 2 % root gap and an integral relaxation. The physics, economics and RL candidates are all correct identities that happen to put the hard coupling in a place where McCormick is loosest (a probability in [0, 1/2], a reciprocal denominator, a quadratic shift), which is a useful negative result in its own right: it says the bilinearity should be attacked at the binary factor, never at the fractional one.

The immediate implication for the research plan is a specific sequence. Settle first whether demand response is a shift or a reweighting, because the shift model is convex for free. If it is a reweighting, implement the per-factor lifted-cut master with parallel (s, factor) separation and measure how the gap against MC-B grows from n = 3 to n = 15–20, adding Boolean-quadric triangle inequalities and, for multilinear p, flower inequalities as the lift degrades; then test whether joint two-copy separation recovers the RLT-1 bound at a cost that parallelism absorbs. The open theoretical prize, a polynomial description of the hull of {(y, r, r ⊗ y)} on the simplex, is the single result that would turn the physics candidate from a curiosity into a competitor, and it is the same object the OR literature has been circling since Noyan et al.'s one-hot hull.
